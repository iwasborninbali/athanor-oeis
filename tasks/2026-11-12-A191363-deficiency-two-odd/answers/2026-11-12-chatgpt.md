$$
\boxed{\forall n\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad
n\text{ odd}\ \land\ \sigma(n)=2n-2
\ \Longrightarrow\ n=3,}
$$

where $\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d$.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

I did not find a proof or counterexample to the full conjecture. However, the following unconditional result can be established by elementary inequalities and congruences.

### Theorem (proved here)

Suppose $n>3$ is odd and

$$
\sigma(n)=2n-2.
$$

Then:

1. $n$ has **at least four distinct prime factors**, i.e. $\omega(n)\ge4$.
2. If $3\nmid n$, then $\omega(n)\ge7$.
3. $n$ is not a square, and $\gcd(n,\sigma(n))=1$.

These are necessary conditions, not a solution of the conjecture. I do not claim that the first two restrictions are previously unpublished.

### Proof

For $p$ prime and $a\ge1$,

$$
1+\frac1p\le
\frac{\sigma(p^a)}{p^a}
<\frac{p}{p-1}.
\tag{1}
$$

Consequently, for $n=\prod p_i^{a_i}$,

$$
2-\frac2n
=\frac{\sigma(n)}n
<\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p-1}.
\tag{2}
$$

The right-hand side decreases when any prime factor increases.

**Step A: Eliminate one or two distinct prime factors.**

If $n=p^a$, then:

- For $p\ge5$, the right side of (1) is at most $5/4$, while $2-2/n\ge8/5$.
- For $p=3,\ a\ge2$, it is less than $3/2$, while $2-2/n\ge16/9$.
- For $p=3,\ a=1$, equality holds: $\sigma(3)=4=2(3)-2$.

Now suppose $n=p^a q^b$, with distinct odd primes $p<q$.

If $p\ge5$, the product in (2) is at most $35/24$, while $n\ge35$, giving a contradiction.

If $p=3,\ q\ge7$, the product is at most $7/4$, while $n\ge21$, again a contradiction.

Finally, if $(p,q)=(3,5)$, then either $n=15$ or $n\ge45$. The first has $\sigma(15)=24\ne28$; in the second,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n<\frac{15}{8}
<2-\frac2{45}\le2-\frac2n.
$$

Therefore the only odd solution with at most two distinct prime factors is $3$. This special case is also consistent with the previously published two-prime-factor classification described in the Romanian olympiad problem indexed as Problem 2467. Olympiad Prep

**Step B: Reduce three-prime-factor solutions to three cases.**

Write $n=p^a q^b r^c$, where $p<q<r$ are odd primes.

If $p\ge5$, the product in (2) is at most

$$
\frac54\frac76\frac{11}{10}
=\frac{77}{48},
$$

too small because $n\ge385$.

If $p=3$ but $q\ge7$, the product is at most

$$
\frac32\frac76\frac{11}{10}
=\frac{77}{40},
$$

also too small because $n\ge231$.

Thus $p=3,\ q=5$.

If $r\ge17$, the product in (2) is at most

$$
\frac32\frac54\frac{17}{16}
=\frac{255}{128}.
$$

For $n\ge285$, this is strictly less than $2-2/n$. The only smaller candidate with these three prime factors is $n=255$, and

$$
\sigma(255)=432\ne508.
$$

Therefore the only remaining possibilities are

$$
\boxed{
n=3^a5^b r^c,\qquad r\in\{7,11,13\}.
}
\tag{3}
$$

**Step C: Use congruences modulo 3.**

Since $3\mid n$,

$$
\sigma(n)=2n-2\equiv1\pmod3.
$$

Also $\sigma(3^a)\equiv1\pmod3$, so

$$
\sigma(5^b)\sigma(r^c)\equiv1\pmod3.
$$

Because $5\equiv-1\pmod3$, the first factor is zero modulo 3 when $b$ is odd. Hence **$b$ must be even**, and $b\ge2$.

Considering the residue of $r$ gives:

| $r$ | Necessary exponent restrictions |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | $b\ge2$ even; $c\ge3$ divisible by 3 |
| 11 | $b\ge2$ even; $c\ge2$ even |
| 13 | $b\ge2$ even; $c\ge3$ divisible by 3 |

**Step D: Eliminate the remaining cases.**

We use the fact that $\sigma(p^a)/p^a$ increases with $a$.

*Case $r=7$.* If $a=1$,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n<
\frac43\frac54\frac76
=\frac{35}{18}
<2-\frac2n,
$$

because $n\ge105$.

If $a\ge2$, the exponent restrictions give

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n
\ge
\frac{\sigma(3^2 5^2 7^3)}{3^2 5^2 7^3}
=\frac{6448}{3087}>2.
$$

Both possibilities contradict the defining equation.

*Case $r=11$.* Here $b,c$ are even. Since $\sigma(n)$ is even, $a$ must be odd: otherwise every exponent would be even, making $\sigma(n)$ odd.

If $a=1$,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n<
\frac43\frac54\frac{11}{10}
=\frac{11}{6}
<2-\frac2n,
$$

because $n\ge165$.

If $a\ge3$,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n
\ge
\frac{\sigma(3^3 5^2 11^2)}
{3^3 5^2 11^2}
=\frac{32984}{16335}>2.
$$

Again impossible.

*Case $r=13$.* If $a\le2$,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n
<
\frac{13}{9}\frac54\frac{13}{12}
=\frac{845}{432}
<2-\frac2n,
$$

because $n\ge195$.

If $a\ge4$,

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n
\ge
\frac{\sigma(3^4 5^2 13^3)}
{3^4 5^2 13^3}
=\frac{1785476}{889785}>2.
$$

The only remaining exponent is $a=3$.

But $b$ is even, so modulo 8,

$$
n=3^3 5^b13^c
\equiv3\cdot1\cdot(1\text{ or }5)
\equiv3\text{ or }7\pmod8.
$$

Therefore $2n-2\equiv4\pmod8$. On the other hand,

$$
\sigma(3^3)=40\equiv0\pmod8,
$$

and multiplicativity implies $\sigma(n)\equiv0\pmod8$, a contradiction.

Thus **there is no odd solution $n>3$ with exactly three distinct prime factors**.

Combining Steps A–D proves

$$
\boxed{n>3,\ n\text{ odd},\ \sigma(n)=2n-2
\implies \omega(n)\ge4.}
$$

**Step E: A stronger bound when $3\nmid n$.**

Suppose $3\nmid n$ and $3\le\omega(n)\le6$. From (2),

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n<
\frac54\frac76\frac{11}{10}
\frac{13}{12}\frac{17}{16}\frac{19}{18}
=\frac{323323}{165888}<1.95.
$$

But $n\ge5\cdot7\cdot11=385$, so

$$
\frac{\sigma(n)}n
=2-\frac2n
\ge2-\frac2{385}>1.99,
$$

a contradiction. Cases $\omega(n)\le2$ were already eliminated in Step A.

Hence

$$
\boxed{3\nmid n\implies\omega(n)\ge7.}
$$

Finally, $n$ cannot be a square: the divisor sum of an odd square is odd, whereas $2n-2$ is even. And since $n$ is odd,

$$
\gcd(n,\sigma(n))
=\gcd(n,2n-2)=\gcd(n,2)=1.
$$

This completes the proof of all three necessary conditions. $\square$

## 3. Computational verification

**Status: computed and independently run.**

I used Python's standard library to sieve $\sigma(n)$ for every integer $1\le n\le2{,}000{,}000$, count distinct prime factors, and check the exact rational inequalities used in the proof.

The program also separately verifies the fifth known term, $2147516416$.

### Reproducible Python 3 code

```python

from array import array
from math import isqrt
from fractions import Fraction as F

B = 2_000_000

# Exact divisor sums.
sigma = array('I', [0]) * (B + 1)
for d in range(1, B + 1):
    for n in range(d, B + 1, d):
        sigma[n] += d

# Number of distinct prime factors.
omega = bytearray(B + 1)
for p in range(2, B + 1):
    if omega[p] == 0:
        for n in range(p, B + 1, p):
            omega[n] += 1

hits = [
    n for n in range(1, B + 1)
    if sigma[n] == 2*n - 2
]
odd_hits = [n for n in hits if n & 1]

assert odd_hits == [3]
assert all(
    n == 3 or omega[n] >= 4
    for n in odd_hits
)
assert all(
    n != 3 or omega[n] == 1
    for n in odd_hits
)

# Independently verify the fifth known term.
p = 65537
assert all(
    p % d for d in range(2, isqrt(p) + 1)
)

large_n = (1 << 15) * p
large_sigma = ((1 << 16) - 1) * (p + 1)
assert large_sigma == 2 * large_n - 2

# Exact-rational checks for the proof.
def ratio(p, a):
    return F(
        sum(p**k for k in range(a + 1)),
        p**a
    )

assert ratio(3, 2)*ratio(5, 2)*ratio(7, 3) > 2
assert ratio(3, 3)*ratio(5, 2)*ratio(11, 2) > 2
assert ratio(3, 4)*ratio(5, 2)*ratio(13, 3) > 2

assert F(13, 9)*F(5, 4)*F(13, 12) < 2 - F(2, 195)
assert F(3, 2)*F(5, 4)*F(17, 16) < 2 - F(2, 285)
assert F(323323, 165888) < 2 - F(2, 385)

assert (2*(3**3*5**2*13**3 - 1)) % 8 == 4
assert sum(3**k for k in range(4)) % 8 == 0

print(f'checked n = 1..{B}')
print(f'all deficiency-2 solutions: {hits}')
print(f'odd deficiency-2 solutions: {odd_hits}')
print('violations of omega(n) >= 4 for odd solutions n > 3: 0')
print(
    f'additional term n = {large_n}: '
    f'sigma(n) = {large_sigma}; '
    f'2n-2 = {2*large_n-2}'
)
print('exact-rational proof checks: PASS (8 assertions)')
```
