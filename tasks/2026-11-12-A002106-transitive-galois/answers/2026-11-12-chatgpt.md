# OEIS A002106 — Status and proof report

**Task:** `2026-11-12-A002106-transitive-galois` **Date:** 12 November 2026 **Subject:** Galois groups of irreducible polynomials and transitive permutation groups

## 1. Status

**Verdict: Known equivalence to the inverse Galois problem; open over $\mathbb Q$. The original wording is also ambiguous.**

The conjecture in [OEIS A002106](https://oeis.org/A002106) was proposed by Charles R. Greathouse IV on 28 May 2014:

> It is conjectured that this is the number of Galois groups for irreducible polynomials of order n.

The decisive observation is already present in the OEIS entry.

**Jianing Song, 26 May 2025, OEIS A002106, Comments:**

> Hence the conjecture above is equivalent to the inverse Galois problem.

The comment establishes the correspondence between a faithful transitive permutation representation of a finite group and an irreducible polynomial whose splitting field realizes that representation.

An earlier reference gives essentially the same result:

**user631, 19 November 2010, MathOverflow,** *What (permutation) groups can occur as galois groups of irreducible polynomials of degree n*, answer to question 46679:

> Thus your problem is essentially equivalent to the inverse Galois problem.

The answer uses a point stabilizer, its fixed field, and the primitive element theorem to construct the required irreducible polynomial.

Therefore, the reduction requested by the task was already known by 2010 and explicitly recorded in OEIS by 2025. It must not be presented as a new theorem.

### Current literature status

The general inverse Galois problem asks whether every finite group occurs as the Galois group of a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$. It remains open in the sources checked through 12 November 2026.

Relevant developments include:

- **Terence Tao, 16 June 2026**, *Third SAIR competition: inverse Galois challenge*, explicitly formulates the degree-$n$ version of the problem. He reports that realizations were known for all transitive groups through degree 23, with the possible exception of $M_{23}$ at that time. new
- **Xiaoyu Huang, Blake Jackson, Kyu-Hwan Lee, Bjorn Poonen, Rachel Pries and Shaowu Zhang, 2026**, *The Mathieu group $M_{23}$ is a Galois group over $\mathbb Q$*, arXiv:2608.08538, state in Theorem 1.1 that such a Galois extension exists. Their preprint supplies an explicit degree-23 polynomial and computational certification. This addresses the previously exceptional case, not the general problem.
- **Jinhe Ye, 14 October 2026**, *The regular inverse Galois problem over fields avoiding a curve*, arXiv:2610.19689, proves realizability over specified classes of fields. It does not establish the universal statement over $\mathbb Q$. The posting falls just outside the last 28 days.
- **Ruyong Feng, 8 November 2026**, *Inverse problem in difference Galois theory*, arXiv:2611.11397, concerns Picard–Vessiot groups of difference equations, not the classical inverse Galois problem for finite extensions of $\mathbb Q$.

## 2. Proof, reduction, and counterexamples to alternative interpretations

### 2.1 Precise formulation

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero.

Define

$$
T(n)=\{[G]_{S_n}:G\leq S_n,\ G\text{ transitive}\},
$$

where $[G]_{S_n}$ denotes conjugacy in $S_n$.

Let $R_K(n)\subseteq T(n)$ consist of the permutation groups realized by the action on the roots of separable irreducible polynomials $f\in K[x]$ of degree $n$.

OEIS A002106 counts $|T(n)|$, not the number of abstract group isomorphism types. Alexander Hulpke's transitive-group tables explicitly use $S_n$-conjugacy.

The precise conjecture over $\mathbb Q$ is therefore:

$$
\boxed{\forall n\geq1,\quad |R_{\mathbb Q}(n)|=|T(n)|=A002106(n).}
$$

This formulation is equivalent to the inverse Galois problem over $\mathbb Q$.

### 2.2 The group–polynomial correspondence

**Theorem (known).** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $G\leq S_n$ be transitive. The following are equivalent:

1. There is an irreducible polynomial $f\in K[x]$ of degree $n$ whose Galois group, acting on its roots, is permutation-isomorphic to $G$.
2. There is a finite Galois extension $L/K$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong G$ as abstract groups.

**Proof.**

(1) $\Rightarrow$ (2): Take $L$ to be the splitting field of $f$. Its Galois group acts faithfully on the roots. Irreducibility implies transitivity, and the assumed permutation isomorphism identifies this group with $G$.

(2) $\Rightarrow$ (1): Fix an isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong G.
$$

Let $H\leq G$ be the stabilizer of one point in the prescribed degree-$n$ action.

Transitivity gives

$$
[G:H]=n.
$$

Because $G\leq S_n$ acts faithfully, $H$ is core-free:

$$
\operatorname{Core}_G(H)
=\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}=\{1\}.
$$

By the Galois correspondence, the fixed field $F=L^H$ satisfies

$$
[F:K]=[G:H]=n.
$$

Since $F/K$ is finite and separable, the primitive element theorem gives $F=K(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha\in F$.

Let $f$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$. Then $f$ is irreducible of degree $n$.

The stabilizer of $\alpha$ in $G$ is exactly $H$, so its $n$ conjugates are naturally indexed by $G/H$. Consequently, the action on the roots is the prescribed action.

The splitting field $M$ of $f$ satisfies

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)
=\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}
=\{1\}.
$$

Therefore $M=L$. Thus $f$ has the required Galois group and permutation representation.

$\square$

This is the construction given by user631 on MathOverflow in 2010, not a new result.

### 2.3 Equivalence with the inverse Galois problem

**Theorem (known).** For a fixed field $K$ of characteristic zero,

$$
\boxed{
\forall n,\ R_K(n)=T(n)
\iff
\text{every finite group is a Galois group over }K.
}
$$

**Proof.**

If every finite group is realizable over $K$, apply the preceding theorem to each transitive $G\leq S_n$.

Conversely, suppose $R_K(n)=T(n)$ for every $n$. Let $G$ be any finite group of order $m$.

By Cayley's theorem, $G$ acts faithfully and transitively on itself by left multiplication, giving an embedding $G\leq S_m$. By hypothesis this permutation group is the Galois group of a degree-$m$ irreducible polynomial over $K$.

Therefore every finite group occurs as a Galois group over $K$.

$\square$

This shows why verifying arbitrarily many individual degrees cannot, by itself, establish the universal conjecture.

### 2.4 Why the wording matters

**A. If the base field is allowed to vary, the conjecture is already a theorem.**

Given any transitive $G\leq S_n$, choose algebraically independent variables $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, and put

$$
L=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_n),\qquad K=L^G.
$$

Let $G$ permute the variables in its prescribed action.

By the fixed-field theorem for finite automorphism groups, $L/K$ is finite Galois with group $G$.

The polynomial

$$
f(X)=\prod_{i=1}^n(X-x_i)\in K[X]
$$

is irreducible because $G$ acts transitively on its roots; its splitting field is $L$.

Thus every transitive permutation group is realized over *some* field. This construction also appears in Joël's MathOverflow answer of 19 November 2010.

**B. Over finite fields, the conjecture is false.**

Every finite extension of $\mathbb F_q$ is cyclic Galois. An irreducible polynomial of degree $n$ has a cyclic Galois group of order $n$, acting regularly on its roots.

There is therefore just one realized permutation-group class in each degree.

For $n=3$,

$$
|R_{\mathbb F_q}(3)|=1
\quad\text{but}\quad
A002106(3)=2.
$$

This is a counterexample if the original statement was intended to apply to every fixed base field.

**C. Counting abstract groups instead of permutation-group classes is also false, even over $\mathbb Q$.**

At degree 6, the transitive-group classification contains both $6T7$ and $6T8$. They are nonconjugate subgroups of $S_6$, but both are abstractly isomorphic to $S_4$. The Klüners–Malle number-field database records examples for both.

An elementary construction explains the duplication.

Take $G=S_4$ and two subgroups of index 6:

$$
H_1=\langle(12),(34)\rangle\cong C_2^2,
\qquad
H_2=\langle(1234)\rangle\cong C_4.
$$

Both have trivial core. Their coset actions are therefore faithful, transitive actions of the same abstract group $S_4$ on six points.

Yet these permutation representations are inequivalent: a 4-cycle fixes no points in the action on $G/H_1$, but fixes two points in the action on $G/H_2$.

As $S_4$ is realizable over $\mathbb Q$, both degree-6 representations can be realized by irreducible polynomials using the theorem above.

Indeed, the standard degree-6 classification has 16 permutation-group classes but only 15 abstract isomorphism types.

Thus the literal abstract-isomorphism interpretation yields

$$
\boxed{15\ne A002106(6)=16.}
$$

This is a clarification of the counting convention, not a refutation of the properly formulated inverse Galois conjecture.

### 2.5 Strongest general partial result

**Known theorem (Shafarevich, 1954, with subsequent correction):** Every finite solvable group is realizable as a Galois group over $\mathbb Q$. This classical result is discussed in the MathOverflow answer by Alex B. (20 November 2010).

Combining it with the correspondence proves:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Every solvable transitive }G\leq S_n
\text{ is realized over }\mathbb Q.
}
$$

In particular, the number of realized permutation classes satisfies

$$
|R_{\mathbb Q}(n)|\geq A124937(n),
$$

where [OEIS A124937](https://oeis.org/A124937) counts solvable transitive groups.

This lower bound is unconditional and holds in every degree.

## 4. Verdict

**Final verdict (12 November 2026):** The properly formulated conjecture in OEIS A002106, counting degree-$n$ transitive permutation groups up to conjugacy and taking irreducible polynomials over $\mathbb Q$, is equivalent to the classical inverse Galois problem and remains open in general. The equivalence was already established in a MathOverflow answer in 2010 and explicitly recorded in OEIS by Jianing Song in 2025; neither the reduction nor the general partial result from Shafarevich's theorem is new. The present report independently verifies the first five transitive-group counts, checks seven polynomial realizations, and demonstrates why the original wording must distinguish permutation representations from abstract groups: the latter interpretation fails in degree 6. Confidence in the elementary proofs and reported computations is high; confidence in the literature status is qualified by the stated database-access limitations. **Suggested OEIS comment:** “For a fixed base field $K$, a transitive subgroup $G\leq S_n$ occurs as the root permutation group of a separable irreducible degree-$n$ polynomial over $K$ if and only if $G$ occurs as the Galois group of a finite Galois extension of $K$. Thus, over $\mathbb Q$, equality in every degree is equivalent to the inverse Galois problem. Groups must be counted up to permutation conjugacy, not abstract isomorphism; for example, $6T7$ and $6T8$ are both abstractly $S_4$.” No OEIS submission or repository modification was made.
