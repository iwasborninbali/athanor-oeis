## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

### 2.1 Precise formulation

Let $G$ be a finite connected loopless multigraph with $n$ edges, each representing a unit resistor, and distinguished terminals $s,t$. Following A337517, require every edge to belong to a simple $s$-$t$ path.

Write its effective resistance in lowest terms as

$$
R_G=\frac pq,\qquad p,q>0,\quad\gcd(p,q)=1.
$$

Define

$$
B(n)=\max_{\lvert E(G)\rvert=n}pq
$$

and

$$
A(n)=\max_{\substack{\lvert E(G)\rvert=n\\
\text{all edge currents integral at }(V,I)=(p,q)}}pq.
$$

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{A(n)=B(n)\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.}
$$

Clearly $A(n)\le B(n)$.

The difficulty is the reverse inequality: a resistance $p/q$ does not automatically admit integer currents on every edge at source voltage $p$ and source current $q$.

### 2.2 Exact integrality criterion — proved

**Lemma.** Let $L$ be the reduced graph Laplacian, formed by grounding terminal $t$. Set

$$
T=\det L,\qquad
x=\operatorname{adj}(L)e_s,\qquad F=x_s.
$$

Extend $x_t=0$, and define

$$
h=\gcd_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}|x_u-x_v|.
$$

Then:

1. $R_G=F/T$.
2. The least positive integer source voltage inducing integer currents on every edge is $V_s=F/h$.
3. Its corresponding source current is $I_s=T/h$.
4. This pair is coprime exactly when

$$
\boxed{h=\gcd(F,T).}
$$

**Proof.**

The adjugate identity gives

$$
Lx=T e_s.
$$

Consequently, the integer potentials $x_v$ yield integer edge currents $x_u-x_v$, with total source current $T$ and voltage difference $F$. Therefore $R_G=F/T$.

Every edge current is a multiple of $h$, and Bezout's identity shows that a common scaling factor makes all edge currents integral precisely when its product with $h$ is an integer.

Thus the smallest positive scaling factor is $1/h$, giving $(V_s,I_s)=(F/h,T/h)$.

Furthermore, $h\mid F$, because the potential difference $F$ is the sum of edge differences along an $s$-$t$ path. Also $h\mid T$, because the source current is a signed sum of incident edge currents.

It follows that

$$
\gcd(V_s,I_s)=\frac{\gcd(F,T)}h.
$$

The coprimality criterion follows. $\square$

**Corollary — proved.** If $\gcd(F,T)=1$, the primitive source pair automatically induces integer currents through every resistor.

This gives an exact, easily computable test for whether a resistance realization satisfies A340726's additional requirement.

### 2.3 Why the condition matters

Consider a four-edge cycle with the terminals at opposite vertices.

There are two length-two paths in parallel, so

$$
R_G=1=\frac11.
$$

But at $V=1$, $I=1$, each branch carries $1/2$ ampere. The individual resistor currents are not integers.

Here

$$
T=4,\qquad F=4,\qquad h=2,
$$

so the minimal all-integral source pair is $(2,2)$, not coprime.

Thus the equality of maxima cannot be proved merely by reducing every effective resistance to lowest terms.

### 2.4 First-occurrence reduction — proved

There is a useful structural restriction on any resistance attaining $B(n)$.

**Proposition.** If $p/q$ attains $B(n)$, then it cannot be realized using fewer than $n$ unit resistors.

**Proof.**

Suppose it can be realized using $m<n$ resistors.

Add $n-m$ unit resistors in series. The resulting network has exactly $n$ resistors and resistance

$$
\frac pq+(n-m)
=\frac{p+(n-m)q}{q}.
$$

Because $\gcd(p,q)=1$, this is still in lowest terms. Its numerator-denominator product is

$$
[p+(n-m)q]q
=pq+(n-m)q^2>pq.
$$

This contradicts maximality. $\square$

**Conditional reduction.** Therefore, the A340726 conjecture would follow if every rational resistance, when it first becomes realizable with $n$ unit resistors, has at least one realization satisfying the integral-current and coprimality conditions.

That stronger first-occurrence property is not proved here for arbitrary $n$. It is, however, verified computationally below for $n\le8$.

### Completeness of the enumeration

For $n\le8$, every admissible network is covered by taking a simple graph skeleton and assigning positive integer edge multiplicities.

The program enumerates all connected simple graphs on at most seven vertices using the NetworkX graph atlas, all choices of terminal vertices, and all multiplicity compositions whose sum is at most eight.

The additional possibilities with more than seven vertices are handled explicitly: terminal-to-terminal paths, and eight-vertex unicyclic graphs consisting of a cycle with terminal arms. Every edge must lie on a simple terminal-to-terminal path, which excludes other branches.

This exhausts the admissible graph structures with at most eight edges. Some isomorphic networks are counted more than once, but this cannot affect the sets of resistances or maxima.

### Explicit witness at $n=8$

The maximizing resistance $35/34$ can be realized with terminals $s=0$, $t=2$, using edges

$$
(0,1),(0,4),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),
(2,3),(2,3),(3,4).
$$

At source voltage $35$, the node potentials are

$$
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(35,16,0,9,20).
$$

The currents through the eight resistors are, in the edge order above,

$$
19,\ 15,\ 16,\ 7,\ -4,\ -9,\ -9,\ -11.
$$

All are integers; the source current is $19+15=34$, and $\gcd(35,34)=1$. Hence the attained power is

$$
35\cdot34=\boxed{1190}.
$$

The exhaustive enumeration confirms that no eight-resistor network has a greater reduced numerator-denominator product.
