# Report: OEIS A345370 — Conjecture on Diagonal Transversals of Order 12

**Investigation date:** 12 November 2026 **Sequence:** [OEIS A345370](https://oeis.org/A345370) **Conjecture:** Natalia Makarova, 26 October 2021

**Verdict:** A refutation has been publicly claimed, but I could not verify a rigorous proof. The conjecture remains mathematically unresolved on the evidence I could independently check. The computations below confirm the published witnesses but do not establish nonexistence of missing values.

## 1. Status and literature search

### 1.1 Precise statement

Let $\mathcal D_n$ be the set of diagonal Latin squares of order $n$.

A diagonal transversal contains exactly one cell in every row, column, and symbol class, as well as exactly one cell on each of the two principal diagonals.

Define

$$
S_n=\{T(L):L\in\mathcal D_n\},
$$

where $T(L)$ counts diagonal transversals.

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{A345370}(n)&=|S_n|,\\
\mathrm{A287647}(n)&=\min S_n,\\
\mathrm{A287648}(n)&=\max S_n.
\end{aligned}
$$

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{|S_{12}|=\max S_{12}-\min S_{12}+1.}
$$

Equivalently, **every integer between the minimum and maximum possible diagonal-transversal counts must occur**.

The statement is well-defined.

### 1.2 Previously reported refutation

The most significant finding is already linked from A345370.

Eduard I. Vatutin is credited with a Russian-language note whose title is translated in OEIS as:

> “On the falsity of conjecture that spectra of diagonal transversals for diagonal Latin squares of order 12 is solid.”

[Referenced VK post](https://vk.com/wall162891802_1790).

This explicitly reports the conjecture as false.

However, I could not retrieve the post's contents. Consequently, I could not determine whether it contains a rigorous impossibility proof, an exhaustive computation, or an inference from the gaps in a heuristically constructed spectrum.

**Status of this evidence:** Reported refutation, not independently verified.

### 1.3 Published computational work

A related publication is:

E. I. Vatutin, V. S. Titov, A. I. Pykhtin, A. V. Kripachev, N. N. Nikitina, M. O. Manzuk, A. M. Albertyan, and I. I. Kurochkin (2022), *Estimation of the Cardinalities of the Spectra of Fast-computable Numerical Characteristics for Diagonal Latin Squares of Orders N>9*, conference proceedings, Murom, Russia.

[Full text (Russian)](https://evatutin.narod.ru/evatutin_spectra_t_dt_i_o_high_orders_1.pdf)

The paper explicitly characterizes the large-order spectra as approximations obtained using heuristic algorithms. Its introductory discussion states, in translation:

> “For orders N > 9, this problem cannot be solved exactly with the currently available computing technology.”

The paper reports lower bounds, not a complete characterization of $S_{12}$. Its table gives an earlier bound of 17,684 distinct diagonal-transversal counts for order 12.

The current OEIS entry reports:

$$
\mathrm{A345370}(12)\ge17698,
$$

$$
\mathrm{A287647}(12)=0,
\qquad
\mathrm{A287648}(12)\ge30192.
$$

These are bounds, not exact values for the spectrum cardinality or its maximum.

## 2. Proof attempt and strongest verified partial result

### 2.1 A necessary condition for the conjecture

**Proved.** The supplied witnesses establish that

$$
0\in S_{12},
\qquad
30192\in S_{12}.
$$

Since counts cannot be negative, $\min S_{12}=0$. Therefore the conjecture would imply

$$
\boxed{\{0,1,2,\ldots,30192\}\subseteq S_{12}.}
$$

In particular, a square with exactly one diagonal transversal would have to exist.

Conversely, a proof that $1\notin S_{12}$ would refute the conjecture.

### 2.2 The published spectrum has gaps

Vatutin's [order-12 proving list](https://evatutin.narod.ru/spectra/spectrum_dls_diagonal_transversals_n12_17698_known_items.txt) begins with the values

$$
0,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ldots
$$

and ends with a witness for 30,192 diagonal transversals. It contains 17,698 entries, each accompanied by a Latin square.

In particular, 1 is missing from the published list.

The interval from 0 through 30,192 contains 30,193 integers, exceeding the size of the published list by

$$
30193-17698=12495.
$$

**Computed from published data:** At least 12,495 values in that interval are not represented in the current proving list.

**Not proved:** Those values are impossible for all diagonal Latin squares of order 12.

This is the central distinction. A list constructed by heuristic search can establish that values occur, but not that unlisted values cannot occur.

### 2.3 Result of the proof attempt

I found no checkable argument establishing $1\notin S_{12}$, nor another provably excluded integer between 0 and 30,192.

Likewise, I found no exhaustive enumeration of all diagonal Latin squares of order 12 that could establish the complete spectrum.

Therefore:

- **Proved:** The conjecture is equivalent to the spectrum being an integer interval.
- **Proved:** If the conjecture holds, every integer from 0 through 30,192 must occur.
- **Computed:** Several values in this interval have explicit witnesses, while others are absent from the known list.
- **Unresolved in this investigation:** Whether the missing values are genuinely unattainable.

No new theorem or counterexample is claimed.

## 3. Computational verification

I wrote and executed a Python program that checks the Latin and diagonal constraints, then counts diagonal transversals by backtracking with bitmasks.

The recursion selects one column per row, rejects repeated columns or symbols, and requires exactly one selected cell on each diagonal.

The program also exhaustively enumerates normalized diagonal Latin squares of orders 4 and 5.

### 3.2 Scope of verification

For orders 4 and 5, fixing the first row to $0,1,\ldots,n-1$ loses no possible transversal counts: every square can be normalized by a symbol permutation, which preserves the defining conditions and transversal count.

Thus, the enumerated spectra are exhaustive:

$$
S_4=\{4\},\qquad S_5=\{1,5\}.
$$

These agree with A345370.

For order 8, five published witnesses were independently checked.

For order 12, five published witnesses were independently checked, including the extreme known counts 0 and 30,192.

All checks passed. This independently verifies the associated numerical certificates, but it does not exhaustively determine $S_{12}$.

## 4. Verdict

**Status: Refutation reported, rigorous resolution not independently verified.** The OEIS entry retains Makarova's 2021 conjecture while linking Vatutin's explicit claim of falsity. I could not inspect that claim's supporting argument. The accessible published work emphasizes heuristic approximations, and the known order-12 witness list cannot by itself prove that any missing count is impossible. My independent computation confirms five order-12 witnesses, including counts 0 and 30,192, and reproduces the complete spectra for orders 4 and 5. Nothing here constitutes a new proof. I would not classify the conjecture as definitively refuted without examining Vatutin's original argument or obtaining a separate impossibility certificate.

**Suggested OEIS comment, subject to editorial verification:**

> The conjecture that the spectrum for order 12 is an interval has been challenged by E. I. Vatutin (see the linked note on its claimed falsity). The currently published proving list contains 17698 distinct values, including 0 and 30192, but no example with exactly 1 diagonal transversal. Since the list is not known to be exhaustive, its gaps alone do not constitute a proof of nonexistence. A rigorous refutation or proof reference would clarify the status.
