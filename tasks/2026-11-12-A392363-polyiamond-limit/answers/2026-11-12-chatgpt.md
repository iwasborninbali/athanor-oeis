# OEIS A392363 — Status and Proof Attempt

**Date:** 12 November 2026 **Problem:** Conjectured asymptotic size of a minimum universal polyiamond **Verdict:** Open; rigorous partial bounds established below.

## 1. Status

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^2}=\frac15,}
$$

where $a(n)$ is the minimum number of triangular cells in a connected polyiamond containing a congruent copy of every free $n$-iamond.

**Status: OPEN, as far as the accessible literature establishes.** I found neither a proof nor a counterexample.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

I cannot prove the proposed limit. However, two unconditional bounds can be established.

PROVED HERE — ASYMPTOTIC BOUNDS

$$
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}n\ge 1.59
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^2}\le\frac38.
}
$$

These are elementary consequences of geometric and enumerative facts. I have not established that either bound is previously unpublished.

### Theorem 1: Universal quadratic upper bound

For every integer $n\ge1$,

$$
\boxed{
a(n)\le
1+\frac32
\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor
\left(\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+1\right).
}
$$

**Proof.**

Represent a polyiamond by its cell-adjacency graph. This is a finite connected subgraph of the infinite honeycomb graph $H$, whose vertices correspond to triangular cells.

Let $P$ be any connected $n$-iamond.

1. Choose a spanning tree $T$ of the adjacency graph of $P$. It has $n$ vertices.
2. Every tree on $n$ vertices has a vertex of eccentricity at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Indeed, the center of a longest path has eccentricity $\lceil D/2\rceil$, where its diameter $D\le n-1$.
3. Thus there is a cell $v\in P$ such that every cell of $P$ is at graph distance at most $r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ from $v$, measured within $T$, hence also within $H$.
4. The symmetry group of the triangular tiling acts transitively on its triangular cells. Apply a lattice symmetry sending $v$ to a fixed reference cell $o$.
5. The transformed $P$ lies entirely inside the honeycomb ball \[ B_r(o)={w\in H:d_H(o,w)\le r}. \]

Therefore $B_r(o)$ is a universal connected container for every free $n$-iamond.

To count its cells, use the honeycomb graph's distance shells. For each $d\ge1$, exactly $3d$ vertices lie at distance $d$ from a fixed vertex.

This can be verified directly in coordinates: write the two vertex classes as $A_{i,j}$ and $B_{i,j}$, with

$$
A_{i,j}\sim B_{i,j},\ B_{i-1,j},\ B_{i,j-1}.
$$

The even-distance shells are hexagonal rings on the $A$-sublattice, each of size $6m$ at distance $2m$; the intervening $B$-shells have $6m+3$ vertices at distance $2m+1$.

Consequently,

$$
|B_r(o)|
=1+\sum_{d=1}^{r}3d
=1+\frac{3r(r+1)}2.
$$

The ball is connected by definition, so it is an admissible container. Substituting $r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ proves the theorem. $\square$

**Corollary.**

$$
a(n)\le\frac38n^2+O(n),
$$

hence

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}a(n)/n^2\le3/8.}
$$

The bound holds for every $n$. It is not sharp for the known terms: for example, it gives $a(14)\le85$, whereas OEIS reports $a(14)=42$.

### Theorem 2: An asymptotic linear lower bound

Let $F(n)=\mathrm{A000577}(n)$ be the number of free $n$-iamonds.

For a universal container $U$ with $k=a(n)$ cells, every free $n$-iamond must be represented by at least one distinct $n$-element subset of $U$.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F(n)\le\binom{a(n)}n.}\tag{1}
$$

This bound requires no assumptions about connectivity of the subsets counted by the binomial coefficient.

A published theorem supplies an exponential lower bound on $F(n)$.

**Vuong Bui (2023)**, *The Number of Polyiamonds is Supermultiplicative*, *The Electronic Journal of Combinatorics*, 30(4), #P4.38, DOI [10.37236/12028](https://doi.org/10.37236/12028).

The abstract states:

> A corollary is an increment of the best known lower bound on the growth constant from 2.8423 to 2.8578.

 Electronic Journal of Combinatorics

Write $T(n)=\mathrm{A001420}(n)$ for fixed polyiamonds. Bui's result gives

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(n)^{1/n}\ge2.8578.
$$

Since the number of fixed representatives in a free symmetry class is at most 12,

$$
F(n)\le T(n)\le12F(n).
$$

Thus $F(n)$ has the same exponential growth constant.

Now suppose $a(n)\le cn$ for infinitely many $n$, where $c>1$ is fixed.

By (1),

$$
F(n)\le\binom{\lfloor cn\rfloor}{n}.
$$

Stirling's formula gives

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\binom{\lfloor cn\rfloor}{n}^{1/n}
=
\frac{c^c}{(c-1)^{c-1}}
=:G(c).
$$

At $c=1.59$,

$$
G(1.59)=2.853768506\ldots<2.8578.
$$

This contradicts the published lower bound on the exponential growth rate of $F(n)$.

Hence $a(n)>1.59n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, and

$$
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}n\ge1.59.
}
\qquad\square
$$

A slightly stronger numerical coefficient, approximately $1.5914$, follows from the same calculation. The rounded value $1.59$ avoids unnecessary precision.

### What remains unresolved

The results establish

$$
1.59n\lesssim a(n)\lesssim0.375n^2
$$

asymptotically.

They do **not** establish quadratic growth, existence of the limit $a(n)/n^2$, or the conjectured coefficient $1/5$.

In particular, the main missing ingredient is a quadratic lower bound, or another argument determining the leading asymptotic coefficient.
