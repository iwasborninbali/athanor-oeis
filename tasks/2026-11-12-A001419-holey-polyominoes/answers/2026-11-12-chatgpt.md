# OEIS A001419 — Status and proof of the holey-polyomino conjecture

**Task:** 2026-11-12-A001419-holey-polyominoes **Investigation date:** November 12, 2026 **Result:** The conjecture is **already proved**, as a consequence of Neal Madras's 1999 pattern theorem. OEIS has explicitly acknowledged this since January 6, 2024.

## 1. Status — PROVED (known result)

The conjecture in [OEIS A001419](https://oeis.org/A001419) states:

> Conjecture: Almost all polyominoes are holey.

Writing

- $P_n=A000105(n)$, the number of free $n$-cell polyominoes;
- $H_n=A001419(n)$, the number with at least one hole;
- $U_n=A000104(n)$, the number without holes;

the precise assertion is

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n}{P_n}=1.}
$$

Since $P_n=H_n+U_n$, this is equivalent to

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{U_n}{P_n}=0.
$$

### Decisive prior work

**Neal Madras (1999).** *A pattern theorem for lattice clusters*, *Annals of Combinatorics* **3**, 357–384, DOI: [10.1007/BF01608793](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608793); preprint [arXiv:math/9902161](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9902161).

From the paper's abstract:

> “if a given local configuration (‘pattern’) of sites and bonds can occur in large clusters, then it occurs at least cN times in most clusters of size n”

This is a general pattern theorem for lattice animals, including square-lattice polyominoes.

**Johann Peters (January 6, 2024).** The [OEIS A000105](https://oeis.org/A000105) entry contains an explicit response to the conjecture:

> “This is true, a consequence of Madras's 1999 pattern theorem.”

This comment refers to the same assertion as A001419, phrased as $A000104(n)/A000105(n)\to0$.

This is decisive: the statement is neither open nor merely supported by enumeration. The appropriate classification is **known theorem, with the proof obtained by applying Madras (1999)**.

A001419 itself still labels the assertion a conjecture, so the issue is an outdated comment in one OEIS entry, not an unresolved mathematical question.

## 2. Proof — direct application of the known theorem

Although no new proof is needed, the following gives an explicit, checkable derivation.

### Step 1. Define a hole-forcing local pattern

Consider this arrangement of nine lattice positions:

Eight occupied cells surrounding one empty cell.

Formally, define a pattern $Q=(M,F)$ with mandatory occupied sites

$$
M=\{-1,0,1\}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\},
$$

and forbidden site

$$
F=\{(0,0)\}.
$$

**Lemma 1 (proved).** Every polyomino containing a translate of $Q$ has a hole.

**Proof.** The missing central cell has an occupied cell on each of its four sides. Consequently, its interior cannot connect to the unbounded component of the complement. It therefore lies in a bounded complementary component, which is a hole. $\square$

### Step 2. Verify that the pattern is proper

A pattern is *proper* if it occurs in connected polyominoes of arbitrarily large size.

For every $n\geq8$, begin with the eight-cell ring and attach $n-8$ additional cells in a straight line extending to the right from the cell $(1,0)$.

The resulting shape is connected, has exactly $n$ occupied cells, and retains the empty central cell.

Therefore $Q$ is proper.

This is precisely the condition needed in the pattern theorem.

### Step 3. Apply Madras's pattern theorem

Let $T_n$ be the number of polyominoes counted up to translation only (fixed polyominoes), and let $B_n$ count those without holes.

Let

$$
\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_n^{1/n}
$$

be the polyomino growth constant.

Madras's pattern theorem implies that, for a proper pattern $Q$, there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that the number of $n$-cell polyominoes with fewer than $\varepsilon n$ occurrences of $Q$ has exponential growth rate strictly less than $\lambda$.

An explicit polyomino formulation appears in Gill Barequet, Neal Madras and Johann Peters (2024), *On t-fold Totally Concave Polyominoes*, §3, Definition 1 and Theorem 6. The authors attribute the theorem to Madras (1999).

Every hole-free polyomino has zero occurrences of $Q$, by Lemma 1. Thus

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}B_n^{1/n}<\lambda.}
$$

This is stronger than merely proving that hole-free polyominoes are asymptotically rare.

Choose constants $\beta,\alpha$ satisfying

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}B_n^{1/n}<\beta
<\alpha<\lambda.
$$

For all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
B_n\leq\beta^n,\qquad T_n\geq\alpha^n.
$$

Hence

$$
\frac{B_n}{T_n}
\leq
\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^n
\longrightarrow0.
$$

### Step 4. Transfer the result to free polyominoes

OEIS A000105 counts free polyominoes, identified under translations, rotations and reflections.

Each free polyomino corresponds to between one and eight fixed polyominoes. Therefore

$$
\frac{T_n}{8}\leq P_n\leq T_n,
\qquad U_n\leq B_n.
$$

It follows that

$$
0\leq\frac{U_n}{P_n}
\leq8\frac{B_n}{T_n}
\leq8\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^n.
$$

Because $\beta/\alpha<1$,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{U_n}{P_n}=0.}
$$

Using $H_n=P_n-U_n$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{A001419(n)}{A000105(n)}=1.
}
$$

**Q.E.D.**

**Stronger conclusion (known consequence):** The fraction of hole-free polyominoes decays exponentially: there exist constants $C>0$ and $0<\rho<1$ such that

$$
\frac{A000104(n)}{A000105(n)}
\leq C\rho^n
$$

for all sufficiently large $n$.

No numerical value of $\rho$ is established here. The theorem guarantees its existence.

This derivation is an explicit specialization of known results, not a claim of original mathematical discovery.

## 4. Verdict

**PROVED — already known; high confidence.** The A001419 conjecture is true and follows from Neal Madras, *A pattern theorem for lattice clusters*, *Annals of Combinatorics* 3 (1999), 357–384. Johann Peters explicitly noted this fact in OEIS A000105 on January 6, 2024. The argument above identifies a proper local pattern that forces a hole and applies Madras's theorem to show that hole-free polyominoes constitute an exponentially vanishing fraction of all polyominoes. An independent exhaustive computation for $1\leq n\leq12$ reproduces the OEIS counts and confirms the local implication throughout that range. **No new mathematical theorem is claimed.** The literature search found no conflicting result, although direct MathSciNet access and exhaustive Zenodo coverage were unavailable. A suitable future OEIS comment, subject to the OEIS AI-submission policy and independent human verification, would be:

> The conjecture that almost all polyominoes have holes is true by Madras's pattern theorem for lattice clusters (Ann. Combin. 3 (1999), 357–384). A proper local pattern consisting of eight occupied cells surrounding one vacant cell forces a hole. The theorem implies that the number of polyominoes avoiding this pattern, and hence the number of hole-free polyominoes, has a strictly smaller exponential growth rate than the total number of polyominoes. Therefore A001419(n)/A000105(n) tends to 1, with exponentially vanishing error. Cf. the comment by Johann Peters in A000105, Jan 06 2024.
