# OEIS A167809 — The Catalan Lower Bound for Admissible Postage-Stamp Bases

**Research report — November 12, 2026**

## 1. Status and literature review

Proved in this report

**Main result.** For every integer $n\geq 1$,

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mathrm{A167809}(n)\geq \mathrm{A000108}(n)
=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.}
$$

An elementary injection from Dyck paths to admissible two-stamp bases proves the conjecture for all $n$. No computational assumptions are needed.

### 1.1. Previously recorded status

OEIS [A167809](https://oeis.org/A167809) defines the sequence as the number of admissible bases with $n$ denominations and $h=2$ stamps.

The entry attributes the following conjecture to **Michael Chu, May 16, 2022**:

> Conjecture: a(n) >= A000108(n).

The available OEIS entry and its supplied snapshot still label this as a conjecture, without a proof.

The comparison sequence, [A000108](https://oeis.org/A000108), consists of the Catalan numbers. OEIS explicitly identifies these as counting Dyck paths of length $2n$, providing the combinatorial interpretation used below.

## 2. Proof

### 2.1. Precise definitions

Let

$$
B=\{b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n\},\qquad
1=b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_n,
$$

be a set of positive integers.

Call $B$ **admissible for $h=2$** if, for every integer $x$ with $1\leq x\leq b_n$, at least one of the following holds:

1. $x=b_i$ for some $i$;
2. $x=b_i+b_j$ for some $i,j$, possibly equal.

Let $a(n)$ denote the number of such bases.

Let

$$
C_n=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
$$

be the $n$-th Catalan number.

We prove $a(n)\geq C_n$ by constructing $C_n$ distinct admissible bases.

### 2.2. Key lemma: a complementary-pair argument

**Lemma (proved).** Let $x$ and $k$ be positive integers, with $x\leq 2k$. If $S$ is a set of $k$ distinct positive integers strictly smaller than $x$, then there exist $u,v\in S$, possibly equal, such that

$$
u+v=x.
$$

**Proof.**

Partition $\{1,\ldots,x-1\}$ into complementary pairs

$$
\{t,x-t\}.
$$

When $x$ is even, the midpoint $x/2$ is a singleton.

Suppose, for contradiction, that no two elements of $S$, including two copies of the same element, sum to $x$.

Then $S$ contains at most one element from each complementary pair. If $x$ is even, $S$ cannot contain $x/2$, since two copies of that denomination would sum to $x$.

Consequently,

$$
|S|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{x-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$

But $x\leq 2k$, so

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{x-1}{2}\right\rfloor
\leq k-1.
$$

This contradicts $|S|=k$.

Therefore $x=u+v$ for some $u,v\in S$. $\square$

### 2.3. A Catalan family of admissible bases

**Proposition (proved).** Every strictly increasing sequence

$$
1=b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_n
$$

satisfying

$$
\boxed{b_i\leq 2i-1\qquad(1\leq i\leq n)}
$$

is an admissible two-stamp basis.

**Proof.**

Fix any integer $x$ with $1\leq x\leq b_n$.

Let $j$ be the smallest index such that

$$
x\leq b_j.
$$

There are two cases.

**Case 1: $x=b_j$.**

Then $x$ is directly representable with one stamp.

**Case 2: $x<b_j$.**

By minimality of $j$, we have

$$
b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_{j-1}<x.
$$

Thus the first $j-1$ denominations form a set of $j-1$ distinct positive integers less than $x$.

Moreover, the defining upper bound gives

$$
x\leq b_j-1\leq 2j-2=2(j-1).
$$

Apply the lemma with $k=j-1$. It follows that

$$
x=b_r+b_s
$$

for some $r,s<j$, with $r=s$ permitted.

Therefore every $x\in[1,b_n]$ is representable using at most two stamps.

Hence $B$ is admissible. $\square$

### 2.4. Counting these bases by Dyck paths

A Dyck path of semilength $n$ is a word of $n$ up-steps $U$ and $n$ down-steps $D$ such that every prefix contains at least as many $U$'s as $D$'s.

For a Dyck path, let

$$
b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_n
$$

be the positions of its up-steps, numbered from $1$ to $2n$.

Since a Dyck path begins with an up-step,

$$
b_1=1.
$$

Immediately before the $i$-th up-step, there are exactly $i-1$ up-steps and $b_i-i$ down-steps.

The Dyck-prefix condition implies

$$
b_i-i\leq i-1,
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{b_i\leq 2i-1.}
$$

By the proposition, these up-step positions form an admissible two-stamp basis.

The construction is injective: distinct Dyck paths have distinct sets of up-step positions, since those positions uniquely determine the entire word.

There are exactly

$$
C_n=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
$$

Dyck paths of semilength $n$.

Therefore there are at least $C_n$ admissible bases of length $n$.

### 2.5. Theorem

**Theorem (proved).** For every $n\geq 1$,

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle a(n)\geq C_n.}
$$

**Proof.** The injective map

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\text{Dyck paths of semilength }n\}
&\longrightarrow
\{\text{admissible two-stamp bases of length }n\},\\
P&\longmapsto\{\text{positions of up-steps in }P\}
\end{aligned}
$$

is well-defined by the proposition in Section 2.3.

Thus the cardinality of the target is at least the cardinality of the source, namely $C_n$. $\blacksquare$

No reduction to an open problem is required. The stated conjecture is proved for all positive integers.

### 3.1. Exhaustive enumeration of admissible bases

The following program enumerates every admissible basis using the standard prefix-extension property: if $g$ is the first positive integer not representable by the current basis, the next denomination must lie between the previous denomination plus one and $g$, inclusive.

It also checks that every generated basis covers $[1,2n]$, an additional consequence of the complementary-pair lemma.

```python

from math import comb

N = 10
counts = [0] * (N + 1)
dyck = [0] * (N + 1)

def cover(B):
    return {0, *B} | {x+y for x in B for y in B}

def search(B):
    n = len(B)
    counts[n] += 1
    dyck[n] += all(b <= 2*i-1 for i,b in enumerate(B,1))

    S = cover(B)
    assert all(x in S for x in range(1, 2*n+1)), B

    if n == N:
        return

    gap = next(x for x in range(1, max(S)+2) if x not in S)

    for b in range(B[-1]+1, gap+1):
        search(B+(b,))

search((1,))

print('n: a(n) / Catalan subfamily / C_n')
for n in range(1, N+1):
    C = comb(2*n,n)//(n+1)
    print(f'{n}: {counts[n]} / {dyck[n]} / {C}')
    assert counts[n] >= dyck[n] == C

assert counts[1:] == [
    1,2,5,17,65,292,1434,7875,47098,305226
]
print('PASS')
```

### 3.2. Independent check of the Catalan construction

The next program constructs the bounded sequences directly, without using the admissible-basis search procedure. It tests each sequence by explicitly constructing every one- and two-stamp sum.

```python

from math import comb

def catalan_bases(n, B=(1,)):
    if len(B) == n:
        yield B
    else:
        i = len(B) + 1
        for b in range(B[-1] + 1, 2*i):
            yield from catalan_bases(n, B + (b,))

for n in range(1, 13):
    total = 0
    for B in catalan_bases(n):
        cover = set(B) | {u + v for u in B for v in B}
        assert all(x in cover for x in range(1, B[-1] + 1)), B
        total += 1

    assert total == comb(2*n, n) // (n+1)
    print(f'n={n:2d}: {total:6d} Catalan bases, all admissible')

print('PASS: direct two-stamp verification for all Catalan bases through n=12.')
```

### 3.3. Exhaustive check of the key lemma

To independently test the complementary-pair lemma, every subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,x-1\}$ with $|S|=k$ and $x\leq2k$ was examined for $2\leq x\leq18$.

```python

from itertools import combinations

checked = 0
for x in range(2, 19):
    for k in range((x+1)//2, x):
        for S in combinations(range(1, x), k):
            T = set(S)
            assert any(x-u in T for u in S), (x, k, S)
            checked += 1

print(f'PASS: complementary-pair lemma exhaustive for 2 <= x <= 18 ({checked} sets).')
```

**Proof of Michael Chu's conjecture:** For h = 2, a(n) >= C_n = binomial(2*n,n)/(n+1). Map each Dyck path of semilength n to the basis whose denominations b_1 < ... < b_n are the positions of its up-steps. Then b_1 = 1 and b_i <= 2*i-1. By pairing t with x-t, any set of j-1 distinct positive integers below x must contain two, possibly equal, summing to x. Thus every such basis is admissible. The construction is injective, and there are C_n Dyck paths. Hence a(n) >= C_n for every n >= 1.
