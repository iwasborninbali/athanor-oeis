# OEIS A334916 — Status and proof attempt: uniquely baseless number bases

**Report date:** 12 November 2026 **Source:** [Athanor task specification](https://github.com/iwasborninbali/athanor-oeis/blob/main/tasks/2026-11-12-A334916-baseless-unique-bases/TASK.md)

## 1. Status

**Verdict: Open, as far as the accessible literature establishes.** The conjecture has not been proved or refuted by the sources located. Two infinite families of bases having at least two baseless numbers can be constructed explicitly, but they do not settle whether another base has exactly one.

### Precise statement

Let $b\ge 2$ be an integer base. For the base-$b$ digits $d_1,\ldots,d_k$, with $d_1\ne0$, define

$$
N_b(d_1,\ldots,d_k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_i b^{k-i}
$$

and

$$
F(d_1,\ldots,d_k)
=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_i\prod_{j=i}^{k}d_j.
$$

A *baseless number in base $b$* is an integer $N>1$ satisfying $N=F(d_1,\ldots,d_k)$, where the digits are those of $N$ in base $b$.

Write

$$
S_b=\{N>1:N\text{ is baseless in base }b\}.
$$

The OEIS question is the proposition

$$
\boxed{\exists\,b\ge4,\quad b\notin\{6,10\},
\quad |S_b|=1.}
$$

This is mathematically well-posed once expressed as a proposition. It is different from the still-open existence question asking whether $S_b\ne\varnothing$ for every $b\ge4$.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

### 2.1. A complete characterization of two-digit solutions

**Proved.** For a two-digit number $(u,v)_b$, the defining equation is

$$
ub+v=(u^2+v)v.
$$

Therefore

$$
ub=u^2v+v(v-1),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{b=uv+\frac{v(v-1)}{u}.}
\tag{1}
$$

Conversely, any positive integers $u,v$ satisfying

$$
1\le u,v<b,\qquad
u\mid v(v-1),\qquad
b=uv+\frac{v(v-1)}{u}
$$

produce a two-digit baseless number.

Thus equation (1), together with the digit restrictions, characterizes **every two-digit baseless number**.

### 2.2. An infinite two-parameter family of nonunique bases

**Proved.** Let $u,v$ be distinct positive integers. Set

$$
t=uv+1,\qquad
b=t(u+v).
$$

Then the two distinct base-$b$ numbers

$$
(u,t)_b,\qquad (v,t)_b
$$

are both baseless.

**Proof.** Since $u,v\ge1$ and $u\ne v$, both numbers are valid two-digit base-$b$ representations. Moreover,

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_b(u,t)
&=ub+t\\
&=ut(u+v)+t\\
&=u^2t+uvt+t\\
&=u^2t+t^2\\
&=(u^2+t)t\\
&=F(u,t).
\end{aligned}
$$

The same calculation with $u$ and $v$ interchanged gives

$$
N_b(v,t)=(v^2+t)t=F(v,t).
$$

Their leading digits differ, so the resulting integers are distinct. $\square$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
b=(uv+1)(u+v),\quad u\ne v
\implies |S_b|\ge2.
}
\tag{2}
$$

For example, $u=2,v=3$ gives $t=7$ and $b=35$, producing

$$
(2,7)_{35}=77,\qquad (3,7)_{35}=112.
$$

The square-base family follows immediately by taking $u=1,\ v=r-1$:

$$
\boxed{r\ge3\implies |S_{r^2}|\ge2.}
$$

This is consistent with the square-base example and the base-9 solutions already discussed by Veselovac. The presentation here is a direct elementary construction, not a claim of priority.

### 2.3. A second infinite family: two- and three-digit witnesses

**Proved.** For every integer $r\ge2$, the base

$$
\boxed{b=r^2+r-1}
$$

has at least two distinct baseless numbers.

The witnesses are

$$
(r,r)_b
\quad\text{and}\quad
(r,r,r^2)_b.
$$

All their digits are valid because $r^2<b$.

For the two-digit witness,

$$
N_b(r,r)=rb+r=r^3+r^2,
$$

while

$$
F(r,r)=(r^2+r)r=r^3+r^2.
$$

For the three-digit witness,

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_b(r,r,r^2)
&=rb^2+rb+r^2\\
&=r(r^2+r-1)^2+r(r^2+r-1)+r^2\\
&=r^5+2r^4.
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand,

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r,r,r^2)
&=\bigl((r^2+r)r+r^2\bigr)r^2\\
&=(r^3+2r^2)r^2\\
&=r^5+2r^4.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus both representations satisfy the defining identity. They have different lengths and therefore represent different integers. $\square$

Some instances:

| $r$ | Base $b$ | Two-digit witness | Three-digit witness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 5 | 12 | 64 |
| 3 | 11 | 36 | 405 |
| 4 | 19 | 80 | 1536 |
| 5 | 29 | 150 | 4375 |
| 6 | 41 | 252 | 10368 |

These two infinite constructions rule out infinitely many bases as candidates for uniqueness. They do not show that every other base possesses multiple solutions.

### 2.4. A rigorous finite-search bound

**Proved.** Every base $b\ge2$ has only finitely many baseless numbers, and the required maximum digit length is effectively computable.

First, a baseless number greater than 1 cannot contain a zero digit.

Let $m=b-1$, and let $M_k$ be the maximum value of $F$ over all strings of $k$ digits. Since every recurrence step $(x+d)d$ is increasing in the nonnegative variables, the maximum occurs when every digit is $m$. Thus

$$
M_0=0,\qquad M_k=mM_{k-1}+m^2.
$$

We have

$$
M_k<b^{k+1}
$$

for every $k\ge0$. For $k=1$ this follows from $m^2<b^2$. For $k\ge2$, induction gives

$$
M_k<m b^k+m^2<b^{k+1},
$$

because $m^2<b^k$.

Suppose a $d$-digit candidate has a zero at position $j\ge2$, leaving $k=d-j$ digits. That zero resets the running value of $F$ to zero, so

$$
F\le M_k<b^{k+1}\le b^{d-1}\le N.
$$

Equality is impossible.

Hence **all digits of a nontrivial baseless number are positive**.

The smallest possible $d$-digit candidate is therefore

$$
L_d=11\cdots1_b=\frac{b^d-1}{b-1}.
$$

The largest possible value of $F$ is $M_d$, defined above.

If

$$
L_d>M_d,
\tag{3}
$$

no $d$-digit solution exists.

Furthermore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{d+1}-M_{d+1}
&=b(L_d-M_d)+M_d+1-m^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $M_d\ge m^2$ for $d\ge1$, once condition (3) holds, it continues to hold at every greater length.

Therefore the first length $D_b\ge2$ satisfying $L_{D_b}>M_{D_b}$ is a rigorous stopping point. Such a length exists because $b>m$, so $L_d$ eventually grows faster than $M_d$.

This gives a finite decision procedure for $|S_b|=1$ for **each fixed base**.

The finiteness principle is already known from the 2020 sources; the bound and recurrence here make it directly checkable.

## 3. Computational check

### Exhaustive search

I implemented an independent C++17 program using arbitrary-precision integers.

For any fixed prefix, let $n$ be its current positional value, $f$ its current baseless recurrence value, and $k$ the number of remaining digits.

Every completion has positional value at least

$$
n b^k+L_k
$$

and baseless evaluation at most

$$
m^k f+M_k.
$$

A prefix is therefore safely discarded whenever

$$
n b^k+L_k>m^k f+M_k.
\tag{4}
$$

This is a necessary-condition pruning rule; it does not assume that the difference $N-F$ is monotonic in the digits.

C++17 — exact implementation executed

```

#include <boost/multiprecision/cpp_int.hpp>
#include <iostream>
#include <vector>
#include <algorithm>
using boost::multiprecision::cpp_int;
using namespace std;

struct Search {
    int b, d;
    vector<cpp_int> powb, rep, fac, add;
    vector<cpp_int> hits;
    unsigned long long leaf=0, nodes=0;

    Search(int base,int len): b(base),d(len),
      powb(len+1),rep(len+1),fac(len+1),add(len+1) {
        powb[0]=1; rep[0]=0;
        fac[0]=1; add[0]=0;
        for (int j=1;j<=d;j++) {
            powb[j]=powb[j-1]*b;
            rep[j]=rep[j-1]*b+1;
            fac[j]=fac[j-1]*(b-1);
            add[j]=add[j-1]*(b-1)+(b-1)*(b-1);
        }
    }

    void dfs(int at, const cpp_int& n, const cpp_int& f) {
        ++nodes;
        int r=d-at;
        if(n*powb[r]+rep[r] > f*fac[r]+add[r])
            return;
        if(at==d) {
            ++leaf;
            if(n==f) hits.push_back(n);
            return;
        }
        for(int digit=1;digit<b;digit++)
            dfs(at+1,n*b+digit,(f+digit)*digit);
    }
};

int main(int argc,char** argv){
  int end=argc>1?stoi(argv[1]):10;
  for(int b=2;b<=end;b++) {
    vector<cpp_int> solutions;
    unsigned long long leaves=0,nodes=0;
    int d=2;
    for(; ;d++) {
       Search s(b,d);
       if (s.rep[d] > s.add[d]) break;
       s.dfs(0,0,0);
       leaves+=s.leaf; nodes+=s.nodes;
       solutions.insert(solutions.end(),
                        s.hits.begin(),s.hits.end());
    }
    sort(solutions.begin(),solutions.end());
    cout << "base=" << b
         << " first_excluded_length=" << d
         << " count=" << solutions.size()
         << " values=";
    for(size_t j=0;j<solutions.size();j++)
        cout << (j?",":"") << solutions[j];
    cout << " leaves=" << leaves
         << " nodes=" << nodes << "\n";
  }
}
```
