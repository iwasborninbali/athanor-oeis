# OEIS A004044 — Status and proof attempt

**Investigation date:** 12 November 2026 **Conjecture:** Thomas Ordowski (2021) **Verdict:** Open for $m\ge 3$; proved for $m=1,2$.

## 1. Status

Let $a(n)=K_3(n,1)$ denote the minimum cardinality of a subset $C\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$ such that every word in $\mathbb F_3^n$ has Hamming distance at most one from some element of $C$.

The conjecture, recorded by Thomas Ordowski in OEIS A004044 on 10 July 2021, states

$$
\boxed{
a\left(\frac{3^m+1}{2}\right)
=3^{(3^m+1)/2-m}
\quad(m\ge1).
}
$$

The statement is well-defined. It concerns the domination number of the ternary Hamming graph $K_3^{\square n}$.

### Literature findings

**Known — $m=1,2$.** H. J. L. Kamps and J. H. van Lint, *The Football Pool Problem for 5 Matches*, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory* **3** (1967), 315–325, prove $a(5)=27$. Their decisive statement is:

> We shall show that there is no solution using less than 27 forecasts.

This is established in Theorem 2.1 of their paper.

The case $a(2)=3$ is elementary and is independently verified below.

**Open — $m\ge3$, according to the literature located.** Javier Marenco and Pablo A. Rey, *An initial polyhedral study of the football pool problem*, *Discrete Optimization* **60** (2026), article 100946, DOI [10.1016/j.disopt.2026.100946](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disopt.2026.100946), state:

> The football pool problem for the remaining values of $n\ge6$ is an important open combinatorial problem.

The live [OEIS A004044 entry](https://oeis.org/A004044) still explicitly labels Ordowski's formula a conjecture. Its cross-reference A060439 supplies no resolution.

Dion Gijswijt and Sven Polak, *Semidefinite lower bounds for covering codes*, *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* **72** (2026), no. 9, 6599–6614, DOI [10.1109/TIT.2026.3706793](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2026.3706793), develop improved semidefinite bounds. Their arXiv version, [2504.01932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01932), Appendix B, reports a numerical SDP bound of approximately $166229.5226$ for $K_3(14,1)$. It does not establish the conjectured equality.

A historical covering-code bounds table (reported as current in 2011) gives

This interval is useful historical evidence of the unresolved gap, but I did not independently verify the underlying proof of its lower endpoint.

## 2. Proof attempt and strongest partial result

The conjecture can be established **for all linear and affine ternary covering codes**, but the unrestricted problem allows nonlinear codes. This is an elementary consequence of standard syndrome counting, not claimed as a new discovery.

### Theorem — exact linear optimum

For every integer $m\ge1$, set

$$
n=\frac{3^m+1}{2}.
$$

Among all linear or affine radius-one covering codes $C\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$, the minimum cardinality is exactly

$$
\boxed{|C|_{\min,\mathrm{linear}}=3^{n-m}.}
$$

**Proof (lower bound).** Let $C$ be a linear code of dimension $k$, codimension $r=n-k$, and covering radius at most one. Choose a rank-$r$ parity-check matrix $P$.

Each syndrome in $\mathbb F_3^r$ must be realizable by an error vector of Hamming weight at most one. There are precisely $1+2n$ such error vectors, so

$$
3^r\le 1+2n=3^m+2<3^{m+1}.
$$

Consequently $r\le m$, hence $k\ge n-m$, and

$$
|C|=3^k\ge 3^{n-m}.
$$

Translations preserve covering radius and cardinality, so the same bound holds for affine codes.

**Proof (upper bound).** Put $N=n-1=(3^m-1)/2$. Construct an $m\times N$ parity-check matrix $H$ whose columns contain exactly one representative from each pair $\{v,-v\}$ of nonzero vectors in $\mathbb F_3^m$.

The kernel

$$
D=\{x\in\mathbb F_3^N:Hx=0\}
$$

has dimension $N-m$. Each nonzero syndrome is uniquely $\pm$ one of the columns of $H$, so every $x\in\mathbb F_3^N$ is at distance at most one from $D$. This is the standard perfect ternary Hamming-code construction.

Now set

$$
C=D\times\mathbb F_3\subseteq\mathbb F_3^{N+1}.
$$

For every $(x,t)\in\mathbb F_3^{N+1}$, choose $d\in D$ with $d(x,d)\le1$. Then $(d,t)\in C$ covers $(x,t)$, and

$$
|C|=3|D|
=3\cdot3^{N-m}
=3^{n-m}.
$$

Together with the lower bound, this proves the theorem. $\square$

### What remains open

The preceding proof does **not** exclude a nonlinear covering code with fewer than $3^{n-m}$ words. That is precisely the unresolved possibility.

For arbitrary (possibly nonlinear) codes, counting radius-one Hamming balls proves the universal sphere bound

$$
\boxed{
\left\lceil\frac{3^n}{2n+1}\right\rceil
\le a(n)\le3^{n-m}
\quad\text{when }n=\frac{3^m+1}{2}.
}
$$

The upper bound has just been constructed explicitly. The lower bound counts $3^n$ words, each codeword covering at most $2n+1$ words.

For the first cases:

| $m$ | $n$ | Sphere lower bound | Exact or constructed upper bound | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | Proved: $a(2)=3$ |
| 2 | 5 | 23 | 27 | Proved: $a(5)=27$ |
| 3 | 14 | 164930 | 177147 | Open |

The $m=3$ case is therefore

$$
\boxed{a(14)\stackrel{?}=177147.}
$$

Any counterexample would necessarily be nonlinear. The 2011 historical lower bound $166610$ remains strictly below the constructed upper bound.

## 3. Computational check

The following self-contained Python 3 program constructs canonical projective parity-check columns over $\mathbb F_3$, exhaustively enumerates all prefix syndromes for $m=1,2,3$, verifies that every syndrome is covered by a weight-at-most-one correction, counts the linear codewords, checks the relevant inequalities, and independently proves $a(2)\ge3$ by testing every pair of radius-one balls.

```python

from collections import Counter
from itertools import combinations, product

def addition_table(m):
    q = 3 ** m
    def add(u, v):
        ans, power = 0, 1
        for _ in range(m):
            ans += ((u % 3 + v % 3) % 3) * power
            u //= 3
            v //= 3
            power *= 3
        return ans
    return [[add(u, v) for v in range(q)] for u in range(q)]

for m in (1, 2, 3):
    vectors = list(product(range(3), repeat=m))
    columns = [sum(x * 3**i for i, x in enumerate(v))
               for v in vectors if next((x for x in v if x), 0) == 1]
    N = len(columns)
    plus = addition_table(m)
    correction_syndromes = {0}
    for v in columns:
        correction_syndromes.update((v, plus[v][v]))
    syndromes = [0]
    for v in columns:
        twice = plus[v][v]
        old = syndromes
        syndromes = old + [plus[x][v] for x in old] + [plus[x][twice] for x in old]
    hist = Counter(syndromes)
    assert N == (3**m - 1) // 2
    assert len(syndromes) == 3**N
    assert len(correction_syndromes) == 3**m
    assert correction_syndromes == set(range(3**m))
    assert len(hist) == 3**m
    assert set(hist.values()) == {3**(N-m)}
    center_count = 3 * hist[0]
    n = N + 1
    assert center_count == 3**(n-m)
    assert 3**(m+1) > 2*n+1
    sphere_lower = (3**n + 2*n) // (2*n + 1)
    print(f'm={m} n={n} prefixes={len(syndromes)} kernel={hist[0]} '
          f'centers={center_count} syndromes={len(hist)}/{3**m} '
          f'sphere_lower={sphere_lower} linear_min={3**(n-m)} cover=YES')

words = list(product(range(3), repeat=2))
def ball(c):
    return {w for w in words if sum(a != b for a, b in zip(w, c)) <= 1}
max_two = max(len(ball(u) | ball(v)) for u, v in combinations(words, 2))
assert max_two == 8
print(f'n=2: max_union_of_two_balls={max_two}/{len(words)}; optimal=3')
```

For $m=3$, the computation exhaustively enumerates all $3^{13}=1,594,323$ length-13 prefixes. The syndrome property proves that the resulting 177,147-word length-14 code covers all $3^{14}=4,782,969$ words. This certifies the construction, **not its unrestricted minimum cardinality**.

The computation also shows that two radius-one balls cover at most eight of the nine words of length two. Together with the constructed three-word covering, this proves $a(2)=3$. For $a(5)=27$, the lower bound comes from Kamps and van Lint's published theorem, not from the computational test.

## 4. Verdict

**Status: open for $m\ge3$, proved for $m=1,2$, with no counterexample found.** The unrestricted conjecture is not settled by this investigation. The strongest result established here is the elementary exact formula for the minimum size of a *linear or affine* ternary radius-one covering code at every conjectured length; it is standard coding-theoretic reasoning and is **not claimed as new**. Exhaustive syndrome enumeration verified the Hamming-code construction through $m=3$, but nonlinear optimality at $n=14$ remains unresolved. Confidence is high in the proofs and computations and moderate-to-high in the literature-status assessment, subject to the stated search limitations.
