# OEIS A058129: Status and Proof Attempt for the Monoid-Counting Asymptotic

**Investigation date:** 12 November 2026 **Target:** OEIS [A058129](https://oeis.org/A058129) **Claim:** $a(n)\sim \mathrm{A027851}(n-1)$

## 1. Status and literature

### 1.1 Precise formulation

Let

- $s(n)$ be the number of isomorphism classes of semigroups of order $n$, OEIS A027851.
- $m(n)$ be the number of isomorphism classes of monoids of order $n$, OEIS A058129.

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{m(n)}{s(n-1)}=1.}
\tag{C}
$$

It is well-posed. The denominator is positive for all positive $n$.

**Status: Open as far as the literature located establishes.** There is a published asymptotic theorem for labeled monoids, but an unresolved issue with a foundational semigroup-enumeration result prevents treating that theorem as an unquestioned resolution of the OEIS conjecture.

The distinction between labeled structures and isomorphism classes is also essential.

### 1.2 Decisive sources

**Koubek and Rödl (1985).** Václav Koubek and Vojtěch Rödl, *Note on the number of monoids of order n*, Commentationes Mathematicae Universitatis Carolinae **26**(2), 309–314, Theorem 2, p. 310.

Writing $M(n)$ and $S(n)$ for numbers of multiplication tables on a fixed labeled $n$-element set, they state:

> “M(n+1) = M₃(n+1) (1+o(1)) = (n+1)S(n)(1+o(1)).”

This is a published claim about **labeled** structures, not directly about A058129.

**Dolinka, FitzGerald and Mitchell (2026).** Igor Dolinka, D. G. FitzGerald and James D. Mitchell, *Semirigidity and the enumeration of nilpotent semigroups of index three*, Semigroup Forum **112**, 24–47 (2026); arXiv:2411.00466, Section 1.

Their discussion of the 1976 argument by Kleitman, Rothschild and Spencer states:

> “Later opinion ... regards their argument as incomplete, and no satisfactory proof has been found.”

This is particularly important because the 1985 proof explicitly invokes that earlier argument as its Theorem 1.

The 2026 paper also describes the predominance of 3-nilpotent semigroups among all semigroups as a belief supported by strong evidence, rather than an established result.

**Grillet (2015).** Pierre A. Grillet, *Counting Semigroups*, Communications in Algebra **43**(2), 574–596 (published online 25 August 2014), Theorem 4.5.

The paper proves that almost all finite 3-nilpotent semigroups are rigid. This is an unconditional asymptotic result **within the 3-nilpotent class**, not a theorem that this class contains almost all semigroups.

**Distler and Mitchell (2012).** Andreas Distler and James D. Mitchell, *The Number of Nilpotent Semigroups of Degree 3*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics **19**(2), P51. Electronic Journal of Combinatorics

They give exact enumeration formulas for 3-nilpotent semigroups, including counts up to isomorphism. Their abstract describes the assertion that almost all finite semigroups are 3-nilpotent as folklore, rather than claiming to establish it.

### 1.3 Literature conclusion

The difficulty is not a missing factor of $n!$. The 1985 labeled result relies on a disputed asymptotic input, while the modern rigidity results concern a restricted class.

Consequently, citing Koubek–Rödl (1985) alone as a proof of (C) would not meet the requested standard of a fully checkable argument.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

### 2.1 Exact reduction to the group of units

**Proposition 1 (proved).** For every $n\ge2$,

$$
\boxed{m(n)=s(n-1)+u(n),}
\tag{1}
$$

where $u(n)$ counts isomorphism classes of order-$n$ monoids whose group of units has at least two elements.

**Proof.**

Let $M$ be a finite monoid, with identity $1$, and let $U(M)$ be its group of invertible elements.

The set

$$
S=M\setminus U(M)
$$

is closed under multiplication. Indeed, if $xy$ is invertible, then $x$ has a right inverse and $y$ has a left inverse. In a finite monoid, either property implies invertibility. Thus $xy\in U(M)$ implies $x,y\in U(M)$.

In particular, when $U(M)=\{1\}$, the complement $M\setminus\{1\}$ is a semigroup of order $n-1$.

Conversely, given any semigroup $S$ of order $n-1$, adjoin a fresh element $1$, with

$$
1x=x1=x
$$

for every $x\in S\cup\{1\}$.

The resulting monoid $S^1$ has no invertible elements other than $1$, since products of elements of $S$ remain in $S$.

These constructions are inverse to one another on isomorphism classes: every isomorphism of unitizations fixes the new identity, and its restriction is an isomorphism of the original semigroups.

Therefore precisely $s(n-1)$ monoid isomorphism classes have trivial unit group. Partitioning the remaining classes proves (1). $\square$

**Corollary (proved).** The OEIS conjecture (C) is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{\frac{u(n)}{s(n-1)}\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{2}
$$

Equivalently, a uniformly chosen isomorphism class of order-$n$ monoids has a trivial group of units with probability tending to $1$.

This is an exact reformulation of the problem, not a proof of its asymptotic assertion. The underlying unitization construction is standard; no novelty is claimed.

### 2.2 Why the labeled result does not suffice

Let $S_{\mathrm{lab}}(n)$ and $M_{\mathrm{lab}}(n)$ be the numbers of labeled semigroups and monoids, respectively.

There is an exact identity

$$
M_{\mathrm{lab}}(n)
=
nS_{\mathrm{lab}}(n-1)+E_{\mathrm{lab}}(n),
\tag{3}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{lab}}(n)$ counts labeled monoids having nontrivial unit groups.

The factor $n$ chooses the identity element.

However, the orbit-stabilizer formula gives

$$
S_{\mathrm{lab}}(n)
=
\sum_{[S]}\frac{n!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(S)|},
$$

and analogously for monoids. Consequently, an asymptotic statement about labeled multiplication tables cannot automatically be transferred to isomorphism classes without controlling automorphism groups.

There is also a technical warning within the published 1985 argument. On pp. 312–313, a bound involving

$$
(n!)^2 n^{-(2n-2)(1+o(1))}
$$

is used to conclude negligibility. This conclusion does not follow from the displayed form alone: an unspecified $o(1)$ in the exponent may be negative and can outweigh the factorial decay. For example, choosing the $o(1)$ term as $-\log\log n/\log n$ makes this expression divergent.

This observation identifies a limitation of that displayed estimate; it is not a counterexample to the theorem itself.

### 2.3 An unconditional asymptotic partial result

The following result avoids the disputed Kleitman–Rothschild–Spencer asymptotic.

**Theorem (proved here; no claim of literature novelty).**

Fix $\delta>0$, and define

$$
K_n=\left\lceil
\left(\frac12+\delta\right)
\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}
\right\rceil.
$$

Let $u_{\ge K_n}(n)$ be the number of isomorphism classes of order-$n$ monoids whose group of units has at least $K_n$ elements.

Then

$$
\boxed{
\frac{u_{\ge K_n}(n)}{s(n-1)}
\le
\exp\left(-(2\delta+o(1))n^2\log\log n\right)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4}
$$

All logarithms in this theorem are natural.

Thus monoids whose unit groups are sufficiently large are asymptotically negligible compared with the number of semigroups of order $n-1$.

#### Proof

**Step 1: A lower bound for semigroups.**

Let $q$ be sufficiently large and put

$$
t=\left\lfloor\frac{q}{\log q}\right\rfloor.
$$

Partition a fixed labeled $q$-element set into $A\sqcup B$, where $|B|=t$, and distinguish $0\in B$.

Define multiplication by choosing arbitrarily

$$
A\times A\longrightarrow B
$$

and stipulating that every product involving an element of $B$ equals $0$.

Every such operation is associative because every product of three elements equals $0$, regardless of parenthesization.

There are exactly

$$
t^{(q-t)^2}
$$

distinct multiplication tables of this form.

Since an isomorphism class contains at most $q!$ labeled tables,

$$
s(q)\ge\frac{t^{(q-t)^2}}{q!}.
\tag{5}
$$

Taking logarithms and using the definition of $t$,

$$
\log s(q)\ge
q^2(\log q-\log\log q)-O(q^2).
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{
\log s(n-1)\ge
n^2\log n-n^2\log\log n-O(n^2).
}
\tag{6}
$$

This bound is independent of any unproved statement about typical semigroups.

**Step 2: Count possible unit groups.**

Write $G(k)$ for the number of labeled group structures on a fixed $k$-element set.

Every group of order $k$ can be generated by at most

$$
r=\lceil\log_2 k\rceil
$$

elements. To see this, start with the trivial subgroup and repeatedly add an element outside the subgroup already generated. Each addition increases the subgroup's order by at least a factor of two.

A labeled group is determined by its identity, a chosen generating tuple, and the left multiplication permutations associated with those generators.

Indeed, composing those permutations and their inverses reconstructs left multiplication by every group element, and hence the complete multiplication table.

Counting these descriptions gives

$$
G(k)\le
k\sum_{j=0}^{r}(k\cdot k!)^j.
$$

Therefore, uniformly for $1\le k\le n$,

$$
\boxed{\log G(k)=O(n\log^2 n).}
\tag{7}
$$

**Step 3: Count monoids with $k$ units.**

Consider a labeled monoid $M$ of order $n$ with $|U(M)|=k$, and write $h=n-k$.

Its multiplication is specified by:

1. The $k$-element subset $U(M)$.
2. A group multiplication on $U(M)$.
3. A semigroup multiplication on its $h$-element complement.
4. Left and right multiplication on the complement by a generating set of the group.

Left and right multiplication by a unit restrict to permutations of the complement. There are at most $(h!)^{2r}$ possible pairs of permutation families for $r$ generators.

Once these data are fixed, every mixed product involving a unit is determined, because every unit is a word in the selected generators and their inverses.

We deliberately ignore compatibility conditions; doing so gives an upper bound.

The number $L_{n,k}$ of labeled monoids with exactly $k$ units therefore satisfies

$$
L_{n,k}
\le
\binom nk\,G(k)\,
\max\{1,h^{h^2}\}\,
(h!)^{2r}.
\tag{8}
$$

Here the factor $1$ handles $h=0$.

Since $r=O(\log n)$, the factors outside $h^{h^2}$ contribute at most $\exp(O(n\log^2 n))$.

Thus

$$
\log L_{n,k}
\le
h^2\log n+O(n\log^2 n).
\tag{9}
$$

**Step 4: Sum over large unit groups.**

For $k\ge K_n$, we have $h\le n-K_n$. Summing (9) over at most $n$ possible values of $k$ gives

$$
u_{\ge K_n}(n)
\le
\sum_{k=K_n}^{n}L_{n,k}
$$

and hence

$$
\log u_{\ge K_n}(n)
\le
(n-K_n)^2\log n+O(n\log^2 n).
\tag{10}
$$

The conversion from labeled tables to isomorphism classes is valid here because each isomorphism class contributes at least one labeled table.

Expand the leading term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(n-K_n)^2\log n
&=n^2\log n-2nK_n\log n+K_n^2\log n\\
&=n^2\log n
 -(1+2\delta)n^2\log\log n\\
&\qquad+o(n^2\log\log n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

Subtracting the lower bound (6) from the upper bound (10), we obtain

$$
\log\frac{u_{\ge K_n}(n)}{s(n-1)}
\le
-(2\delta+o(1))n^2\log\log n.
$$

This proves (4). $\square$

### 2.4 What remains unsolved

The theorem eliminates unit groups larger than approximately

$$
\frac{n\log\log n}{2\log n}
$$

by an arbitrarily fixed positive relative margin.

It does **not** establish that monoids with smaller nontrivial unit groups are negligible. In particular, it does not settle the contribution from groups of order $2$.

To prove the original conjecture, the remaining task is to show

$$
\sum_{k=2}^{K_n-1}u_k(n)
=o(s(n-1)),
$$

where $u_k(n)$ counts monoid isomorphism classes with exactly $k$ units.

This is the residual problem left by the unconditional result above.

## 3. Computational check

### 3.1 Independent exhaustive enumeration

I implemented and ran a Python 3 program which:

- Enumerates all semigroup multiplication tables of orders $1,2,3$.
- Enumerates all monoid multiplication tables of orders $1,2,3,4$, fixing the identity at element $0$.
- Checks associativity directly.
- Classifies structures up to relabeling.
- Computes the group-of-units size of each monoid isomorphism class.
- Tests Proposition 1 independently.

For $n=4$, this requires examining $4^9=262144$ candidate monoid tables. The full program is:

```python

from itertools import product, permutations

def associative(T, n, identity=False):
    points = range(1, n) if identity else range(n)
    return all(
        T[T[x*n+y]*n+z] == T[x*n+T[y*n+z]]
        for x in points for y in points for z in points
    )

def signature(T, n, perms):
    best = None
    for p in perms:
        code = tuple(
            p[T[p.index(i)*n + p.index(j)]]
            for i in range(n) for j in range(n)
        )
        if best is None or code < best:
            best = code
    return best

def semigroups(n):
    perms = list(permutations(range(n)))
    types = set()
    labeled = 0

    for entries in product(range(n), repeat=n*n):
        if associative(entries, n):
            labeled += 1
            types.add(signature(entries, n, perms))

    return len(types), labeled

def monoids(n):
    perms = [
        (0,) + p
        for p in permutations(range(1, n))
    ]
    classes = {}
    labeled_fixed = 0

    for interior in product(range(n),
                            repeat=(n-1)**2):
        T = [0] * (n*n)

        for x in range(n):
            T[x] = x
            T[x*n] = x

        for (x, y), z in zip(
            product(range(1, n), repeat=2),
            interior
        ):
            T[x*n+y] = z

        if not associative(T, n, identity=True):
            continue

        labeled_fixed += 1

        units = sum(
            1 for x in range(n)
            if any(
                T[x*n+y] == 0 and T[y*n+x] == 0
                for y in range(n)
            )
        )

        key = signature(T, n, perms)

        if key in classes:
            assert classes[key] == units
        else:
            classes[key] = units

    distribution = {
        k: sum(v == k for v in classes.values())
        for k in sorted(set(classes.values()))
    }

    return len(classes), labeled_fixed*n, distribution

for n in range(1, 5):
    m, labeled, byunits = monoids(n)
    s, _ = semigroups(n-1) if n > 1 else (1, 1)

    print(
        f'n={n}: semigroups(n-1)={s}; '
        f'monoids={m}; excess={m-s}; '
        f'units={byunits}; '
        f'labeled_monoids={labeled}'
    )

    assert byunits.get(1, 0) == s
```

**Exact output from execution:**

```
n=2: semigroups(n-1)=1; monoids=2; excess=1; units={1: 1, 2: 1}; labeled_monoids=4
n=3: semigroups(n-1)=5; monoids=7; excess=2; units={1: 5, 2: 1, 3: 1}; labeled_monoids=33
n=4: semigroups(n-1)=24; monoids=35; excess=11; units={1: 24, 2: 8, 3: 1, 4: 2}; labeled_monoids=624
```

## 4. Verdict

**Verdict — Open, with an unconditional partial result.** As of 12 November 2026, I found no verified proof or counterexample to $\mathrm{A058129}(n)\sim\mathrm{A027851}(n-1)$. Koubek and Rödl (1985) state a related labeled asymptotic, but their argument depends on the Kleitman–Rothschild–Spencer result whose proof remains disputed in the modern literature. The exact identity $m(n)=s(n-1)+u(n)$ reduces the OEIS conjecture to negligibility of monoids with nontrivial unit groups. The additional result proved in Section 2.3 shows unconditionally that unit groups of size at least $(1/2+\delta)n\log\log n/\log n$ contribute a negligible number of isomorphism classes relative to $s(n-1)$, for every fixed $\delta>0$. This counting bound is derived here, but I make no claim that it is new to the literature. Independent exhaustive enumeration through $n=4$ confirms the exact decomposition; OEIS data through $n=9$ support, but do not prove, the conjecture.

**Suggested OEIS comment:**
