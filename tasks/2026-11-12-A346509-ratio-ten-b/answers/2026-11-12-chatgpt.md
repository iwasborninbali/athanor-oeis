# 1. Status — PROVED (March 16, 2026)

**Verdict: The conjecture in OEIS A346509 is already known to be true.** It is neither open nor refuted, and no reduction to an unresolved problem is necessary.

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}{a(n-1)}=10,}
$$

where $a(n)$ counts $n$-digit positive integers expressible as $uv$, with $u,v>1$ and $u\equiv v\equiv1\pmod{10}$.

**Source 2 — Spezia's mathematical article.**

The corollary establishes

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{b_k}{b_{k-1}}=B
$$

for the digit-length counts of any increasing sequence of positive asymptotic density. The relevant base is $B=10$.

**Source 3 — OEIS A346507.**

# 2. Proof — independent elementary verification

Although the conjecture is already resolved, the following argument independently establishes the density assumption needed for Spezia's corollary. It is not claimed as a new result.

**Lemma 1 (proved).** Every positive integer $m\equiv1\pmod{10}$ with at least five prime factors, counted with multiplicity, is a product of two integers greater than 1, each congruent to 1 modulo 10.

**Proof.** Write

$$
m=p_1p_2\cdots p_r,\qquad r=\Omega(m)\ge5.
$$

Since $m\equiv1\pmod{10}$, none of its prime factors is 2 or 5. Every $p_i$ therefore lies in the group

$$
(\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z)^\times=\{1,3,7,9\},
$$

which has four elements.

Consider the five partial products

$$
P_0=1,\quad P_j=p_1p_2\cdots p_j
\pmod{10},\quad 1\le j\le4.
$$

By the pigeonhole principle, some $P_i=P_j$, with $0\le i<j\le4$. As these residues are invertible,

$$
d=p_{i+1}\cdots p_j\equiv1\pmod{10}.
$$

The factor $d>1$ uses at most four primes. Since $r\ge5$, its complementary factor $m/d>1$ also exists and satisfies

$$
m/d\equiv1\pmod{10}.
$$

Thus $m=d(m/d)$ is an admissible factorization. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (proved).** The integers with $\Omega(m)\le4$ have asymptotic density zero.

One can invoke the classical normal-order theorem for the number of prime factors. Here is an elementary justification.

For fixed $y$, define

$$
R_y(m)=\sum_{p\le y}\mathbf1_{p\mid m},
\qquad
\mu_y=\sum_{p\le y}\frac1p.
$$

For uniformly selected integers $m\le X$, as $X\to\infty$, divisibility by distinct primes in this fixed finite set becomes independent. Consequently,

$$
\mathbb E R_y\longrightarrow\mu_y,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(R_y)\longrightarrow
\sum_{p\le y}\frac1p\left(1-\frac1p\right)\le\mu_y.
$$

If $\Omega(m)\le4$, necessarily $R_y(m)\le4$. Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives, whenever $\mu_y>4$,

$$
\limsup_{X\to\infty}
\frac{\#\{m\le X:\Omega(m)\le4\}}X
\le
\frac{\mu_y}{(\mu_y-4)^2}.
$$

Euler's classical divergence theorem for the reciprocals of the primes implies $\mu_y\to\infty$. The right-hand side tends to zero, proving the lemma. $\square$

**Theorem (proved).** Let

$$
C(X)=\#\{m\le X:m=uv,\ u,v>1,\
u\equiv v\equiv1\pmod{10}\}.
$$

Every counted integer is congruent to 1 modulo 10. Conversely, Lemma 1 shows that every integer congruent to 1 modulo 10 with at least five prime factors is counted.

Thus the number of omitted integers in that residue class is bounded by the number with $\Omega(m)\le4$. By Lemma 2,

$$
C(X)=\frac X{10}+o(X).
$$

Now, for $n\ge2$,

$$
a(n)=C(10^n-1)-C(10^{n-1}-1).
$$

It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(n)
&=\frac{10^n-10^{n-1}}{10}+o(10^n)\\
&=9\cdot10^{n-2}+o(10^n).
\end{aligned}
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{a(n)\sim9\cdot10^{n-2}}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{a(n)}{a(n-1)}
\sim
\frac{9\cdot10^{n-2}}{9\cdot10^{n-3}}
=10.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}{a(n-1)}=10.}
\qquad\square
$$

This supplies a checkable verification of both the density claim in A346507 and the resulting limit in A346509.

```python

from math import isqrt

MAX_N = 8
limit = 10**MAX_N

represented = bytearray(limit // 10)

for a in range(11, isqrt(limit - 1) + 1, 10):
    for b in range(a, (limit - 1)//a + 1, 10):
        represented[(a*b)//10] = 1

values = []
for n in range(1, MAX_N + 1):
    lo, hi = 10**(n-1), 10**n
    idx_lo = (lo + 8)//10
    idx_hi = (hi + 8)//10
    values.append(sum(represented[idx_lo:idx_hi]))

print("A346509 n=1..8:", values)
print("ratios n=4..8:", [
    f"{values[n-1]}/{values[n-2]} = "
    f"{values[n-1]/values[n-2]:.9f}"
    for n in range(4, 9)
])

M = 1_000_000
spf = list(range(M+1))
for p in range(2, isqrt(M)+1):
    if spf[p] == p:
        for m in range(p*p, M+1, p):
            if spf[m] == m:
                spf[m] = p

omega = bytearray(M+1)
for m in range(2, M+1):
    omega[m] = omega[m//spf[m]] + 1

tested = sum(
    m%10 == 1 and omega[m] >= 5
    for m in range(1, M+1)
)
failures = [
    m for m in range(1, M+1, 10)
    if omega[m] >= 5 and not represented[m//10]
]

print("Omega lemma, m<=1000000:", tested,
      "tested, failures:", len(failures))
print("OEIS prefix n=1..8 match:",
      values == [
          0, 0, 12, 200, 2660,
          31850, 361985, 3982799
      ])
```
