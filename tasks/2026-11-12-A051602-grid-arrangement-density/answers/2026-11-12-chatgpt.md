# OEIS A051602 — Status and proof attempt for the circular-arrangement density conjecture

**Report date:** 12 November 2026 **Problem:** OEIS A051602, maximal number of squares determined by $n$ planar points.

## 1. Status and literature

**Verdict: Open, as far as the sources checked establish.** I found no published proof, counterexample, or established equivalence to another named open problem. The conjecture is mathematically meaningful, although its wording requires distinguishing unrestricted circular regions from the more restrictive constructions counted by OEIS A348469.

### Primary source and decisive quotation

Peter Munn proposed the conjecture on 30 September 2021. It appears as **Conjecture 5.2** in:

Sascha Kurz, Peter Munn, and Hugo van der Sanden, *Plane point sets with many squares*, preprint dated 13 January 2022, §5, p. 31, hosted by OEIS.

The decisive statement is:

> The asymptotic density of the numbers n such that there is no maximal arrangement formed by all the grid points within a suitably chosen circle, is 0.

The authors explicitly label this a conjecture, not a theorem. The same paper's Conjecture 5.1 asks for a stronger assertion covering every $n$, allowing a suitable subset of boundary points.

### What was previously proved

Sascha Kurz, *Plane point sets with many squares or isosceles right triangles*, arXiv:2112.12716 (23 December 2021), establishes exact values of A051602 through $n=17$ and supplies lower bounds for $18\leq n\leq100$. It also proves that integer-grid configurations suffice for maximizing the number of squares.

The same paper gives the universal bound (Proposition 35)

$$
a(n)\leq\frac{n^2-1}{8},\qquad n\geq1.
$$



The known circular construction has asymptotic coefficient

$$
c_{\mathrm{disk}}=\frac{1-2/\pi}{4}\approx0.0908451.
$$

This is Peter Munn's 2021 construction, presented in his Sequence Fans message of 4 October 2021 and as Theorem 5.3 of the 2022 joint preprint.

The OEIS entry reports circular realizations of the known exact maxima through $n=17$, and no better known unrestricted configurations than the circular records through $n=100$. That is evidence, not a proof for $18\leq n\leq100$.

## 2. Precise formulation and proof attempt

### 2.1. Mathematical formulation

Let $q(S)$ denote the number of distinct, nondegenerate squares whose four vertices belong to a finite set $S\subset\mathbb R^2$.

By Kurz's grid-reduction theorem, we may define

$$
a(n)=\max_{\substack{S\subset\mathbb Z^2\\|S|=n}}q(S).
$$

Define the unrestricted circular maximum

$$
B(n)=\max_{\substack{c\in\mathbb R^2,\ r>0\\
|\mathbb Z^2\cap D(c,r)|=n}}
q\bigl(\mathbb Z^2\cap D(c,r)\bigr),
$$

where $D(c,r)$ is the open Euclidean disk.

Open disks are sufficient: every finite intersection with a closed disk can also be obtained using a slightly larger open disk.

The conjecture becomes

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac{1}{N}
\#\{1\le n\le N:B(n)<a(n)\}=0.
}
$$

This formulation is precise and testable.

**Important distinction:** $B(n)$ is not defined identically to A348469. That sequence restricts the admissible circular regions to a computationally convenient family. The OEIS authors explicitly acknowledge that this restriction has not been shown to be harmless in general.

### 2.2. Lemma: every cardinality is realizable by a disk

**Status: Proved.**

For every nonnegative integer $n$, there exists an open disk containing exactly $n$ integer lattice points, with no lattice points on its boundary.

**Proof.**

Fix the center

$$
c=(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).
$$

For distinct lattice points $p,q\in\mathbb Z^2$,

$$
|p-c|^2-|q-c|^2
=
|p|^2-|q|^2
-2\sqrt2(p_x-q_x)
-2\sqrt3(p_y-q_y).
$$

The numbers $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. Therefore the expression cannot vanish for distinct $p,q$.

Thus all distances from $c$ to lattice points are distinct.

Order the lattice points by increasing distance from $c$. For every $n\ge1$, choose a radius strictly between the distances of the $n$-th and $(n+1)$-st points.

The resulting disk contains exactly $n$ lattice points and has no lattice points on its boundary. The empty configuration is immediate.

$\square$

This establishes that the set defining $B(n)$ is nonempty for every $n$. The conjecture concerns optimal square counts, not the existence of disks with the desired cardinality.

### 2.3. Quantitative partial theorem

**Status: Proved here from the known circular asymptotic; no claim of research novelty.**

For every sufficiently large integer $n$, there is an ordinary open disk containing exactly $n$ lattice points and at least

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1-2/\pi}{4}n^2-O(n^{3/2})
}
$$

squares.

Consequently,

$$
B(n)\ge
\frac{1-2/\pi}{4}n^2-O(n^{3/2}),
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{B(n)}{a(n)}
\ge 2-\frac4\pi
\approx0.726760.
}
$$

Thus circular configurations attain asymptotically at least about 72.68% of the unrestricted optimum.

**Proof.**

Fix the generic center $c=(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. Write

$$
S_R=\mathbb Z^2\cap D(c,R),\qquad
N_R=|S_R|.
$$

The elementary lattice-point estimate for disks gives

$$
N_R=\pi R^2+O(R).
$$

Let $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$, a counterclockwise quarter-turn.

Every nondegenerate square in $S_R$ can be represented by an ordered pair $(p,v)$ satisfying

$$
p,\quad p+v,\quad p+Jv,\quad p+v+Jv\in S_R,
$$

with $v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}$.

Each square has exactly four such representations.

Define the bounded region in $\mathbb R^4$

$$
K=\left\{(u,w):
\begin{array}{l}
|u|<1,\quad |u+w|<1,\\
|u+Jw|<1,\quad |u+w+Jw|<1
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$

Its boundary lies in a finite union of smooth hypersurface pieces. Counting four-dimensional lattice points by comparison with unit cubes gives

$$
\#\bigl((\mathbb Z^4-(c,0))\cap RK\bigr)
=\operatorname{vol}(K)R^4+O(R^3).
$$

Indeed, cubes wholly inside or outside $RK$ contribute exactly their volumes; only cubes intersecting the boundary cause an error. The volume of a bounded-width neighborhood of that boundary is $O(R^3)$.

The degenerate choices $v=0$ contribute only $O(R^2)$ points.

The circular lens calculation of Munn gives

$$
\operatorname{vol}(K)=\pi(\pi-2).
$$

Equivalently, its coefficient follows by integrating the admissible opposite-vertex lens area $(\pi-2)t^2$ over the disk, with the checkerboard parity factor. This is the known calculation in the 2021 Sequence Fans note and the 2022 preprint.

Dividing by four representations per square,

$$
q(S_R)=\frac{\pi(\pi-2)}4R^4+O(R^3).
$$

Now use the preceding lemma to choose $R=R_n$ such that $N_{R_n}=n$. Since

$$
n=\pi R_n^2+O(R_n),
$$

we obtain

$$
R_n^2=\frac n\pi+O(\sqrt n).
$$

Substitution yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(S_{R_n})
&=\frac{\pi(\pi-2)}4R_n^4+O(R_n^3)\\
&=\frac{\pi-2}{4\pi}n^2+O(n^{3/2}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, combining this with Kurz's established bound

$$
a(n)\le\frac{n^2-1}{8}
$$

proves

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{B(n)}{a(n)}
\ge
8\frac{\pi-2}{4\pi}
=
2-\frac4\pi.
$$

$\square$

**Limitation:** This quantitative theorem does not imply that $B(n)=a(n)$ for any sufficiently large $n$. Even asymptotic equality of the leading quadratic coefficients would not establish exact equality of the integer-valued square counts.

### 2.4. Relation to another conjecture

Kurz, Munn, and van der Sanden formulate a continuous variational problem in §5.1 of their 2022 preprint. Their Conjecture 5.6 says that, among unit-area planar domains, a disk maximizes the continuous square-count functional.

This is a natural related open problem, but **not a proved equivalent formulation** of Munn's density conjecture.

The continuous problem concerns leading-order square counts. The density conjecture concerns exact finite optimality for almost every cardinality. Bridging that gap would require substantially stronger discrete information.

I did not find a valid proof or counterexample to the density conjecture.

## 3. Computational verification

**Status: Computed, using exact integer arithmetic.**

I performed two independent finite checks.

First, I searched disks whose centers have the form

$$
c=\left(\frac a{41},\frac b{41}\right),
\qquad 0\le a,b<41.
$$

This gives 1,681 rational centers.

For each center, I sorted lattice points by their squared distances, avoiding floating-point calculations. Whenever the $n$-th and $(n+1)$-st distances differed, the first $n$ points were certified to be the complete lattice-point intersection of an open disk.

The search used coordinates in $[-12,12]^2$ and cardinalities $0\le n\le100$. This finite coordinate range is sufficient: already the 121 lattice points in $[-5,5]^2$ are closer to every tested center than any lattice point outside $[-12,12]^2$.

Second, I exhaustively enumerated all $2^{16}=65{,}536$ subsets of the $4\times4$ integer grid and determined their square counts.

### Reproducible program

The full Python program includes the 101 published circular values, the rational-center search, square enumeration, and the exhaustive $4\times4$ check.

[Download the complete verification program](#file-1)

The key square-counting routine is:

```python

def square_curve(points):
    inside, count, curve = set(), 0, [0]
    for x, y in points:
        for u, v in inside:
            dx, dy = u-x, v-y
            if (dx+dy) % 2:
                continue
            q = ((x+u-dy)//2, (y+v+dx)//2)
            r = ((x+u+dy)//2, (y+v-dx)//2)
            if q in inside and r in inside:
                count += 1
        inside.add((x, y))
        curve.append(count)
    return curve
```

This works because every newly completed square has exactly one vertex added last, and exactly one opposite vertex already present. The other two vertices are determined by rotating the diagonal.

## 4. Verdict

**Open; not proved or refuted.** The strongest established general information remains the bounds and circular constructions of Kurz, Munn, and collaborators. The work here supplies a complete rational-center computational reproduction of the published A348469 values through $n=100$, an explicit disk certificate for every tested cardinality, and a checkable $O(n^{3/2})$ quantitative version of the known circular asymptotic. None of these establishes the claimed density-zero exceptional set, and I make no claim of priority for the quantitative refinement. Confidence is high in the finite calculations and the stated partial theorem; the literature-status assessment is qualified by the inaccessible bibliographic catalogues. **A suitable OEIS comment** would report that Munn's conjecture remains unresolved, that ordinary disks with rational centers on a $41\times41$ mesh reproduce all published A348469 values through $n=100$, and that this finite agreement does not prove unrestricted optimality beyond $n=17$. No OEIS submission or repository edit was made.
