# OEIS A059442 — Fence conjecture: status, disproof, and computational verification

**Report date:** 12 November 2026 **Source:** [Athanor task specification](https://github.com/iwasborninbali/athanor-oeis/blob/main/tasks/2026-11-12-A059442-ramsey-fence/TASK.md)

Refuted — rigorous counterexample

## 1. Status and literature

**Verdict: The Fence conjecture is false as stated.** It is neither ill-posed nor equivalent to an unresolved Ramsey problem. Its failure follows from classical probabilistic lower bounds for diagonal Ramsey numbers and elementary properties of integer partitions.

A fully checkable counterexample is

$$
\boxed{m=n=74.}
$$

In fact, the conjecture fails for all sufficiently large diagonal pairs $m=n$.

### 1.1 The conjecture

Jianglin Luo, in OEIS A059442, 8 January 2024, states:

> Fence conjecture: R(m,n) = m >= 3.

This wording remains in the live OEIS entry.

Write $T(a,b)$ for A008284_T$(a,b)$. OEIS A008284 defines it as the number of partitions of $a$ into exactly $b$ positive parts, equivalently the number of partitions of $a$ whose greatest part equals $b$.

Thus the conjecture is precisely

$$
\boxed{
R(m,n)\le (2m-1)T(n+2m-6,m)+m+1
\quad(n\ge m\ge3).
}
\tag{F}
$$

Here $R(m,n)$ is the ordinary two-colour graph Ramsey number.

### 1.2 Decisive existing theorem

**Known theorem — Paul Erdős (1947).**

P. Erdős, *Some remarks on the theory of graphs*, Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society **53** (1947), 292–294.

The classical first-moment argument establishes

$$
\binom Nk\,2^{1-\binom{k}{2}}<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(k,k)>N.
\tag{E}
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{R(k,k)>2^{k/2}\qquad(k\ge3).}
$$

An accessible statement appears in Shayan Oveis Gharan, *Introduction to the Probabilistic Method*, University of Washington lecture notes, 1 April 2025, Lemma 1.1. The decisive sentence is:

> “In particular, $R(k)>2^{k/2}$ for $k\ge3$.”

The same notes give the binomial-coefficient criterion and its proof.

**Novelty status:** The probabilistic Ramsey theorem and partition identities used below are established mathematics. Their application to this specific OEIS conjecture provides a rigorous refutation, but priority or publication novelty is not claimed.

## 2. Proof and counterexample

### 2.1 Reduction to diagonal Ramsey numbers

Set $n=m=k$ in (F). Define

$$
B_k=(2k-1)T(3k-6,k)+k+1.
$$

The conjecture implies

$$
R(k,k)\le B_k
\qquad(k\ge3).
\tag{1}
$$

A partition of $3k-6$ into exactly $k$ positive parts becomes a partition of $2k-6$ into at most $k$ parts by subtracting one from every part and discarding zeros.

This operation is bijective, so

$$
T(3k-6,k)
=p_{\le k}(2k-6),
\tag{2}
$$

where $p_{\le k}(s)$ counts partitions of $s$ into parts of size at most $k$, equivalently partitions into at most $k$ parts.

In particular, if $p(s)$ denotes the unrestricted partition function,

$$
T(3k-6,k)\le p(2k-6).
\tag{3}
$$

**Status: proved**, directly by the partition bijection.

### 2.2 General disproof: incompatible growth rates

We establish an elementary subexponential upper bound for $p(s)$.

For $0<x<1$, Euler's generating function gives

$$
\sum_{j\ge0}p(j)x^j
=\prod_{r\ge1}(1-x^r)^{-1}.
$$

Set $x=e^{-t}$, with $t>0$. Because all coefficients are nonnegative,

$$
p(s)e^{-ts}
\le \prod_{r\ge1}(1-e^{-tr})^{-1}.
$$

Taking logarithms and expanding the geometric series,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log p(s)
&\le ts+
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{1}{j(e^{tj}-1)}\\
&\le ts+\frac1t\sum_{j\ge1}\frac1{j^2}\\
&=ts+\frac{\pi^2}{6t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second inequality uses $e^u-1\ge u$ for $u>0$.

Choosing $t=s^{-1/2}$, for $s\ge1$, yields

$$
\boxed{
p(s)\le
\exp\left[\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{6}\right)\sqrt{s}\right].
}
\tag{4}
$$

Consequently, (2)–(4) imply

$$
B_k\le
(2k-1)
\exp\left[\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{6}\right)\sqrt{2k-6}\right]
+k+1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\log B_k=O(\sqrt{k}+\log k)=o(k).
\tag{5}
$$

But Erdős's 1947 theorem gives

$$
\log R(k,k)>\frac{k}{2}\log2.
\tag{6}
$$

For all sufficiently large $k$, (5) and (6) force

$$
R(k,k)>B_k,
$$

contradicting (1).

**Theorem — proved.** The Fence conjecture fails for every sufficiently large diagonal pair $(k,k)$. Thus the universally quantified statement (F) is false.

No unproved estimate or numerical computation is required for this asymptotic disproof.

### 2.3 Explicit counterexample at $m=n=74$

We now establish a finite counterexample with exact integer arithmetic.

For $k=74$,

$$
3k-6=216,\qquad 2k-6=142.
$$

The number of partitions of 142 into at most 74 parts is

$$
T(216,74)=18\,420\,969\,414.
$$

Hence the proposed upper bound is

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{74}
&=147T(216,74)+75\\
&=147(18\,420\,969\,414)+75\\
&=\boxed{2\,707\,882\,503\,933}.
\end{aligned}
$$

To refute the conjecture, it suffices to establish

$$
R(74,74)>B_{74}.
$$

Consider independently colouring every edge of the complete graph $K_{B_{74}}$ red or blue with equal probability.

For any fixed set of 74 vertices, the probability that its induced clique is monochromatic is

$$
2^{1-\binom{74}{2}}=2^{-2700}.
$$

There are $\binom{B_{74}}{74}$ such vertex sets. The expected number $X$ of monochromatic 74-cliques is therefore exactly

$$
\mathbb E[X]
=
\frac{2\binom{2\,707\,882\,503\,933}{74}}{2^{2701}}.
$$

An exact integer calculation verifies

$$
\boxed{
2\binom{2\,707\,882\,503\,933}{74}<2^{2701}.
}
\tag{7}
$$

Numerically, for orientation only,

$$
\mathbb E[X]\approx0.51768746122464<1.
$$

Since $X$ is a nonnegative integer-valued random variable, some edge colouring has $X=0$. In other words, there exists a red/blue colouring of $K_{B_{74}}$ with no monochromatic $K_{74}$.

Thus

$$
R(74,74)\ge2\,707\,882\,503\,934,
$$

whereas the Fence conjecture asserts

$$
R(74,74)\le2\,707\,882\,503\,933.
$$

These inequalities are incompatible.

**Counterexample — proved.**

A slightly simpler sufficient form of (7), also verified with exact integers, is

$$
\boxed{
2(2\,707\,882\,503\,933)^{74}
<
74!\,2^{2701}.
}
\tag{8}
$$

Indeed, $\binom N{74}\le N^{74}/74!$, so (8) implies (7).

This proof is nonconstructive: it certifies the existence of a suitable colouring without explicitly listing its edges. That is sufficient to disprove the claimed Ramsey upper bound.

## 3. Computational verification

The following Python 3 program uses exact integers throughout. It verifies the partition count using two independent recurrences, checks the available small exact Ramsey numbers, and applies Erdős's first-moment criterion for diagonal indices $3\le m\le200$.

### 3.1 Reproducible program

```python

from functools import cache
from math import comb, factorial

def T(N, k):
    """Partitions of N into exactly k positive parts."""
    q = N - k
    if q < 0:
        return 0
    dp = [1] + [0]*q
    for part in range(1, min(k, q) + 1):
        for s in range(part, q + 1):
            dp[s] += dp[s-part]
    return dp[q]

@cache
def T2(N, k):
    if N == 0:
        return int(k == 0)
    if k <= 0 or k > N:
        return 0
    return T2(N-1, k-1) + T2(N-k, k)

def B(m, n):
    return (2*m-1)*T(n+2*m-6, m)+m+1

known = {
    (3,n): r
    for n,r in zip(range(3,10),
                   [6,9,14,18,23,28,36])
}
known.update({(4,4):18, (4,5):25})

assert all(r <= B(m,n)
           for (m,n),r in known.items())
assert T(216,74) == T2(216,74)
assert T(282,96) == T2(282,96)

bad = [
    m for m in range(3,201)
    if 2*comb(B(m,m),m) < 2**comb(m,2)
]

print('Exact known R(m,n) tests: 9/9 pass')
print('Known (m,n,R,B):',
      [(m,n,r,B(m,n))
       for (m,n),r in sorted(known.items())])
print('Diagonal first-moment tests: m = 3..200')
print('Successful contradiction certificates: 74..200 (127 consecutive):',
      bad == list(range(74,201)))

for m in (73,74):
    b = B(m,m)
    a = 2*comb(b,m)
    d = 2**comb(m,2)
    print(f'm={m}: T={T(3*m-6,m)}, B={b}, '
          f'first-moment={a<d}, '
          f'floor(10^12 E)={10**12*a//d}')

b = B(74,74)
x = 2*b**74
y = factorial(74)*2**comb(74,2)
print('Sufficient integer inequality:', x<y)
print('Left/right bit lengths:',
      x.bit_length(), y.bit_length())
print('Independent recurrence agrees at m=74 and m=96: True')
print('m=96: B=', B(96,96),
      ', 2^48=', 2**48, sep='')
```

### 3.3 Interpretation

**Computed:** All nine tested exact Ramsey values from A059442 satisfy the proposed inequality. These cases are consistent with the original conjecture.

**Computed and mathematically certified:** The first-moment criterion produces counterexamples for every diagonal index from 74 through 200, inclusive. The lack of a first-moment certificate for $3\le m\le73$ does not establish the conjecture for those indices.

**Independent check:** There is also a simpler finite counterexample using only the standard published Erdős lower bound:

$$
\begin{aligned}

&=281\,474\,976\,710\,656\\
&>223\,665\,517\,913\,524\\

\end{aligned}

This provides a second rigorous disproof without the large binomial-coefficient calculation.

**Verdict — refuted, with high mathematical confidence.** The Fence conjecture attributed to Jianglin Luo (OEIS A059442, 8 January 2024) is false. The proof combines known partition identities with Erdős's classical 1947 probabilistic lower bound; the specific finite counterexample and its numerical verification are provided here, without claiming literature priority. Suggested OEIS comment: *“The Fence conjecture is false. At $m=n=74$, A008284_T(216,74) = 18420969414, so the proposed upper bound for $R(74,74)$ is 2707882503933.
