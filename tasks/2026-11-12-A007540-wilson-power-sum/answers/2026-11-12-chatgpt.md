# OEIS A007540 — Wilson Primes and the Power-Sum Conjecture

**Task date:** November 12, 2026 **Object:** OEIS A007540 **Result:** PROVED — known theorem, not a new result **Confidence:** Certain (elementary proof and independent computational verification)

## 1. Status and literature review

### 1.1 Statement

The conjecture by Thomas Ordowski and Giovanni Resta, dated July 25, 2018, in [OEIS A007540](https://oeis.org/A007540) asserts that the Wilson primes are exactly the odd primes $p$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}\equiv p-1\pmod{p^2}.}
$$

A Wilson prime is a prime $p$ for which

$$
(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}.
$$

The known terms are $5,13,563$.

### 1.2 Decisive finding

**The conjecture is an immediate consequence of Lerch's congruence, proved in 1905:**

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}
\equiv (p-1)!+p\pmod{p^2}
}
$$

for every odd prime $p$.

Consequently,

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}\equiv p-1\pmod{p^2}
\iff
(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}.
$$

This proves the conjecture in both directions.

The decisive source is OEIS [A197632 — Lerch primes](https://oeis.org/A197632), which states:

> “The congruence holds mod p^2 for any odd prime p; see Lerch (1905).”

The immediately preceding sentence in that entry specifies the congruence as the power sum minus the factorial being congruent to $p$.

## 2. Proof

**Theorem (Lerch, 1905; standard consequence).** For every odd prime $p$,

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}\equiv p-1\pmod{p^2}
$$

if and only if $p$ is a Wilson prime.

### Proof

Define the Fermat quotient

$$
q_p(a)=\frac{a^{p-1}-1}{p},
\qquad 1\le a\le p-1,
$$

and the Wilson quotient

$$
w_p=\frac{(p-1)!+1}{p}.
$$

Both are integers by Fermat's little theorem and Wilson's theorem, respectively.

By definition,

$$
a^{p-1}=1+pq_p(a).
$$

Multiplying these identities for $a=1,\ldots,p-1$ gives

$$
((p-1)!)^{p-1}
=\prod_{a=1}^{p-1}(1+pq_p(a)).
$$

Expanding the product modulo $p^2$, all terms involving at least two Fermat quotients vanish. Therefore,

$$
((p-1)!)^{p-1}
\equiv1+p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}q_p(a)
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{1}
$$

Now write

$$
(p-1)!=pw_p-1.
$$

Since $p-1$ is even, the binomial theorem yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
((p-1)!)^{p-1}
&=(pw_p-1)^{p-1}\\
&\equiv1-(p-1)pw_p\pmod{p^2}\\
&\equiv1+pw_p\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Comparing (1) and (2), then dividing by $p$, gives

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}q_p(a)\equiv w_p\pmod p.
}
\tag{3}
$$

This is Lerch's congruence.

Substituting the Fermat-quotient definition into (3),

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(a^{p-1}-1)
\equiv pw_p\pmod{p^2}.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}
&\equiv(p-1)+pw_p\\
&=(p-1)+(p-1)!+1\\
&=(p-1)!+p\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

We have proved the stronger identity

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}-(p-1)!-p
\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{p-1}
\equiv p-1\pmod{p^2}\\
&\quad\iff (p-1)!+p\equiv p-1\pmod{p^2}\\
&\quad\iff (p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last condition is precisely the definition of a Wilson prime.

$$
\boxed{\text{Conjecture proved.}\quad\square}
$$

**Scope.** The restriction to odd primes is necessary. For $p=2$, the proposed power-sum condition holds ($1\equiv1\pmod4$), but the Wilson-prime condition fails ($1!\not\equiv-1\pmod4$).

**Novelty status:** None claimed. Both the stronger congruence and the proof technique are established mathematical material.

## 3. Computational verification

**Method:** Python 3, exact modular arithmetic, deterministic sieve of Eratosthenes.

**Range:** Every odd prime $3\le p\le10000$, totaling 1,228 primes.

The program checks four properties: the Wilson-prime condition, the proposed power-sum condition, the stronger Lerch identity, and the Fermat-quotient/Wilson-quotient identity.

```python

def odd_primes_upto(n):
    sieve = bytearray([1]) * (n + 1)
    sieve[:2] = b'\x00\x00'
    for d in range(2, int(n**0.5) + 1):
        if sieve[d]:
            sieve[d*d:n+1:d] = bytes(
                len(sieve[d*d:n+1:d])
            )
    return [
        p for p in range(3, n+1, 2)
        if sieve[p]
    ]

wilson, power_sum = [], []
lerch_bad, quotient_bad = [], []
ps = odd_primes_upto(10000)

for p in ps:
    m = p*p
    f = 1
    for a in range(1, p):
        f = f*a % m

    powers = [
        pow(a, p-1, m)
        for a in range(1, p)
    ]
    s = sum(powers) % m
    w = (f+1)//p
    qsum = sum((v-1)//p for v in powers) % p

    if f == m-1:
        wilson.append(p)
    if s == p-1:
        power_sum.append(p)
    if (s-f-p) % m:
        lerch_bad.append(p)
    if qsum != w % p:
        quotient_bad.append(p)

print("range = odd primes 3..10000")
print("odd_primes_tested =", len(ps))
print("wilson_hits =", wilson)
print("power_sum_hits =", power_sum)
print("lerch_identity_failures =", lerch_bad)
print("fermat_wilson_quotient_failures =", quotient_bad)
print("p2: wilson = False, power_sum = True")
```
