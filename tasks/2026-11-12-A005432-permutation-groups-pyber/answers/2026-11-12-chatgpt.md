# OEIS A005432 — Pyber's conjecture on subgroups of symmetric groups

**Research report — 12 November 2026**

## 1. Status: PROVED (2025)

**Verdict: Pyber's 1993 conjecture is solved.** The proof was obtained by Colva M. Roney-Dougal and Gareth Tracey in 2025, approximately 31 years after the original conjecture.

### Decisive reference

Colva M. Roney-Dougal and Gareth Tracey, *Subgroups of symmetric groups: enumeration and asymptotic properties*, arXiv:2503.05416 [math.GR], submitted 7 March 2025.

Primary source: [arXiv:2503.05416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05416)

The authors state in the abstract:

> In this paper, we prove that the symmetric group $S_n$ has $2^{n^2/16+o(n^2)}$ subgroups, settling a conjecture of Pyber from 1993.

This is exactly the conjecture described in OEIS A005432, not merely a related result.

### Precise statement

Define

$$
a(n)=|\{H:H\leq S_n\}|,
$$

where different conjugate subgroups are counted separately.

Pyber conjectured that

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 a(n)}{n^2}=\frac1{16}.}
$$

**Status: PROVED.**

The paper establishes the stronger quantitative estimate

$$
\boxed{
2^{n^2/16+\alpha n\log n}
\leq a(n)\leq
2^{n^2/16+\beta n^{3/2}},
}
$$

for suitable absolute constants $\alpha>0$ and $\beta$, and all integers $n>1$. Logarithms in this estimate are base 2. This is Theorem 1 of Roney-Dougal and Tracey.

Consequently,

$$
\log_2 a(n)=\frac{n^2}{16}+O(n^{3/2}),
$$

which implies the required limit.

The OEIS entry still presents Pyber's prediction as a conjecture, despite the available proof.

## 2. Proof or counterexample: existing theorem applies directly

**Status: PROVED by Roney-Dougal–Tracey (2025); no new proof claimed here.**

There is no counterexample to construct and no outstanding conjecture to resolve: Theorem 1 establishes the desired asymptotic.

Writing the theorem's estimate in logarithmic form gives

$$
\frac1{16}+\alpha\frac{\log n}{n}
\leq \frac{\log_2 a(n)}{n^2}
\leq \frac1{16}+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt n}.
$$

Both error terms tend to zero, so the squeeze theorem yields

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 a(n)}{n^2}
=\frac1{16}.
$$

This deduction is elementary; the substantive group-theoretic proof is the cited authors' work.

The original conjecture and its resolution must therefore be credited to Pyber (1993) and Roney-Dougal–Tracey (2025), respectively.

## 3. Computational check

**Status: COMPUTED**, with exact arithmetic.

The computation has two independent components:

1. Enumerate every subgroup of $S_n$, without identifying conjugates, for $0\leq n\leq5$, and compare the counts with OEIS.
2. Verify a classical lower-bound construction against all 19 published OEIS terms, $0\leq n\leq18$.

### Lower-bound lemma

For any $n$, put $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. The $m$ disjoint transpositions

$$
(1\,2),(3\,4),\ldots,(2m-1\,\,2m)
$$

generate an elementary abelian group

$$
E\cong(C_2)^m\leq S_n.
$$

Every subgroup of $E$ is an $\mathbb F_2$-linear subspace. Thus, setting $k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$,

$$
a(n)\geq B(n):=
{m\brack k}_2
=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
\frac{2^{m-j}-1}{2^{k-j}-1}.
$$

Here ${m\brack k}_2$ is a Gaussian binomial coefficient.

Each factor is at least $2^{m-k}$, so

$$
a(n)\geq B(n)\geq 2^{k(m-k)}
=2^{n^2/16-O(n)}.
$$

**Status: PROVED (elementary, known construction).** This recovers Pyber's conjectured leading exponent as a lower bound, but it does not establish the upper bound. The latter is the substantive achievement of Roney-Dougal and Tracey.

### Reproducible Python 3 program

```python
from itertools import permutations
from collections import deque

# Published OEIS A005432 terms, n = 0..18.
A = [
    1, 1, 2, 6, 30, 156, 1455, 11300,
    151221, 1694723, 29594446, 404126228,
    10594925360, 175238308453,
    5651774693595, 117053117995400,
    5320744503742316, 125889331236297288,
    7598016157515302757
]

def count_subgroups(n):
    ps = list(permutations(range(n)))
    index = {p: i for i, p in enumerate(ps)}

    # Multiplication table of S_n.
    times = [
        [index[tuple(p[q[j]] for j in range(n))]
         for q in ps]
        for p in ps
    ]

    e = index[tuple(range(n))]
    start = frozenset([e])

    # Store generators for each distinct subgroup.
    found = {start: ()}
    pending = deque([start])

    while pending:
        H = pending.popleft()
        for g in range(len(ps)):
            if g in H:
                continue

            generators = found[H] + (g,)
            elements, queue = {e}, [e]

            # Enumerate the generated subgroup.
            for x in queue:
                for y in generators:
                    z = times[x][y]
                    if z not in elements:
                        elements.add(z)
                        queue.append(z)

            K = frozenset(elements)
            if K not in found:
                found[K] = generators
                pending.append(K)

    return len(found)

def gaussian_2(m, k):
    a = b = 1
    for j in range(k):
        a *= 2**(m-j) - 1
        b *= 2**(k-j) - 1
    assert a % b == 0
    return a // b

small = [count_subgroups(n) for n in range(6)]
B = [
    gaussian_2(n//2, n//4)
    for n in range(len(A))
]

print('Enumerated a(0..5):', small)
print('Matches OEIS (0..5):', small == A[:6])
print('Subspace lower bounds B(0..18):', B)
print('a(n) >= B(n), 0..18:',
      all(a >= b for a, b in zip(A, B)))
```
