# OEIS A092506 — Status and proof of the totient equation conjecture

**Date:** 12 November 2026 **Problem:** Benoit Cloitre's 2018 conjecture in OEIS A092506 **Result:** Complete elementary proof of the sequence characterization; the finiteness of Fermat primes remains open.

## 1. Status

**Conclusion: The conjecture is true when interpreted as a characterization of A092506, and is proved below.** A stronger interpretation—that the six currently listed numbers are the only solutions—is an open problem.

Benoit Cloitre wrote in [OEIS A092506](https://oeis.org/A092506) on 2 March 2018:

> Conjecture: these are the only solutions to the equation A000010(x)+A000010(x-1)=floor((3x-2)/2).

The entry defines A092506 as the prime numbers of the form $2^n+1$, including $n=0$.

The mathematically precise claim is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\varphi(x)+\varphi(x-1)
=\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
x=2^n+1\text{ is prime for some }n\geq0,
\end{aligned}}
$$

for integers $x\geq2$, where $\varphi$ denotes Euler's totient function.

### Existing results and prior art

The most relevant earlier result appears in [OEIS A171271](https://oeis.org/A171271), originally authored by Farideh Firoozbakht on 23 February 2010. Its currently unsigned theorem comment states:

> Theorem: A prime p is in the sequence iff p is a Fermat prime.

Here A171271 consists of integers $k$ satisfying $\varphi(k)=2\varphi(k-1)$. Jaroslav Krizek also explicitly records this characterization in OEIS A019434 on 17 March 2016.

Thus the underlying relationship between Fermat primes and consecutive totients is **already known**. The exact floor equation in A092506 requires an additional argument excluding composite solutions.

A separate issue is whether more Fermat primes exist. Dmitry Kamenetsky wrote in [OEIS A019434](https://oeis.org/A019434) on 28 September 2008:

> It is conjectured that there are only 5 terms.

The [OEIS Wiki's Fermat primes article](https://oeis.org/wiki/Fermat_primes) and Eric W. Weisstein's [Fermat Prime, MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/FermatPrime.html) likewise identify five known Fermat primes.

The other directly linked sources were also examined: Rafay A. Ashary's *A Property of A092506* concerns primitive roots, while Barry Brent's 2022 paper, *On the constant terms of certain meromorphic modular forms for Hecke groups* (arXiv:2212.12515), concerns modular forms. Neither supplies a proof of the exact totient equation.

Targeted web searches covering OEIS, its wiki, arXiv, and publicly indexed mathematical literature did not locate a prior proof explicitly addressing Cloitre's exact equation. I did not conduct authenticated searches inside MathSciNet or zbMATH, so this is not an exhaustive bibliographic claim.

## 2. Complete proof

**Theorem.** For every integer $x\geq2$,

$$
\varphi(x)+\varphi(x-1)
=\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor
$$

if and only if $x$ is a prime of the form $2^n+1$, $n\geq0$.

### Lemma 1: Maximum totient value

For every integer $m\geq2$,

$$
\varphi(m)\leq m-1,
$$

with equality if and only if $m$ is prime.

*Proof.* Among $1,\ldots,m$, only $1,\ldots,m-1$ can be coprime to $m$. If $m$ is prime, all of them are coprime. If $m$ is composite, it possesses a proper divisor $d$ with $1<d<m$, which is not coprime to $m$. Hence the inequality is strict. $\square$

### Lemma 2: Maximum totient value for even integers

For every even integer $m\geq2$,

$$
\varphi(m)\leq\frac m2,
$$

with equality if and only if $m$ is a positive power of $2$.

*Proof.* Every integer coprime to even $m$ must be odd. There are exactly $m/2$ odd integers in $1,\ldots,m$.

If $m=2^k$, all these odd integers are coprime to $m$, so $\varphi(m)=m/2$.

Conversely, if $m$ has an odd prime divisor $q$, then $q$ itself is among those $m/2$ odd integers, but $\gcd(q,m)>1$. Therefore $\varphi(m)<m/2$. $\square$

### Case 1: $x=2$

Direct calculation gives

$$
\varphi(2)+\varphi(1)=1+1=2
=\left\lfloor\frac{6-2}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$

Thus $x=2=2^0+1$ is a solution.

### Case 2: Even $x\geq4$

By Lemma 2,

$$
\varphi(x)\leq\frac x2.
$$

Since $x-1\geq3$, Lemma 1 gives

$$
\varphi(x-1)\leq x-2.
$$

Adding,

$$
\varphi(x)+\varphi(x-1)
\leq\frac{3x}{2}-2.
$$

But for even $x$,

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor
=\frac{3x}{2}-1.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\varphi(x)+\varphi(x-1)
<
\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$

**No even integer $x\geq4$ is a solution.**

### Case 3: Odd $x\geq3$

Set $m=x-1$. Then $m$ is even and

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor
=\frac{3m}{2}.
$$

Applying Lemmas 1 and 2,

$$
\varphi(x)+\varphi(m)
\leq m+\frac m2
=\frac{3m}{2}.
$$

Equality in this sum holds precisely when both inequalities are equalities:

$$
\begin{cases}
\varphi(x)=x-1,\\
\varphi(x-1)=\dfrac{x-1}{2}.
\end{cases}
$$

By the lemmas, these conditions are equivalent to

$$
\begin{cases}
x\text{ is prime},\\
x-1=2^n\quad\text{for some }n\geq1.
\end{cases}
$$

Therefore

$$
x=2^n+1\text{ is prime}.
$$

Conversely, every such prime satisfies both equality conditions and hence the original equation.

Combining the three cases proves

$$
\boxed{
\left\{x\geq2:
\varphi(x)+\varphi(x-1)
=\left\lfloor\frac{3x-2}{2}\right\rfloor
\right\}
=
\left\{2^n+1:n\geq0,\ 2^n+1\text{ prime}\right\}.
}
$$

$\square$

### Connection to Fermat primes

For $n\geq1$, if $n$ is not a power of $2$, it can be written $n=ab$ with odd $b>1$. Then

$$
2^n+1=(2^a)^b+1
$$

is divisible by $2^a+1$ and is therefore composite.

Thus every prime $2^n+1$ with $n\geq1$ has $n=2^k$.

The solution set is consequently

$$
\boxed{\{2\}\ \cup\
\{\,2^{2^k}+1:k\geq0,\ 2^{2^k}+1\text{ prime}\,\}.}
$$

This is exactly A092506. The proof does not assume that only five Fermat primes exist.

## 3. Computational verification

The following Python 3 program computes the totient values by a sieve, constructs an independent primality sieve, and checks the equation and both lemmas for every applicable integer up to $10^6$.

```python

from math import isqrt

N = 1_000_000

# Euler's totient sieve
phi = list(range(N + 1))
for p in range(2, N + 1):
    if phi[p] == p:
        for m in range(p, N + 1, p):
            phi[m] -= phi[m] // p
phi[1] = 1

# Independent primality sieve
prime = bytearray([1]) * (N + 1)
prime[0:2] = b"\x00\x00"
for p in range(2, isqrt(N) + 1):
    if prime[p]:
        prime[p*p::p] = b"\x00" * (
            ((N - p*p) // p) + 1
        )

# Direct equation solutions
solutions = [
    x for x in range(2, N + 1)
    if phi[x] + phi[x-1] == (3*x - 2) // 2
]

# Independently predicted solutions
predicted = [
    x for x in range(2, N + 1)
    if prime[x] and (
        x == 2 or ((x-1) & (x-2)) == 0
    )
]

# Lemma 1
prime_lemma = all(
    (phi[n] == n-1) == bool(prime[n])
    for n in range(2, N + 1)
)

# Lemma 2
even_lemma = all(
    2*phi[n] <= n
    and ((2*phi[n] == n) == ((n & (n-1)) == 0))
    for n in range(2, N + 1, 2)
)

print(f"range: 2..{N}")
print(f"equation solutions: {solutions}")
print(f"predicted solutions: {predicted}")
print(f"all solution sets agree: {solutions == predicted}")
print(f"prime lemma: {prime_lemma}")
print(f"even lemma: {even_lemma}")
```

```
all solution sets agree: True

```

**Computed:** Exactly six solutions occur in the integer range $2\leq x\leq1{,}000{,}000$. Both supporting lemmas pass every applicable test in this range, and the independently computed predicted set agrees with the direct solution set.

This is finite verification, not the basis of the proof. The theorem in Section 2 establishes the characterization without an upper bound.

## 4. Verdict

**PROVED, with an important qualification.** Benoit Cloitre's 2 March 2018 conjecture in OEIS A092506 is true when “these” denotes the sequence as defined: all primes of the form $2^n+1$. The complete equivalence follows from two elementary, sharp upper bounds for Euler's totient function, with $x=2$ handled separately. The prime-case characterization is already documented in OEIS A171271 and A019434, so no mathematical priority or novelty is claimed for that relationship; I found no earlier explicit proof of the exact floor equation in the sources searched. The stronger assertion that the six displayed terms are the complete finite solution set remains **OPEN**, because it is equivalent to the conjecture that no further Fermat primes exist. The computation through $10^6$ confirms, but does not establish, that stronger assertion. A possible OEIS update would replace the ambiguous conjecture with the proven equivalence, cite the earlier OEIS characterization, and retain the distinction between known terms and all possible terms.
