# OEIS A030052 — Status, Proof, Computation, and Verdict

**Task date:** November 12, 2026 **Source:** [Athanor research task](https://github.com/iwasborninbali/athanor-oeis/blob/main/tasks/2026-11-12-A030052-power-sum-distinct/TASK.md)

## 1. Status and literature

### Main finding: the conjecture is false as literally stated

The formula in [OEIS A030052](https://oeis.org/A030052), attributed to M. F. Hasler (May 25, 2020), is:

$$
\boxed{a(n)\geq A332101(n)=A078607(n)+2.}
$$

Its two components have different mathematical statuses:

- **Proved, elementary:** $a(n)\geq A332101(n)$ for every $n\geq1$.
- **Refuted as stated:** $A332101(n)=A078607(n)+2$ fails at $n=1$.

Indeed, the three sequences give

$$
\begin{aligned}
A030052(1)&=3,\\
A332101(1)&=3,\\
A078607(1)&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{3\geq3\neq4.}
$$

The stronger claimed inequality $a(1)\geq A078607(1)+2$ is also false: $3\not\geq4$.

This is an **exact counterexample**, not a numerical approximation. It follows from the definitions and the published sequence values.

The meaningful corrected question is whether the equality, or at least the stronger lower bound, holds for every $n\geq2$.

### What the existing literature establishes

**M. F. Hasler (2020), OEIS A332101.** The related entry gives the more carefully qualified conjecture:

> a(n) = A078607(n) + 2 for almost all n > 1.

It also identifies $n=777451915729368$ as a possible exception. This is an important distinction: an assertion about *almost all* indices is not disproved by one exceptional index.

**Touch Sungkawichai (June 25, 2026), OEIS A332101.** Sungkawichai reports verification of the alternative approximation

$$
A332101(n)=\operatorname{round}(n/\log 2)+2
$$

through $n=50000$, plus four selected larger indices. The entry observes that this approximation and Hasler's equality disagree at $n=777451915729368$, without determining which equality holds there.

**R. Sprague (1948),** *Über Zerlegungen in n-te Potenzen mit lauter verschiedenen Grundzahlen*, *Mathematische Zeitschrift* **51**, 466–468. Sprague proved the completeness of the sequence of positive $n$-th powers under distinct summation. This is an existence theorem, not a proof of Hasler's proposed equality.

**Doyon Kim (2017),** *On the Largest Integer that is not a Sum of Distinct Positive nth Powers*, *Journal of Integer Sequences* **20**, Article 17.7.5; also arXiv:1610.02439. Kim proves an explicit upper bound for the threshold beyond which integers have distinct-power representations. This does not establish the proposed formula for A332101.

**Brennan Benfield and Oliver Lippard (2024; revised March 31, 2025),** *Integers that are not the Sum of Positive Powers*, arXiv:2404.08193v2. This examines sums of prescribed numbers of positive powers, generally allowing repeated summands. Its results do not settle the distinct-power lower-bound equality here.

**Al Zimmermann (2020),** *Sum of Powers: Final Report*, Al Zimmermann's Programming Contests, July 18, 2020. The report provides explicit distinct-power representations for exponents 16–30, but not a general proof of the bound. Report

## 2. Proof and a second counterexample

### 2.1 Precise formulation

For each integer $n\geq1$, write

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n&=A030052(n),\\
b_n&=A332101(n),\\
c_n&=A078607(n).
\end{aligned}
$$

Their definitions are:

$$
a_n=\min\left\{m\geq2:
m^n=\sum_{j\in S}j^n
\text{ for some }S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\right\},
$$

$$
b_n=\min\left\{m\geq1:
m^n\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^n\right\},
$$

and

$$
c_n=\min\{k\geq1:2k^n>(k+1)^n\}.
$$

### 2.2 Theorem 1: an unconditional lower bound

**Theorem (proved).** For every integer $n\geq1$,

$$
\boxed{a_n\geq b_n\geq c_n+1.}
$$

*Proof.*

Suppose $m=a_n$. By definition, there exists a subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m-1\}$ with

$$
m^n=\sum_{j\in S}j^n
\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^n.
$$

Thus $m$ satisfies the condition defining $b_n$, giving $a_n\geq b_n$.

For the second inequality, define

$$
E_n(m)=m^n-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^n.
$$

Direct subtraction yields the exact identity

$$
\boxed{E_n(m+1)-E_n(m)=(m+1)^n-2m^n.}
$$

By the definition of $c_n$,

$$
2m^n\leq(m+1)^n
\qquad(1\leq m<c_n).
$$

Therefore $E_n(m)$ is nondecreasing from $m=1$ through $m=c_n$. Since $E_n(1)=1$,

$$
E_n(m)\geq1>0
\qquad(1\leq m\leq c_n).
$$

The first index with $E_n(m)\leq0$ must consequently be at least $c_n+1$. Hence

$$
b_n\geq c_n+1.
$$

Together these give the theorem. $\square$

The first inequality was already noted in OEIS A332101 and is **not a new result**. The second is an immediate consequence of the difference identity, offered as an independently checkable argument, without a novelty claim.

### 2.3 Theorem 2: an exact counterexample with $n>1$

The indexing counterexample $n=1$ might be dismissed by correcting the conjecture's domain to $n\geq2$.

However, the equality still fails for a much larger exponent.

**Theorem (certified by integer arithmetic).** Let

$$
\boxed{n_0=777451915729368.}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
A078607(n_0)&=1121626023352384,\\
A332101(n_0)&=1121626023352385.
\end{aligned}}
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{A332101(n_0)=A078607(n_0)+1}
$$

and not $A078607(n_0)+2$.

This determines which of the two competing formulas noted in OEIS A332101 is correct at that index: the $A078607+2$ formula fails.

#### Proof certificate

Put

$$
k=1121626023352384,\qquad N=k+1.
$$

First, the integer-only calculation in Section 3 certifies

$$
2\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{n_0}<1
$$

and

$$
2\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{n_0}>1.
$$

Because $x/(x+1)$ is strictly increasing for positive $x$, these inequalities establish

$$
c_{n_0}=k.
$$

By Theorem 1, $b_{n_0}\geq k+1$.

Next, the same calculation rigorously establishes

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{h=1}^{120}
\left(\frac{N-h}{N}\right)^{n_0}>1.
}
$$

Every term is positive. Because $120<k$,

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{N}\right)^{n_0}
\geq
\sum_{h=1}^{120}
\left(\frac{N-h}{N}\right)^{n_0}
>1.
$$

Multiplying by $N^{n_0}$ gives

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}j^{n_0}>N^{n_0},
$$

so

$$
E_{n_0}(N)<0.
$$

Therefore $b_{n_0}\leq N=k+1$.

Combined with the previously proved lower bound,

$$
\boxed{b_{n_0}=k+1=1121626023352385.}
$$

This proves Theorem 2, conditional only on the explicitly reproducible integer comparisons below. $\square$

**Scope:** This refutes the universal equality $b_n=c_n+2$ even for $n\geq2$. It does **not** refute $a_n\geq b_n$, nor establish whether $a_{n_0}\geq c_{n_0}+2$. The minimum distinct-power representation at this enormous exponent was not computed.

## 3. Computational verification

The following self-contained Python 3 program performs two independent types of checks: direct exact-integer sequence evaluation for exponents 1–500, and a rigorous fixed-point interval certificate at $n_0$.

For the large exponent, it represents nonnegative rational quantities by enclosing integers $L,U$, where

$$
\frac LQ\leq x\leq\frac UQ,\qquad Q=10^{60}.
$$

Multiplication with downward rounding on lower bounds and upward rounding on upper bounds preserves the enclosure. Binary exponentiation preserves it by induction. Thus the final inequalities do not depend on floating-point accuracy.

```python

# Python 3: exact-integer verification

A = [3,5,6,15,12,25,40,84,47,63,
     68,81,102,95,104,162,123]

def B(n):  # A332101(n)
    m, s = 1, 0
    while m**n > s:
        s += m**n
        m += 1
    return m

def C(n):  # A078607(n)
    x = 1
    while 2*x**n <= (x+1)**n:
        x += 1
    return x

pairs = [(n, B(n), C(n)) for n in range(1,501)]
print('range: 1..500')
print('B(n) != C(n)+2:',
      [(n,b,c) for n,b,c in pairs if b != c+2])
print('published A030052 prefix below B:',
      [n for n in range(1,18) if A[n-1] < pairs[n-1][1]])
print('B(1..17):', [x[1] for x in pairs[:17]])

# Rigorous large-index certificate
n = 777451915729368
k = 1121626023352384
Q = 10**60
H = 120

def pow_bounds(p, q, e):
    # Exact enclosure:
    # L/Q <= (p/q)**e <= U/Q
    lo = p*Q//q
    hi = (p*Q+q-1)//q
    L = U = Q
    while e:
        if e & 1:
            L = L*lo//Q
            U = (U*hi + Q-1)//Q
        e //= 2
        if e:
            lo = lo*lo//Q
            hi = (hi*hi+Q-1)//Q
    return L, U

before = pow_bounds(k-1, k, n)
after  = pow_bounds(k, k+1, n)

S = sum(pow_bounds(k+1-h, k+1, n)[0]
        for h in range(1,H+1))

x = 2*before[1]-Q
y = 2*after[0]-Q
z = S-Q

assert x < 0 and y > 0 and z > 0

print('n, k, Q exponent, H:', n, k, 60, H)
print('2*previous upper - 1 (scaled):', x)
print('2*current lower - 1 (scaled):', y)
print('partial-sum lower - 1 (scaled):', z)
print('certified B(n), C(n), C(n)+2:',
      k+1, k, k+2)
```

## 4. Verdict

**Verdict: REFUTED as a universal equality, while the underlying A030052 lower bound is PROVED and already known.** The literal chain in A030052 fails at $n=1$. More significantly, an independently reproducible integer-only certificate establishes $A332101(777451915729368)=1121626023352385=A078607(777451915729368)+1$, refuting the proposed equality even after restricting to $n\geq2$. This resolves the specific possible exception already identified in OEIS A332101; the index itself and its significance were previously known, so no claim of discovering a new exceptional index is made. The exact evaluation of A332101 at that index was not found in the checked sources and is the potentially new contribution of this report, subject to independent verification and priority review. The weaker *almost-all* statement remains unresolved by these counterexamples, and the possibility of a stronger direct lower bound on A030052 remains a separate question. No OEIS submission or repository modification was made.

**Suggested OEIS comment**, subject to independent verification:

> The equality A332101(n) = A078607(n) + 2 is not valid for all n >= 2. At n = 777451915729368, exact integer interval arithmetic gives A078607(n) = 1121626023352384 and A332101(n) = 1121626023352385. The latter follows from a certified inequality involving only the largest 120 preceding n-th powers. The bound A030052(n) >= A332101(n) remains valid for every n >= 1 by definition. The previously noted exceptional index is therefore a counterexample to the universal equality, but not to the lower bound itself.
