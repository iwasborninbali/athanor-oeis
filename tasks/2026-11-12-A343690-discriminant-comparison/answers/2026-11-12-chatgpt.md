# OEIS A343690 — Discriminant comparison

## 1. Status and precise statement

**Status: Open in general, as far as the sources examined establish.** The original conjecture appears in Jianing Song's 2021 entry [OEIS A343690](https://oeis.org/A343690), and is repeated in [OEIS A343772](https://oeis.org/A343772). Both entries still label it a conjecture.

For a number field $K$, write $d_K$ for its discriminant and define

$$
P_n=\min_{\substack{[K:\mathbb Q]=n\\d_K>0}}d_K,
\qquad
N_n=\min_{\substack{[K:\mathbb Q]=n\\d_K<0}}|d_K|.
$$

Thus $P_n=\mathrm{A343690}(n)$ and $N_n=\mathrm{A343772}(n)$.

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P_n&<N_n &&(n\equiv0,1\pmod4),\\
P_n&>N_n &&(n\equiv2,3\pmod4).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The appropriate domain is **all integers $n\ge2$**. At $n=1$, every degree-one number field is $\mathbb Q$, whose discriminant is $+1$; therefore $N_1$ does not exist. The original statement needs this minor domain restriction.

An important distinction is that the conjecture compares *global minima over discriminant signs*, not minima over individual field signatures.

For a field of signature $(r_1,r_2)$, where $r_1+2r_2=n$, the standard discriminant-sign identity gives

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(d_K)=(-1)^{r_2}.
$$

Consequently, the conjecture predicts that the smallest absolute discriminant has the same sign as a field with the **maximum possible number of complex places**, namely $r_2=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

This is an equivalence concerning the sign, not an assertion that the minimizing field must actually have that signature. That stronger assertion would require a separate proof.

### Relevant published results

The most useful source is:

**Francesco Battistoni (2020)**, “On small discriminants of number fields of degree 8 and 9,” *Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux* **32**(2), 489–501, Theorem 1.1, pp. 491–492, and Table 2.2, p. 495. DOI: [10.5802/jtnb.1131](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.1131).

The paper explicitly states:

> “This completes the search for minimum discriminants for fields of degree 8”

Its Theorem 1.1 also gives the exact minima for degree-nine signatures $(1,4)$ and $(3,3)$:

$$
29\,510\,281,\qquad109\,880\,167.
$$

These are existing published results, not new computations.

**An important consequence:** the comparison is already provable for $n=9$, even though neither A343690 nor A343772 currently lists a ninth-degree term.

For degree nine, the possible signatures are $(9,0),(7,1),(5,2),(3,3),(1,4)$. Battistoni's Table 2.2 gives unconditional discriminant lower bounds, including

$$
|d_K|\ge1\,133\,345\,241
\quad\text{for signature }(7,1).
$$

The other unclassified signature, $(5,2)$, has a lower bound of $295\,584\,269$. The remaining signatures have established minima. Hence the exact sign-separated minima in degree nine follow from published results.

For degree eight, the known minimum in the negative-discriminant signature $(2,3)$ is $4\,286\,875$, established by Battistoni's 2019 classification. It exceeds the positive minimum $1\,257\,728$.

Thus the published classification and lower-bound results establish the conjectured inequalities for **every degree $2\le n\le9$**. They do not imply the conjecture for arbitrary degree.

## 2. Proof and strongest established partial result

### Theorem

**Proved from published results.** For every integer $n$ with $2\le n\le9$,

$$
\begin{cases}
P_n<N_n,&n\equiv0,1\pmod4,\\
P_n>N_n,&n\equiv2,3\pmod4.
\end{cases}
$$

Furthermore,

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P_8&=1\,257\,728,&N_8&=4\,286\,875,\\
P_9&=29\,510\,281,&N_9&=109\,880\,167.
\end{aligned}}
$$

These are consequences of known classifications, not newly established minimum-discriminant theorems.

### Preliminary facts

**Existence of both minima.** Kiran S. Kedlaya (2011), “A construction of polynomials with squarefree discriminants,” arXiv:1103.5728, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3, establishes the existence of infinitely many number fields in every prescribed admissible degree and signature. Thus $P_n$ and $N_n$ both exist for every $n\ge2$.

**Discriminant-sign lemma.** For every degree-$n$ number field of signature $(r_1,r_2)$,

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(d_K)=(-1)^{r_2}.
$$

To verify this, order the roots of a defining polynomial so that conjugate nonreal roots occur in pairs. Complex conjugation acts on the Vandermonde determinant

$$
V=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)
$$

by $r_2$ transpositions. Therefore $\overline V=(-1)^{r_2}V$. Since the polynomial discriminant is $V^2$, its sign is $(-1)^{r_2}$. The field discriminant differs from the polynomial discriminant by a positive integer square, so the signs agree.

### Degrees 2–7

**Known.** All signature-wise minimum discriminants in degrees at most seven have been classified. Relevant references include Buchmann–Ford–Pohst (1993) for degree four, Schwarz–Pohst–Diaz y Diaz (1994) for degree five, Pohst (1982) and Liang–Zassenhaus (1977) for degree six, and Diaz y Diaz (1983, 1984, 1988) for degree seven.

Battistoni's 2019 paper also records the state of these classifications, explicitly noting that minimum discriminants were already known for all signatures in degrees at most seven.

The exact comparisons, shown in Section 3, prove the conjecture in these degrees.

### Degree 8

**Known:** Francisco Diaz y Diaz's 1987 classification of totally imaginary octic fields establishes a field of signature $(0,4)$ with minimum discriminant

$$
1\,257\,728.
$$

Francesco Battistoni (2019), “The minimum discriminant of number fields of degree 8 and signature $(2,3)$,” *Journal of Number Theory* **198**, pp. 386–395, Theorem 1, proves:

> “Then the minimum value of $|d_K|$ is equal to 4286875.”

Battistoni (2020), Table 2.1, gives unconditional lower bounds for every signature. The relevant information is:

| Signature | Sign | Minimum or unconditional lower bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(0,4)$ | + | Exact: 1,257,728 |
| $(2,3)$ | − | Exact: 4,286,875 |
| $(4,2)$ | + | ≥ 11,660,853 |
| $(6,1)$ | − | ≥ 42,071,532 |
| $(8,0)$ | + | ≥ 158,960,873 |

The exact values are from the cited classifications; the remaining bounds are from Table 2.1.

Taking the minimum separately over positive and negative signatures gives

$$
P_8=1\,257\,728<4\,286\,875=N_8.
$$

This proves the conjecture for $n=8$.

### Degree 9

This is the most useful result for the OEIS entries.

Battistoni (2020), Theorem 1.1, establishes that the minimum absolute discriminants for signatures $(1,4)$ and $(3,3)$ are respectively

$$
29\,510\,281,\qquad109\,880\,167.
$$

His Table 2.2, p. 495, provides the following unconditional bounds for the other signatures:

| Signature | Sign | Minimum or unconditional lower bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(1,4)$ | + | Exact: 29,510,281 |
| $(3,3)$ | − | Exact: 109,880,167 |
| $(5,2)$ | + | ≥ 295,584,269 |
| $(7,1)$ | − | ≥ 1,133,345,241 |
| $(9,0)$ | + | ≥ 4,516,673,524 |



Every degree-nine number field has exactly one of these five signatures.

For positive discriminants, the candidate $29\,510\,281$ is smaller than the unconditional lower bounds for both other positive signatures. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P_9=29\,510\,281.}
$$

For negative discriminants, the established minimum $109\,880\,167$ for $(3,3)$ is smaller than the lower bound for $(7,1)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{N_9=109\,880\,167.}
$$

Consequently,

$$
P_9<N_9,
$$

as predicted because $9\equiv1\pmod4$.

**This completes the proof for $2\le n\le9$.**

The calculation uses unconditional bounds; no Riemann hypothesis or generalized Riemann hypothesis is assumed.

### What remains open

For $n\ge10$, the classifications and bounds checked here do not establish the general inequality.

In particular, knowing a small number field of the predicted sign provides an upper bound for one of $P_n,N_n$, but does not exclude a smaller field of the opposite sign.

Minkowski's and Odlyzko's bounds distinguish signatures, but their ordering alone does not prove the ordering of actual minima.

I found no established reduction of this general conjecture to a separately named open problem.

## 3. Computational verification

I ran a Python/SymPy test for all eight degrees $n=2,\ldots,9$. It checks irreducibility, polynomial discriminants, signatures, and the expected inequalities, using the published field discriminants.

The complete reproducible script is available here:

[Download check_A343690.py](#file-0)

The main computational check is:

```python
import sympy as s

for n, (positive, negative, P, N) in fields.items():
    for poly, expected in [(positive, P), (negative, -N)]:
        f = s.Poly(poly, x)
        assert f.is_irreducible

        d = int(s.discriminant(f))
        index = 17 if (n == 8 and expected < 0) else 1
        assert d == expected * index**2

        r1 = int(f.count_roots(-s.oo, s.oo))
        r2 = (n - r1) // 2
        assert (-1)**r2 == (1 if d > 0 else -1)

    assert (P < N) == (n % 4 in (0, 1))
```

### Important verification detail

The defining polynomial for the degree-eight field with discriminant $-4\,286\,875$ has polynomial discriminant

$$
-1\,238\,906\,875=-4\,286\,875\cdot17^2.
$$

The discrepancy arises because the power basis does not equal the full ring of integers. The LMFDB entry [8.2.4286875.1](https://www.lmfdb.org/NumberField/8.2.4286875.1) supplies an integral basis whose change-of-basis determinant is $1/17$. field 8.2.4286875.1

The script additionally verifies that the nontrivial basis element is integral by computing its monic characteristic polynomial:

$$
t^8+9t^7+38t^6+89t^5+128t^4
+106t^3+48t^2+11t+1.
$$

This accounts for the square factor exactly.

**Scope of computation:** The test confirms the arithmetic of the supplied polynomial witnesses and the numerical comparisons for $2\le n\le9$. It does not independently enumerate every number field below each bound. The global minimality assertions depend on the cited published classifications and unconditional lower bounds.
