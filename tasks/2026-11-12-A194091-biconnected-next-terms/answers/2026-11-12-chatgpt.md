# OEIS A194091 — Status, Proof, and Verification Report

**Investigation date:** November 12, 2026 **Sequence:** [OEIS A194091](https://oeis.org/A194091) **Problem:** Michael D. Weiner's conjecture concerning eight additional terms of the enumeration of labeled biconnected squaregraphs.

## 1. Status: Proved, although still labeled conjectural in OEIS

**Verdict: The eight proposed terms are correct. The underlying generating-function identity already appears with a mathematical derivation in Robert Scherer's 2021 dissertation. Furthermore, Knuth's 2011 description of squaregraphs gives a direct route to proving the conjecture through connected 3-noncrossing matchings.**

The OEIS entry nevertheless continues to label both the numerical terms (2016) and the generating-function identity (2017) as conjectures.

### Decisive existing sources

**Don Knuth (2011), `squaregraph.w`, Stanford University.**

Knuth describes biconnected squaregraphs using perfect matchings on cyclically arranged boundary points. His construction imposes two restrictions: exclusion of the cyclic pattern `abcabc`, and a condition on the regions formed by the chords.

The decisive sentence is:

> A final constraint is that none of the regions may include more than one circle segment.

Knuth explains that this condition excludes articulation points.

As proved explicitly in Section 2 below, these two restrictions are precisely the conditions that a matching be 3-noncrossing and have a connected crossing graph.

**Robert Scherer (2021), *Topics in Number Theory and Combinatorics*, Ph.D. dissertation, University of California, Davis, §4.4, pp. 88–91.**

Scherer identifies A194091 with quadrangulations of a $2n$-gon whose interior vertices have degree at least four and states the generating-function identity

$$
\boxed{D(x)=C(xD(x)^2)} \tag{1}
$$

as equation (4.30), deriving it using $B_2$ basis webs.

His text explicitly acknowledges the OEIS conjecture and then sketches its derivation. The discussion is described as somewhat informal, so it is more accurate to call this a published derivation than a fully detailed standalone formal proof.

The underlying representation-theoretic results are found in Greg Kuperberg, *Spiders for rank 2 Lie algebras*, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **180** (1996), 109–151, particularly Theorem 6.9 and §8.4.

## 2. Proof: Exact enumeration through connected matchings

We establish the relevant combinatorial equivalence and derive an exact coefficient formula.

### 2.1 Precise definitions

A *3-noncrossing perfect matching* on $[2n]$ is a perfect matching having no three chords that cross pairwise when drawn inside a circle in the order $1,\ldots,2n$.

Its *crossing graph* has one vertex for each chord; two vertices are adjacent when their chords cross.

Let $m_n$ be the number of such matchings whose crossing graph is connected.

Let $a(n)$ count the labeled biconnected squaregraphs of perimeter $2n$, with Knuth's boundary-edge orientation convention. We retain the OEIS convention for the degenerate $n=1$ case.

We will prove

$$
\boxed{a(n)=m_n\quad(n\geq1).} \tag{2}
$$

### 2.2 A planar-topological lemma

**Lemma (proved).** For a perfect matching drawn as chords of a circle, its crossing graph is connected if and only if every region of the chord arrangement contains at most one arc between consecutive boundary endpoints.

**Proof.**

Take a generic chord drawing with $n$ chords, $q$ interior crossing points, and $c$ connected components in the union of the chords.

The number $c$ is exactly the number of connected components of the crossing graph.

Regarding the union of chords as a plane graph, its numbers of vertices and edges are

$$
V_H=2n+q,\qquad E_H=n+2q.
$$

By Euler's formula for a graph with $c$ connected components, the number of bounded faces not incident to the enclosing circle is

$$
F_{\mathrm{int}}=E_H-V_H+c=q-n+c.
$$

Now add the boundary circle, subdivided into its $2n$ arcs. The resulting graph is connected, with

$$
V=2n+q,\qquad E=3n+2q.
$$

Euler's formula therefore gives the number of regions inside the circle:

$$
F_{\mathrm{total}}=E-V+1=n+q+1.
$$

Consequently, the number of regions incident to at least one boundary arc is

$$
F_{\mathrm{boundary}}
=F_{\mathrm{total}}-F_{\mathrm{int}}
=\boxed{2n-c+1}.
$$

There are exactly $2n$ boundary arcs, and each belongs to one region.

Thus every boundary arc belongs to a different region if and only if

$$
2n-c+1=2n,
$$

which is equivalent to $c=1$.

This proves the lemma. $\square$

### 2.3 Squaregraphs and matchings

Knuth's construction associates biconnected squaregraphs with cyclic perfect matchings satisfying two conditions.

First, the forbidden pattern `abcabc` is exactly a triple of pairwise crossing chords. Its exclusion is therefore equivalent to the 3-noncrossing condition.

Second, Knuth's boundary-region restriction is, by the lemma, equivalent to connectedness of the crossing graph.

His construction and inverse recover the squaregraph from its chord partition. With the distinguished oriented boundary edge, the cyclic positions become the ordered boundary labels used for matchings on $[2n]$.

Hence the two enumerated classes coincide, establishing (2).

**Result:** Weiner's 2017 conjecture identifying A194091 with connected 3-noncrossing perfect matchings follows from Knuth's existing correspondence and the elementary Euler-characteristic lemma above.

The lemma is made explicit here for checkability; no priority or originality claim is made for the underlying correspondence.

### 2.4 Generating-function identity

Define

$$
D(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}d_nx^n,
$$

where $d_n$ counts all 3-noncrossing perfect matchings on $2n$ points, with $d_0=1$.

This is the established sequence A005700, with

$$
\boxed{
d_n=\frac{6(2n)!(2n+2)!}
{n!(n+1)!(n+2)!(n+3)!}.
} \tag{3}
$$

The interpretation and formula are recorded in OEIS A005700.

Set

$$
C(x)=1+\sum_{n\geq1}a(n)x^n.
$$

Consider an arbitrary nonempty 3-noncrossing matching, and select the connected crossing-graph component containing the chord incident to boundary point 1.

Suppose this component contains $k$ chords.

Its $2k$ endpoints determine $2k$ boundary gaps. Any remaining chord does not cross a chord of the selected component, so the lemma implies that its two endpoints belong to the same gap. Conversely, inserting an arbitrary 3-noncrossing matching in each gap introduces no crossings with the selected component.

Thus the original matching decomposes uniquely into one connected matching of size $k$ and $2k$ independent smaller matchings.

Counting all possibilities gives

$$
D(x)=1+\sum_{k\geq1}a(k)x^kD(x)^{2k},
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{D(x)=C(xD(x)^2).}
$$

This supplies a direct combinatorial proof of the identity stated by Scherer.

### 2.5 Exact coefficient formula

Since $D(0)=1$, the series

$$
y=xD(x)^2
$$

has a unique formal compositional inverse $x=\phi(y)$.

Then

$$
C(y)=D(\phi(y)).
$$

Lagrange–Bürmann inversion gives, for $n\geq1$,

$$
a(n)=\frac1n[x^{n-1}]D'(x)D(x)^{-2n}.
$$

Using

$$
\frac{d}{dx}D(x)^{1-2n}
=(1-2n)D'(x)D(x)^{-2n},
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{
a(n)=-\frac{1}{2n-1}[x^n]D(x)^{-(2n-1)}.
} \tag{4}
$$

This is an exact formula, not an asymptotic approximation.

Equivalently, expanding (1) yields the triangular recurrence

$$
\boxed{
a(n)=d_n-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}
a(k)[x^{n-k}]D(x)^{2k}.
} \tag{5}
$$

Together with (3), either (4) or (5) determines every term of A194091.

## 3. Computational verification

All calculations below were executed using exact integer arithmetic.

### 3.1 Reproducible Python program

This program evaluates the generating function through degree 18 by the triangular recurrence, independently checks every coefficient through Lagrange inversion, and compares the eight conjectured values.

```python

from math import factorial as f

N = 18
d = [6*f(2*n)*f(2*n+2)//
     (f(n)*f(n+1)*f(n+2)*f(n+3))
     for n in range(N+1)]

def mul(a, b):
    return [
        sum(a[j]*b[k-j] for j in range(k+1))
        for k in range(N+1)
    ]

unit = [1] + [0]*N
powers = [unit]

for _ in range(2*N):
    powers.append(mul(powers[-1], d))

# Method 1: triangular recurrence
a = [1]
for n in range(1, N+1):
    a.append(d[n] - sum(
        a[k]*powers[2*k][n-k]
        for k in range(1, n)
    ))

# Method 2: Lagrange inversion
inv = unit.copy()
for n in range(1, N+1):
    inv[n] = -sum(
        d[k]*inv[n-k]
        for k in range(1, n+1)
    )

negative = [unit]
for _ in range(2*N):
    negative.append(mul(negative[-1], inv))

for n in range(1, N+1):
    assert -negative[2*n-1][n] == (2*n-1)*a[n]

assert a[1:11] == [
    1,1,3,14,82,554,4132,33154,281459,2499523
]

claimed = [
    23031395,218862682,2134993642,21301755122,
    216752986922,2243977451994,
    23590001630055,251411579942151
]

print("a(1..10):", a[1:11])
print("a(11..18):", a[11:19])
print("all eight conjectured terms match:",
      a[11:19] == claimed)
print("triangular and Lagrange agree for n=1..18: True")
```

## 4. Verdict and proposed OEIS comment

**Final verdict:** **Proved; the conjectured numerical terms are all correct.** Scherer's 2021 dissertation already presents the generating-function identity that determines them, while Knuth's earlier squaregraph construction supports a direct combinatorial proof. The explicit Euler-characteristic lemma in this report establishes the connection with crossing-connected matchings, and the canonical decomposition of those matchings proves the generating-function identity for every $n$. Computations independently reproduce all existing OEIS terms through $n=10$ and both formal-series algorithms agree through $n=18$. Confidence in the mathematical conclusion is high, subject to the standard graph-class and labeling conventions of Knuth's construction. The report is not a claim of a new research theorem: its contribution is an explicit proof reconstruction and reproducible verification of a result supported by older sources.
