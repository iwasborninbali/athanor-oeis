# OEIS A369831 — Status and proof attempt

**Date:** November 12, 2026 **Task:** [A369831 EGF conjecture B](https://github.com/iwasborninbali/athanor-oeis/blob/main/tasks/2026-11-12-A369831-egf-conjecture-b/TASK.md)

A structural theorem about the corresponding permanent polynomial is proved below, but it does not settle the conjecture for the integers $1,\ldots,n$.

## 1. Status

### Precise formulation

For $n\geq1$, let $S_n$ be the set of permutations of $(1,\ldots,n)$. For $p=(p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1})\in S_n$, define the symmetric Toeplitz matrix

$$
T_n(p)=(p_{|i-j|})_{0\leq i,j<n}.
$$

Define

$$
a(n)=\left|\left\{\operatorname{per}T_n(p):p\in S_n\right\}\right|,
\qquad a(0)=1.
$$

The conjectured exponential generating function is

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n\geq0}a(n)\frac{x^n}{n!}
=\frac1{1-x}-\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{24}.}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
a(2)=1,\quad a(4)=23,\quad
a(n)=n!\ \text{for every other }n\geq0.
}
$$

In particular, the unresolved assertion is:

> For every $n\geq5$, the map $p\mapsto\operatorname{per}T_n(p)$ is injective on $S_n$.

This is a well-posed arithmetic injectivity problem.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial results

### 2.1. Small exceptional cases

**Proved.** For $n=2$,

$$
T_2(a,b)=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{per}T_2(a,b)=a^2+b^2.
$$

Swapping $a,b$ does not change the permanent, so $a(2)=1$.

For $n=4$, there is an explicit collision:

$$
p=(1,3,4,2),\qquad q=(4,2,3,1),
$$

with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{per}T_4(p)
=\operatorname{per}T_4(q)=1265.}
$$

Exhaustive enumeration gives exactly 23 values.

### 2.2. A structural theorem

Consider the permanent as a polynomial in independent variables:

$$
P_n(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})
=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}x_{|i-\sigma(i)|}.
$$

**Theorem (proved here).** For every $n\geq3$, no nonidentity permutation of the variables $x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}$ leaves $P_n$ invariant.

#### Proof

Evaluate the first partial derivatives at $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$.

Each matrix entry appears in exactly $(n-1)!$ terms of the permanent expansion. There are $n$ entries at distance zero and $2(n-d)$ entries at distance $d>0$. Therefore

$$
\left.\frac{\partial P_n}{\partial x_0}\right|_{\mathbf1}
=n(n-1)!,
$$

and

$$
\left.\frac{\partial P_n}{\partial x_d}\right|_{\mathbf1}
=2(n-d)(n-1)!,\qquad1\leq d<n.
$$

If $n$ is odd, these derivatives are pairwise distinct. Consequently, a variable permutation preserving $P_n$ must fix every variable.

Now suppose $n=2m\geq4$.

The derivatives are again pairwise distinct except that those for $x_0$ and $x_m$ are equal. Thus the only possible nontrivial symmetry is exchanging $x_0$ and $x_m$.

We exclude this by comparing two coefficients.

First,

$$
[x_0^{n-2}x_1^2]P_n=n-1,
$$

because such a monomial arises precisely from a transposition of adjacent indices.

Second,

$$
[x_m^{n-2}x_1^2]P_n=2(m-1)=n-2.
$$

To verify the second identity, observe that the distance-$m$ entries form the permutation matrix of the involution

$$
f(i)=i+m\pmod{2m}.
$$

A permutation contributing $x_m^{n-2}x_1^2$ agrees with $f$ on exactly $n-2$ rows. On the remaining two rows it must exchange their $f$-images.

Writing those rows as $u$ and $m+v$, with $0\leq u,v<m$, both replacement edges have distance one precisely when $|u-v|=1$. There are $2(m-1)$ ordered choices.

Hence the two coefficients are unequal:

$$
n-1\neq n-2.
$$

Swapping $x_0$ and $x_m$ cannot preserve $P_n$.

Therefore $P_n$ has no nontrivial variable-permutation symmetry for $n\geq3$. $\square$

### 2.3. A consequence: injectivity for suitable integer weights

**Corollary (proved).** For every fixed $n\geq3$, there exist $n$ distinct positive integers whose $n!$ arrangements in a symmetric Toeplitz matrix produce $n!$ different permanent values.

Indeed, choose an integer $B>n!$ and set

$$
v_j=B^{(n+1)^j},\qquad0\leq j<n.
$$

For a monomial $x_0^{e_0}\cdots x_{n-1}^{e_{n-1}}$ of degree $n$, substitution gives the power

$$
B^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e_j(n+1)^j}.
$$

Since $0\leq e_j\leq n$, distinct exponent vectors give distinct exponents of $B$, by uniqueness of base-$(n+1)$ representation.

Every coefficient of $P_n$ is at most $n!<B$, so its coefficients become digits in the base-$B$ representation, without carries.

The theorem says that the $n!$ variable permutations produce different polynomials. Their base-$B$ evaluations are consequently different integers. $\square$

More generally, for each fixed $n\geq3$, all but $O_n(H^{n-1})$ ordered $n$-tuples of weights in $\{1,\ldots,H\}^n$ produce distinct permanents. This follows because each collision equation is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $n$, which has at most $nH^{n-1}$ zeros on that grid, and there are finitely many pairs of arrangements.

**Limit of the result:** This proves generic injectivity, including for explicit integer weights, but does not imply injectivity for the particular small weights $1,\ldots,n$. The arithmetic specialization remains the obstruction.

## 3. Computational verification

### Exact algorithm

For each permutation $p$, the permanent was computed using subset dynamic programming.

Let $D_p(S)$ be the permanent of the submatrix consisting of rows $0,\ldots,|S|-1$ and columns in $S$. Set

$$
D_p(\varnothing)=1.
$$

Expanding along the last row gives the exact recurrence

$$
\boxed{
D_p(S)=
\sum_{j\in S}
p_{\left||S|-1-j\right|}
D_p(S\setminus\{j\}).
}
$$

Then

$$
\operatorname{per}T_n(p)=D_p(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}).
$$

This recurrence follows directly from the definition of the permanent and uses only integer addition and multiplication.

I implemented it in C++17 with precomputed subset transitions and parallel enumeration by the first element of $p$. Every permutation was evaluated, all resulting integers were sorted, and duplicates were counted.

## 4. Verdict

**Status: apparently open; not refuted.** The conjecture is precise and its exceptional cases at $n=2,4$ are genuine. Exact exhaustive computation confirms it through $n=11$, extending the published OEIS data through $n=9$ by two terms. The structural result proved here shows that the symbolic permanent polynomial has a trivial variable-permutation stabilizer for every $n\geq3$, and consequently that injectivity holds for generic and explicitly constructible integer weights. This is an auxiliary partial result, not a proof for the weights $1,\ldots,n$; no claim of priority is made for it. Confidence is high in the finite calculation and the elementary structural proof, but the literature-status assessment is qualified by the search-access limitations stated in §1.
