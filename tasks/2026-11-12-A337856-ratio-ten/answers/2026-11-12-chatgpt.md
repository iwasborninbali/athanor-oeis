## 2. Proof: independent verification

**Result: PROVED.** In fact, the stronger asymptotic formula holds:

$$
\boxed{a(n)\sim\frac{9}{20}\,10^{n-1}}
$$

This implies the desired limit immediately.

The density $1/20$ and the ratio limit are already stated in OEIS. The argument below independently establishes the density, including why the exceptional integers have density zero. No claim of mathematical novelty is made.

### 2.1. Precise formulation

Let

$$
S=\{(10r+6)(10s+6):r,s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\},
$$

where repeated products are counted only once.

Define

$$
A(X)=\#\{m\in S:m\leq X\}.
$$

For $n\geq1$, the sequence A337856 satisfies

$$
a(n)=A(10^n-1)-A(10^{n-1}-1).
$$

We will prove

$$
\boxed{A(X)=\frac{X}{20}+o(X).}
$$

### 2.2. Necessary congruence

Every factor ending in 6 has the form

$$
10r+6=2(5r+3).
$$

Consequently, every $N\in S$ is

$$
N=4uv,\qquad u\equiv v\equiv3\pmod5.
$$

Thus

$$
uv\equiv9\equiv4\pmod5,
$$

and hence

$$
N\equiv16\pmod{20}.
$$

Therefore

$$
S\subseteq\{N\geq1:N\equiv16\pmod{20}\},
$$

which establishes the upper bound

$$
A(X)\leq\frac{X}{20}+O(1).
$$

The congruence is not sufficient for membership: for example, $76\equiv16\pmod{20}$, but $76\notin S$. We must show that such exceptions become negligible.

### 2.3. Sufficient factorization criterion

**Lemma (proved).** Suppose $M$ is a positive integer such that

$$
M\equiv4\pmod5
$$

and $M$ has a prime divisor $p\equiv3\pmod5$. Then $4M\in S$.

**Proof.** Write $M=pq$. Since

$$
p\equiv3\pmod5,\qquad M\equiv4\pmod5,
$$

and $3^{-1}\equiv2\pmod5$, we have

$$
q\equiv4\cdot2\equiv3\pmod5.
$$

Therefore both $2p$ and $2q$ end in 6:

$$
2p\equiv2q\equiv6\pmod{10}.
$$

Finally,

$$
4M=(2p)(2q)\in S.
$$

This proves the lemma. $\square$

### 2.4. Exceptional integers have density zero

Define the exceptional set

$$
E=\{M\geq1:\text{no prime divisor of }M
\text{ is }3\pmod5\}.
$$

We claim

$$
\#\{M\leq T:M\in E\}=o(T).
$$

The only non-elementary input needed is the classical divergence

$$
\sum_{\substack{p\text{ prime}\\p\equiv3\pmod5}}\frac1p
=\infty.
$$

This follows from Mertens' theorem for primes in arithmetic progressions, itself a standard consequence of the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions. In particular,

$$
\sum_{\substack{p\leq y\\p\equiv3\pmod5}}\frac1p
=\frac14\log\log y+O(1).
$$

A reference giving this result and its derivation by partial summation is [*Dirichlet L-functions and primes in progressions*, Section 5, Corollary 5.1](https://openmaths.org/courses/NT-DIRL/NT-DIRL-11.html). L-functions and primes in progressions

**Proof of the zero-density claim.**

Fix any finite collection $P$ of distinct primes congruent to $3\pmod5$.

Every integer in $E$ avoids divisibility by every prime in $P$. By inclusion-exclusion, the number of positive integers up to $T$ avoiding these primes is

$$
T\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right)
+O(2^{|P|}).
$$

Thus, for every fixed finite $P$,

$$
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac{\#(E\cap[1,T])}{T}
\leq
\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
$$

But

$$
\log\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right)
\leq-\sum_{p\in P}\frac1p.
$$

Taking successively larger finite collections $P$ of primes congruent to $3\pmod5$, the right-hand side tends to $-\infty$. Hence

$$
\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right)\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\#(E\cap[1,T])=o(T).}
$$

The claim is proved. $\square$

### 2.5. Exact asymptotic density of A324297

Every integer $N\equiv16\pmod{20}$ can be written uniquely as

$$
N=4M,\qquad M\equiv4\pmod5.
$$

By the lemma, whenever $M$ has a prime divisor $3\pmod5$, the integer $N=4M$ belongs to $S$.

Consequently, any missing integer in the progression $16\pmod{20}$ corresponds to an exceptional integer $M\in E$.

The total number of integers in this progression up to $X$ is

$$
\frac X{20}+O(1),
$$

and the number of potentially missing integers is at most

$$
\#(E\cap[1,X/4])=o(X).
$$

It follows that

$$
\frac X{20}+O(1)-o(X)
\leq A(X)
\leq\frac X{20}+O(1).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{A(X)}X=\frac1{20}.}
$$

This proves the asymptotic density stated in OEIS A324297, without assuming that every integer congruent to $16\pmod{20}$ belongs to the sequence.

### 2.6. Derivation of the ratio limit

Recall

$$
a(n)=A(10^n-1)-A(10^{n-1}-1).
$$

From the density theorem,

$$
A(X)=\frac X{20}+o(X).
$$

Substituting the two endpoints gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(n)
&=\frac{10^n-10^{n-1}}{20}
+o(10^n)\\
&=\frac9{20}10^{n-1}
+o(10^{n-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{a(n)\sim\frac9{20}10^{n-1}.}
$$

In particular, $a(n)>0$ for all sufficiently large $n$, so the ratio is well-defined on a tail.

Now

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a(n)}{a(n-1)}
&=
\frac{\frac9{20}10^{n-1}(1+o(1))}
{\frac9{20}10^{n-2}(1+o(1))}\\[2mm]
&=10\frac{1+o(1)}{1+o(1)}
\longrightarrow10.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a(n)}{a(n-1)}=10.
}
$$

**Q.E.D.**

This is an unconditional proof. It uses an established theorem about primes in arithmetic progressions, not an unresolved conjecture.

## 3. Computational verification

**Status: COMPUTED.** I independently enumerated all distinct products below $10^9$ using Python.

The enumeration uses the fact proved in Section 2.2 that all possible products are congruent to $16\pmod{20}$. A byte array stores one flag per integer in that congruence class. Each pair of factors is enumerated once, in nondecreasing order.

The program also independently constructs primes with a sieve and tests the sufficient factorization criterion from Section 2.3.

### Executed Python program

```python

from math import isqrt

LIMIT = 10**9

# Index j represents the integer N = 20*j + 16.
seen = bytearray((LIMIT - 1 - 16)//20 + 1)

# Enumerate every distinct product of factors ending in 6.
for x in range(6, isqrt(LIMIT - 1) + 1, 10):
    for y in range(x, (LIMIT - 1)//x + 1, 10):
        seen[(x*y - 16)//20] = 1

counts = []
for n in range(1, 10):
    lo, hi = 10**(n-1), 10**n
    left = max(0, (lo - 16 + 19)//20)
    right = max(0, (hi - 16 + 19)//20)
    counts.append(sum(seen[left:right]))

print("A337856(1..9):", counts)
print("ratios a(n)/a(n-1), n=3..9:",
      [round(counts[n-1]/counts[n-2], 6)
       for n in range(3, 10)])
print("density among N < 10^9:",
      f"{sum(counts)/LIMIT:.8f}")

# Independently test the prime-divisor lemma.
T = 250000
prime = bytearray(b'\x01')*(T+1)
prime[0:2] = b'\x00\x00'

for p in range(2, isqrt(T)+1):
    if prime[p]:
        prime[p*p:T+1:p] = (
            b'\x00' * ((T-p*p)//p + 1)
        )

has_p3 = bytearray(T+1)
for p in range(2, T+1):
    if prime[p] and p % 5 == 3:
        has_p3[p:T+1:p] = b'\x01' * (T//p)

witnesses = sum(
    1 for m in range(4, T+1, 5) if has_p3[m]
)
violations = [
    m for m in range(4, T+1, 5)
    if has_p3[m] and not seen[(4*m-16)//20]
]

print("witness test M<=250000, M==4 (mod 5):",
      "witnessed=", witnesses,
      "violations=", len(violations))

print("unrepresented N==16 (mod 20), N<=1000000:",
      sum(1 for m in range(4, T+1, 5)
          if not seen[(4*m-16)//20]))
```
