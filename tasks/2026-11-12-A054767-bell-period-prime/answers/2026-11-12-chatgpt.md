# OEIS A054767 — Status and proof attempt

**Report date:** 12 November 2026 **Subject:** Period of the Bell numbers modulo primes and prime powers **Overall finding:** The central conjecture remains open in the sources located. Its prime-power consequence is already proved.

## 1. Status and literature review

### 1.1 Precise statement

Let $B_n$ denote the $n$-th Bell number, with $B_0=1$, and let

$$
a(m)=\min\{T\geq1:B_{n+T}\equiv B_n\pmod m
\text{ for all }n\geq0\}.
$$

For each prime $p$, define

$$
N_p=\frac{p^p-1}{p-1}
=1+p+\cdots+p^{p-1}.
$$

The conjecture underlying OEIS A054767 is

$$
\boxed{\text{For every prime }p,\quad a(p)=N_p.}
\tag{C}
$$

This is the **Bell-number maximal-period conjecture**, also associated with Samuel Wagstaff's work.

The remaining statements in the OEIS comment are established results, not independent open conjectures:

- **Known (1979):** $a(2^s)=3\cdot2^s$ for $s\geq2$.
- **Known (1979):** For an odd prime $p$, if $a(p)=N_p$, then $a(p^s)=p^{s-1}N_p$.
- **Known unconditionally (1979):** For every odd prime $p$ and $s\geq2$, \[ a(p^s)\mid p^{s-1}N_p,\qquad v_p(a(p^s))=s-1. \]

These are consequences of Theorems 6.2 and 6.4 of W. F. Lunnon, P. A. B. Pleasants and N. M. Stephens, *Arithmetic properties of Bell numbers to a composite modulus I*, *Acta Arithmetica* **35** (1979), 1–16. The 2025 OEIS commentary and its linked proof summary confirm the distinction.

### 1.2 Decisive literature

**Levine and Dalton (1962).** Jack Levine and R. E. Dalton, *Minimum Periods, Modulo p, of First-Order Bell Exponential Integers*, *Mathematics of Computation* **16** (1962), 416–423, established foundational results on minimum Bell periods. The original title says *Integers*, although the OEIS bibliography says *Integrals*.

**Wagstaff (1996).** Samuel S. Wagstaff Jr., *Aurifeuillian factorizations and the period of the Bell numbers modulo a prime*, *Mathematics of Computation* **65** (1996), 383–391, investigated the maximal-period question through factorization.

**Car, Gallardo, Rahavandrainy and Vaserstein (2008).** *About the period of Bell numbers modulo a prime*, *Bulletin of the Korean Mathematical Society* **45**(1), 143–155.

Their abstract explicitly describes the assertion as a conjecture about the multiplicative order of a root of $x^p-x-1$ over $\mathbb F_p$. The authors prove restrictions on possible orders, not the universal conjecture. State

**Montgomery, Nahm and Wagstaff (2010).** Peter L. Montgomery, Sangil Nahm and Samuel S. Wagstaff Jr., *The period of the Bell numbers modulo a prime*, *Mathematics of Computation* **79**(271), 1793–1800, Theorem 1.1, MR2630013, Zbl 1216.11028.

The theorem establishes $a(p)=N_p$ for every prime $p<126$, and also for

$$
p\in\{137,149,157,163,167,173\}.
$$

Its abstract states:

> “The period is shown to equal $N_p$ for most primes $p$ below 180.”

This is a computational theorem, not a proof for all primes.

**Gallardo (2016).** Luis Henri Gallardo, *A Property Of The Period Of A Bell Number Modulo A Prime Number*, *Applied Mathematics E-Notes* **16** (2016), 72–79, introduction, p. 72.

The paper explicitly says that equality of the minimum period with $N_p$ is:

> “conjectured, but not proved”

It then establishes conditional restrictions on the possible order.

## 2. Proof attempt and rigorous reduction

### 2.1 Equivalence to a known open problem

**Proved (classical):** Conjecture (C) is equivalent to the assertion that every root of

$$
f_p(X)=X^p-X-1\in\mathbb F_p[X]
$$

has multiplicative order $N_p$ in $\mathbb F_{p^p}^{\times}$.

Here is a self-contained proof of the equivalence.

**Step 1: Irreducibility.**

Choose a root $\alpha$ of $f_p$ in an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_p$. Then

$$
\alpha^p=\alpha+1.
$$

Applying Frobenius repeatedly gives

$$
\alpha^{p^j}=\alpha+j,\qquad 0\leq j\leq p.
$$

The first $p$ values are distinct and the next returns to $\alpha$. Thus the Frobenius orbit has length $p$, so the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ has degree $p$.

Consequently $f_p$ is irreducible and

$$
\mathbb F_p(\alpha)\cong\mathbb F_{p^p}.
$$

**Step 2: The Bell sequence has minimal shift polynomial $f_p$.**

Let $b=(B_0,B_1,\ldots)\bmod p$, and let $S$ denote the left-shift operator:

$$
(Sb)_n=B_{n+1}\pmod p.
$$

Touchard's congruence states that, for every $n\geq0$,

$$
B_{n+p}\equiv B_{n+1}+B_n\pmod p.
$$

Therefore

$$
f_p(S)b=(S^p-S-1)b=0.
$$

Consider the polynomial annihilator ideal

$$
I=\{g\in\mathbb F_p[X]:g(S)b=0\}.
$$

It contains $f_p$. Because $f_p$ is irreducible, the only ideals containing $(f_p)$ are $(f_p)$ and the whole ring.

But $I$ cannot be the whole ring, since $B_0=1$, hence $b\ne0$.

Thus

$$
\boxed{I=(f_p).}
$$

**Step 3: Characterize periods.**

For any positive integer $T$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
T\text{ is a period of }b
&\iff (S^T-1)b=0\\
&\iff X^T-1\in(f_p)\\
&\iff \alpha^T=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{a(p)=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathbb F_{p^p}^{\times}}(\alpha).}
\tag{1}
$$

**Step 4: Prove the universal upper bound.**

Since the Frobenius conjugates of $\alpha$ are $\alpha+j$, for $j\in\mathbb F_p$, their product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{1+p+\cdots+p^{p-1}}
&=\prod_{j\in\mathbb F_p}(\alpha+j)\\
&=\alpha^p-\alpha\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{a(p)\mid N_p.}
\tag{2}
$$

All steps are valid for $p=2$ as well.

The unresolved issue is whether the order can ever be a **proper divisor** of $N_p$.

This reduction is already known; it is not a new theorem. It is discussed explicitly by Car et al. (2008), Montgomery–Nahm–Wagstaff (2010), and in the MathOverflow question on the multiplicative order of roots of the Artin–Schreier polynomial.

### 2.2 Exact computational criterion

**Proved:** For a prime $p$, let $Q_p$ be the set of distinct prime factors of $N_p$. Then

$$
\boxed{
a(p)=N_p
\iff
X^{N_p/q}\not\equiv1\pmod{(p,\ X^p-X-1)}
\quad\text{for every }q\in Q_p.
}
\tag{3}
$$

Indeed, the order of $X$ divides $N_p$. If it is smaller, it divides $N_p/q$ for at least one prime factor $q$ of $N_p$.

This gives a finite verification algorithm for every fixed $p$, provided the prime factorization of $N_p$ is known.

It is the standard strategy of Wagstaff and his collaborators, not a new method.

### 2.3 The prime-power statement follows from published results

**Proved, conditional on the maximal-period assertion for $p$:**

Suppose $p$ is odd, $s\geq2$, and $a(p)=N_p$.

Set $T_s=a(p^s)$. Lunnon–Pleasants–Stephens, Theorem 6.2, gives

$$
T_s\mid p^{s-1}N_p,
\qquad
T_s\nmid p^{s-2}N_p.
$$

Reduction modulo $p$ also shows

$$
N_p=a(p)\mid T_s.
$$

Because $\gcd(p,N_p)=1$, these three facts force

$$
\boxed{T_s=p^{s-1}N_p.}
$$

This proves the conditional implication exactly as stated in A054767, using established results.

### 2.4 Partial result at the first reported unresolved prime

**Computed:** Take $p=139$, for which $N_{139}$ has 296 decimal digits.

The prime $q=557$ divides $N_{139}$, with

$$
v_{557}(N_{139})=1.
$$

A modular polynomial exponentiation gives

$$
\left[X^0\right]\left(
X^{N_{139}/557}\bmod(X^{139}-X-1)
\right)=48
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{139}.
$$

Since the constant coefficient of the identity polynomial is $1$, this establishes computationally that

$$
X^{N_{139}/557}\ne1
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{139}[X]/(X^{139}-X-1).
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{557\mid a(139).}
$$

This excludes one possible shortening of the maximal period, **but does not establish** $a(139)=N_{139}$.

It is not claimed as new: Montgomery–Nahm–Wagstaff (2010) already report checking all prime-factor pairs with $p<1100$ and $q<2^{31}$ in the corresponding range.

The value $48$ is a reproducible explicit witness from the computation below.

For each prime, the program used the factorization reported by SymPy and verified the nonidentity criterion (3). SymPy's probable-prime handling of sufficiently large factors means these outputs should be described as computational checks rather than formal primality-certified proofs.

All results agree with the established conjectural picture. No counterexample was found, and the verification range does not extend the previously published full-period range.

## 4. Verdict

**Verdict — open conjecture, proved conditional prime-power formula, no new general theorem.** As of 12 November 2026, the available literature supports the conclusion that $a(p)=(p^p-1)/(p-1)$ remains unproved for arbitrary primes, with no established counterexample. It is equivalent to the known open multiplicative-order problem for roots of $X^p-X-1$ over $\mathbb F_p$. The statements about $2^s$ and the implication for odd prime powers are established by Lunnon–Pleasants–Stephens (1979). I supplied a self-contained proof of the classical reduction, reproduced small-prime computations, and obtained an explicit modular witness for the factor $557$ at $p=139$, without claiming mathematical novelty. Confidence is high in the algebraic reduction and published theorems, high in the reproducibility of the reported calculations, and qualified regarding whether a very recent unindexed preprint has changed the global status. A suitable OEIS comment would say: *“The assertion that the Bell numbers have period $(p^p-1)/(p-1)$ modulo every prime $p$ is still open. Equivalently, a root of $X^p-X-1$ over $\mathbb F_p$ is conjectured to have multiplicative order $(p^p-1)/(p-1)$. The prime-power consequence is already Theorem 6.2 of Lunnon–Pleasants–Stephens (1979). Montgomery–Nahm–Wagstaff (2010) proved the equality for $p<126$ and six further primes; a December 2025 MathOverflow update reports additional verification at $p=127,131$, leaving $139$ as the first unresolved prime.”* No OEIS submission or repository modification was made.
