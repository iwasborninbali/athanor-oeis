# 1. Status — Barina’s congruence conjecture (OEIS A000043)

**Verdict as of November 12, 2026: Apparently open.** I found neither a published proof nor a counterexample. The statement is mathematically well-posed, and its forward implication is elementary. The reverse implication is a particular nonexistence assertion about Fermat pseudoprimes.

Let $p$ be prime and write $M_p=2^p-1$. Barina's conjecture is

$$
\boxed{
(p-1)^{M_p-1}\equiv1\pmod{M_p}
\iff
\left(M_p\text{ is prime or }p=2^{2^m}+1
\text{ for some }m\ge0\right).
}
$$

### Literature and provenance

**David Barina (2024), OEIS A000043, comment dated November 25, 2024.** The entry explicitly labels the assertion a conjecture, beginning:

> “Conjecture: for p prime, 2^p-1 is (a Mersenne) prime or p = 2^(2^m)+1 (is a Fermat number)”

and specifies the congruence displayed above. The live entry still presents it as conjectural.

**Thomas Ordowski (2023), OEIS A366029, October 1, 2023**, proposed the related criterion

$$
(k-1)^{2^k-2}\equiv1
\pmod{(2^k-1)k^2},\qquad k>1.
$$

The entry states:

> “Conjecture: for k > 1, (2^k-1)*k^2 divides (k-1)^(2^k-2)-1”

and supplies the same proposed Mersenne/Fermat classification.

This is a stronger congruence with a different domain. Its conjectural converse does not establish Barina's converse.

**Vladimir Shevelev, Gilberto García-Pulgarín, Juan Miguel Velásquez-Soto, and John H. Castillo (2012)**, *Overpseudoprimes, and Mersenne and Fermat Numbers as Primover Numbers*, *Journal of Integer Sequences* **15**, Article 12.7.7, Corollary 4, prove:

> “For a prime p, M_p = 2^p − 1 is either a prime or an overpseudoprime to base 2.”

This is relevant background, but it concerns base $2$, not base $p-1$. It neither proves nor refutes Barina's conjecture.

# 2. Proof attempt and rigorous partial results

## 2.1. Coprimality lemma — proved

**Lemma.** For every prime $p$,

$$
\gcd(p-1,2^p-1)=1.
$$

**Proof.** Suppose a prime $q$ divides both $p-1$ and $2^p-1$. Since $q\mid2^p-1$, the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $q$ divides $p$. That order cannot be $1$, so, because $p$ is prime,

$$
\operatorname{ord}_q(2)=p.
$$

Fermat's little theorem gives $p\mid q-1$. But $q\mid p-1$ implies $q\le p-1$, hence $q-1<p$, a contradiction. $\square$

Consequently the congruence is always a legitimate Fermat primality test with a coprime base.

## 2.2. Forward implication — proved

**Theorem.** If $p$ is prime and either $M_p$ is prime or $p$ is a Fermat prime, then

$$
(p-1)^{M_p-1}\equiv1\pmod{M_p}.
$$

**Proof.**

*Case 1: $M_p$ is prime.*

By the coprimality lemma and Fermat's little theorem,

$$
(p-1)^{M_p-1}\equiv1\pmod{M_p}.
$$

*Case 2: $p=2^{2^m}+1$ is prime.*

Here $p-1=2^{2^m}$.

Fermat's little theorem modulo $p$ yields

$$
2^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,
$$

and therefore

$$
p\mid 2(2^{p-1}-1)=M_p-1.
$$

As $2^p\equiv1\pmod{M_p}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(p-1)^{M_p-1}
&=2^{2^m(M_p-1)}\\
&=(2^p)^{\,2^m(M_p-1)/p}\\
&\equiv1\pmod{M_p}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both cases are proved. $\square$

In particular, $p=257$ satisfies the congruence even though $2^{257}-1$ is composite. A verified factor is

$$
535006138814359\mid 2^{257}-1.
$$

This is an intended exception, not a counterexample.

The argument also works for every prime with $p-1$ a power of $2$. Such primes are exactly Fermat primes: if $2^s+1$ is prime, $s$ must itself be a power of $2$, since an odd proper factor of $s$ would give a factorization of $2^s+1$.

## 2.3. A quadratic-nonresidue obstruction — proved

The following supplies a checkable sufficient condition for the Barina congruence to fail.

**Proposition.** Let $p$ be an odd prime and $q$ a prime divisor of $2^p-1$. If

$$
q\equiv1\pmod4
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\left(\frac{p-1}{q}\right)=-1,
$$

where the second expression is the Legendre symbol, then

$$
(p-1)^{2^p-2}\not\equiv1\pmod{2^p-1}.
$$

**Proof.** Put $a=p-1$, $E=2^p-2$, and write

$$
q-1=2^t u,\qquad u\text{ odd},\quad t\ge2.
$$

Since $a$ is a quadratic nonresidue modulo $q$, Euler's criterion gives

$$
a^{(q-1)/2}\equiv-1\pmod q.
$$

Thus the order $d=\operatorname{ord}_q(a)$ cannot divide $(q-1)/2$. Since $d\mid q-1$, its exact $2$-adic valuation must be $t\ge2$.

On the other hand,

$$
E=2(2^{p-1}-1)
$$

has $2$-adic valuation exactly $1$. Hence $d\nmid E$, so $a^E\not\equiv1\pmod q$.

Because $q\mid M_p$, the congruence also fails modulo $M_p$. $\square$

This is an elementary consequence of Euler's criterion, not a claim of a new theorem in the literature.

**Explicit certificate, $p=23$.**

$$
2^{23}-1=47\cdot178481.
$$

The integer $178481$ is prime (verifiable by trial division through $422$), and

$$
22^{89240}\equiv-1\pmod{178481}.
$$

Since $178481\equiv1\pmod4$, the proposition applies. Consequently the Barina congruence fails at $p=23$, as required.

The obstruction does not cover every composite Mersenne number, since a failing Fermat test need not arise from quadratic nonresiduosity.

## 2.4. What remains unproved

The entire unresolved part is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
p\text{ prime},\quad p-1\text{ not a power of }2,\\
2^p-1\text{ composite}
\\[2pt]\Longrightarrow\\[2pt]
(p-1)^{2^p-2}\not\equiv1\pmod{2^p-1}.
\end{gathered}
}
$$

Equivalently, no composite Mersenne number $M_p$, for a non-Fermat prime $p$, may be a Fermat pseudoprime to the particular base $p-1$.

This is a restricted pseudoprimality problem. I found no established equivalence with a different named open problem, such as the infinitude of Mersenne primes. The standard fact that every composite $M_p$ is a base-$2$ pseudoprime does not settle the question for base $p-1$.

# 3. Computational verification

**Computed:** all $669$ prime exponents $2\le p\le5000$.

I evaluated the defining modular exponentiation using GMP arbitrary-precision integers and independently classified each $M_p$ using the deterministic Lucas–Lehmer test. The program also checked the coprimality lemma.

The following C++17 code is reproducible with GMP and `gmpxx`:

```
#include <gmpxx.h>
#include <iostream>
#include <vector>
using namespace std;

bool lucas_lehmer(int p, const mpz_class& m) {
    if (p == 2) return true;
    mpz_class s = 4;
    for (int i = 0; i < p - 2; ++i)
        s = (s * s - 2) % m;
    return s == 0;
}

int main() {
    constexpr int B = 5000;
    vector<bool> prime(B + 1, true);
    prime[0] = prime[1] = false;

    for (int i = 2; i * i <= B; ++i)
        if (prime[i])
            for (int j = i * i; j <= B; j += i)
                prime[j] = false;

    vector<int> mersenne, exceptions, mismatches;
    int checked = 0, hits = 0, gcd_fail = 0;

    for (int p = 2; p <= B; ++p) {
        if (!prime[p]) continue;
        ++checked;

        mpz_class m, e, a = p - 1, r, g;
        mpz_ui_pow_ui(m.get_mpz_t(), 2, p);
        m -= 1;
        e = m - 1;

        mpz_powm(r.get_mpz_t(), a.get_mpz_t(),
                 e.get_mpz_t(), m.get_mpz_t());

        bool pass = (r == 1);
        bool fermat = p > 2 &&
                      (((p - 1) & (p - 2)) == 0);
        bool mp = lucas_lehmer(p, m);

        if (pass) ++hits;
        if (mp) mersenne.push_back(p);
        if (fermat && !mp) exceptions.push_back(p);
        if (pass != (mp || fermat))
            mismatches.push_back(p);

        mpz_gcd(g.get_mpz_t(), a.get_mpz_t(),
                m.get_mpz_t());
        if (g != 1) ++gcd_fail;
    }

    auto show = [](const string& s, const vector<int>& v) {
        cout << s << " (" << v.size() << "):";
        for (int x : v) cout << " " << x;
        cout << "\n";
    };

    cout << "prime exponents 2<=p<=5000: "
         << checked << "\n";
    show("Lucas-Lehmer Mersenne primes", mersenne);
    show("Fermat-prime exceptions", exceptions);
    cout << "congruence hits: " << hits << "\n";
    show("mismatches", mismatches);
    cout << "gcd(p-1,2^p-1)!=1: " << gcd_fail << "\n";
}
```

Compilation:

```

./barina
```

# 4. Verdict

**Status: open, to the extent established by this literature search.** The conjecture is coherent, and the forward implication is proved above using only multiplicative orders and Fermat's little theorem. The quadratic-nonresidue proposition gives an additional sufficient condition for rejecting composite cases, but it does not prove the converse. The GMP/Lucas–Lehmer computation checks all $669$ prime exponents through $5000$, finding precisely the $20$ Mersenne-prime exponents and the Fermat-prime exception $257$, with no counterexamples. These mathematical tools and observations are elementary or previously known in general form; **no claim of original discovery is made**. A suitable OEIS comment, if independently reviewed, would say: “The forward implication in Barina's conjecture follows from Fermat's little theorem: for prime $p$, $p\mid2^p-2$, and when $p$ is a Fermat prime, $p-1$ is a power of $2$. Direct verification using the Lucas–Lehmer test finds no counterexample for prime $p\le5000$ (669 exponents tested). The converse remains unproved.” No OEIS submission or repository modification was made.
