# OEIS A013588 — Status and Proof Report

**Date:** 12 November 2026 **Task:** A013588 — determinant majorization conjecture **Verdict:** Open in general; proved for an infinite family of orders and verified for several additional orders.

## 1. Status and literature review

### 1.1 Precise statement

Let

$$
\mathcal D_n=\{|\det A|:A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\},
$$

and define

$$
d_n=\min\{k\ge1:k\notin\mathcal D_n\},
\qquad
M_n=\max\mathcal D_n.
$$

Here $d_n$ is OEIS A013588 and $M_n$ is OEIS A003432 (with the latter indexed from $n=0$).

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{d_n<M_n\quad\text{for every }n\ge7.}
\tag{C}
$$

Because $M_n$ is itself attained, this is equivalent to saying that $\mathcal D_n$ is not an interval of consecutive integers from zero to its maximum.

It is well-posed. It is not the original *Consecutive Integer Determinant Conjecture*, which asserted the opposite property for every order and was refuted in 1990.

### 1.2 Historical results

**Rob Craigen (1990).** *The Range of the Determinant Function on the Set of $n\times n$ (0,1)-Matrices*, *Journal of Combinatorial Mathematics and Combinatorial Computing* **8**, 161–171.

Craigen proved that no $7\times7$ binary matrix has determinant 28, 29, 30, or 31, although determinant 32 is attainable.

His Section 4 explicitly asks:

> “Can we have $\beta'_n=\beta_n$ for $n>7$?”

Here $\beta'_n=d_n-1$ and $\beta_n=M_n$. This is the historical problem directly related to (C).

**Miodrag Živković (2006).** *Classification of small (0,1) matrices*, *Linear Algebra and its Applications* **414**, 310–346, arXiv:math/0511636.

This establishes determinant spectra for small orders, including $d_9=103$. Together with the subsequent computation by William Orrick, the values of A013588 are recorded through $n=10$.

**Richard P. Brent, William Orrick, Judy-anne Osborn and Paul Zimmermann (2011/2012).** *Maximal determinants and saturated D-optimal designs of orders 19 and 37*, arXiv:1112.4160, Section 8.

Their decisive abstract statement is:

> “we also find the complete spectrum of determinant values for -1 matrices of order 13.”

By the standard determinant correspondence, this gives the spectrum for $12\times12$ binary matrices, including $d_{12}=2173$.

**Rikhav Shah (2022).** *Determinants of binary matrices achieve every integral value up to $\Omega(2^n/n)$*, *Linear Algebra and its Applications* **645**, 229–236, DOI: 10.1016/j.laa.2022.03.024.

The abstract states:

> “the smallest natural number $d_n$ that is not the determinant of some binary matrix is at least $c\,2^n/n$”

with $c=1/201$. This is a lower bound for $d_n$, not a proof that $d_n<M_n$.

The live OEIS entry still explicitly labels the majorization statement a conjecture and gives no general resolution.

### 1.3 Recent preprints: 15 October–12 November 2026

A particularly relevant development is:

**Ronan Egan, Ilias Kotsireas, Stephen London and Pádraig Ó Catháin (2026).** *Recent progress in the construction of Hadamard matrices*, arXiv:2610.27175, submitted 19 October, revised 3 November 2026.

The authors report verification of the previously missing Hadamard matrices at the twelve orders below 2000 where existence had remained unsettled. Consequently, Hadamard matrices are now known at every positive multiple of four below 2000.

This does not directly resolve (C), but, combined with the elementary argument below, establishes (C) for **every $n\equiv3\pmod4$ with $7\le n\le1999$**, as well as infinitely many larger orders.

## 2. Proof attempt and rigorous partial result

### Theorem — a gap below every Hadamard maximum

**Proved.** Suppose a Hadamard matrix of order $m\ge8$ exists, where $4\mid m$. Put $n=m-1$ and

$$
M=\frac{m^{m/2}}{2^{m-1}}.
$$

Then $M=M_n$, and every binary $n\times n$ matrix $A$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{
|\det A|=M
\quad\text{or}\quad
|\det A|\le M\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}.
}
\tag{1}
$$

Moreover,

$$
M\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}<M-1.
\tag{2}
$$

Consequently $M_n-1$ is not a determinant, and

$$
\boxed{d_n<M_n.}
$$

### Proof

**Step 1. Convert binary matrices to sign matrices.**

Given $A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$, form the $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrix

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\mathbf1^T\\
\mathbf1&J-2A
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where $J$ is the all-ones matrix.

Subtracting the first row from all other rows gives

$$
|\det H|=2^n|\det A|.
\tag{3}
$$

Conversely, any sign matrix can be normalized to have its first row and column all $+1$, without changing its absolute determinant. Thus the correspondence is sufficient for both maximum-determinant and gap arguments.

**Step 2. Apply Hadamard's inequality.**

Every row of $H$ has Euclidean norm $\sqrt m$. Hence

$$
|\det H|\le m^{m/2}.
$$

Equality holds exactly when its rows are pairwise orthogonal.

Because a Hadamard matrix of order $m$ exists, normalization and (3) show that

$$
M_n=\frac{m^{m/2}}{2^{m-1}}=M.
\tag{4}
$$

**Step 3. Bound every nonmaximal determinant.**

Suppose $|\det H|<m^{m/2}$. Then some two rows $u,v$ are not orthogonal.

Their inner product is a nonzero even integer because $m$ is even and their entries lie in $\{-1,+1\}$. Therefore

$$
|\langle u,v\rangle|\ge2.
$$

The area of the parallelogram spanned by these two rows is

$$
\sqrt{\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2}
\le\sqrt{m^2-4}.
$$

Applying the volume form of Hadamard's inequality to these two rows and bounding the lengths of the remaining $m-2$ rows by $\sqrt m$ gives

$$
|\det H|
\le m^{(m-2)/2}\sqrt{m^2-4}
=m^{m/2}\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}.
$$

Dividing by $2^{m-1}$ proves (1).

**Step 4. Show that the gap contains an integer.**

For every $x\in(0,1)$,

$$
1-\sqrt{1-x}>\frac{x}{2}.
$$

Taking $x=4/m^2$ gives

$$
M-M\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}
>\frac{2M}{m^2}.
\tag{5}
$$

We now establish $M\ge m^2/2$ for every multiple of four $m\ge8$.

Indeed,

$$
M=2\left(\frac m4\right)^{m/2}
\ge 2^{m/2+1}.
$$

For $m=8$, $2^{m/2+1}=m^2/2=32$. The inequality persists when $m$ increases by four, since its exponential side is multiplied by four while $(m+4)^2/m^2<4$.

Thus $2M/m^2\ge1$, so (5) implies

$$
M-M\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}>1.
$$

This proves (2). Hence $M-1$ cannot occur, while $M$ does occur.

Therefore $d_n\le M-1<M$. $\square$

### Consequences

**Proved for an infinite family.** Sylvester's construction supplies Hadamard matrices of every order $m=2^k$, $k\ge2$. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{d_{2^k-1}<M_{2^k-1}\qquad(k\ge3).}
$$

**Proved for further orders via known constructions.** The theorem applies to every Hadamard order $m\ge8$, not just powers of two. In particular, the October–November 2026 verification mentioned in Section 1 yields the conjecture for all $n\equiv3\pmod4$, $7\le n\le1999$.

The inequality supplies more than one missing integer: all integers strictly between

$$
M\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}
\quad\text{and}\quad M
$$

are unattainable.

This is a deduction from the classical determinant inequality, not a claimed new general theorem about determinant spectra. The unresolved orders, especially those with $n\not\equiv3\pmod4$, require other arguments.

## 3. Computational verification

All calculations below use exact integer arithmetic. There are no floating-point determinants or numerical tolerances.

The program performs three checks:

1. Exhaustively enumerates determinant spectra for $1\le n\le5$, using unordered sets of distinct nonzero rows (which suffice for all nonzero determinants).
2. Constructs Hadamard matrices of orders 8, 12, 16, and 32; verifies $HH^T=mI$; and computes the associated binary determinants and missing intervals.
3. Tests the non-Hadamard determinant bound on 1,000 pseudorandom sign matrices at each of orders 8 and 12.

```python

from itertools import combinations
from math import comb, isqrt
from random import Random

def det(a):
    """Exact determinant via fraction-free Bareiss elimination."""
    a = [list(r) for r in a]
    n, s, prev = len(a), 1, 1
    for k in range(n - 1):
        if a[k][k] == 0:
            i = next((i for i in range(k + 1, n)
                      if a[i][k]), None)
            if i is None:
                return 0
            a[k], a[i] = a[i], a[k]
            s = -s
        p = a[k][k]
        for i in range(k + 1, n):
            for j in range(k + 1, n):
                x = a[i][j]*p - a[i][k]*a[k][j]
                assert x % prev == 0
                a[i][j] = x // prev
            a[i][k] = 0
        prev = p
    return s * a[-1][-1]

def sylvester(k):
    h = [[1]]
    for _ in range(k):
        h = ([r+r for r in h]
             + [r+[-x for x in r] for r in h])
    return h

def paley12():
    q = 11
    residues = {x*x % q for x in range(1, q)}
    def chi(z):
        z %= q
        return 0 if z == 0 else (
            1 if z in residues else -1)
    return ([[1]*12]
            + [[1] + [chi(i-j) - (i == j)
                       for j in range(q)]
               for i in range(q)])

print("EXHAUSTIVE BINARY SPECTRA")
for n in range(1, 6):
    rows = [tuple((v >> j) & 1 for j in range(n))
            for v in range(1, 2**n)]
    vals = {abs(det(t)) for t in combinations(rows, n)}
    d = next(k for k in range(1, max(vals)+2)
             if k not in vals)
    print(f"n={n} sets={comb(2**n-1,n)} "
          f"M={max(vals)} d={d}")

print("HADAMARD GAP CERTIFICATES")
for tag, h in [
    ("Sylvester8", sylvester(3)),
    ("Paley12", paley12()),
    ("Sylvester16", sylvester(4)),
    ("Sylvester32", sylvester(5))
]:
    m = len(h)
    assert all(
        sum(x*y for x, y in zip(h[i], h[j]))
        == (m if i == j else 0)
        for i in range(m) for j in range(m)
    )
    b = [[(1-h[i][j])//2 for j in range(1, m)]
         for i in range(1, m)]
    M = m**(m//2) // 2**(m-1)
    assert abs(det(b)) == M

    U = isqrt((M*M*(m*m-4)) // (m*m))
    assert U < M-1
    print(f"{tag}: n={m-1} M={M} "
          f"nonmax<={U} missing_tail={M-U-1}")

print("RANDOM LEMMA CHECKS")
rng = Random(13588)
for m in (8, 12):
    checked = 0
    for _ in range(1000):
        h = [[2*rng.randrange(2)-1
              for _ in range(m)] for _ in range(m)]
        D = abs(det(h))
        if D*D != m**m:
            assert m*m*D*D <= m**m*(m*m-4)
            checked += 1
    print(f"m={m} tested={checked} passed=True")
```
