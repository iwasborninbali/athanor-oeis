# OEIS A053695: Status and proof attempt for the record-prime-gap conjecture

**Investigation date:** November 12, 2026 **Verdict:** Apparently open; no proof or counterexample identified. **Main results:** An unconditional upper bound for successive record prime gaps, an independent sieve verification through $10^8$, and a check of all 84 differences in the published OEIS table.

## 1. Status

### Precise statement

Let $p_1=2,p_2=3,\ldots$ denote the primes, and define

$$
g_k=p_{k+1}-p_k.
$$

Let $G_1,G_2,\ldots$ be the successive strict records among the $g_k$. Thus

$$
G_1,G_2,\ldots=1,2,4,6,8,14,18,20,\ldots
$$

is OEIS A005250.

OEIS A053695 is

$$
a(n)=G_{n+1}-G_n.
$$

The conjecture is

$$
\boxed{\forall n\ge1,\quad G_{n+1}\le 2G_n.}
\tag{C}
$$

It is well-defined and equivalent to the A053695 inequality.

### Primary source

John W. Nicholson, December 30, 2015, OEIS [A053695](https://oeis.org/A053695):

> Conjecture: a(n)

I also checked the OEIS wiki's prime-conjecture list and literature references associated with the relevant sequences, including works by Shanks, Nicely, Kourbatov, and Visser.

**Search limitation:** Direct arXiv search-page access was unavailable; searches therefore used external indexing and accessible abstracts. MathSciNet and zbMATH were searched through public web indexing, not through comprehensive authenticated database searches. The literature search is not exhaustive.

**Status conclusion:** I found no accepted proof, refutation, or established equivalence to another named open conjecture. The best-supported classification is **open, subject to the stated literature-search limitations**.

## 2. Proof attempt and partial result

A proof of (C) remains unavailable. Nevertheless, there is a rigorous, unconditional upper bound on the next record gap in terms of the current one.

### Proposition — unconditional bound

Let $G_n$ denote the $n$-th record prime gap. Then, for every $n\ge1$,

$$
\boxed{
G_{n+1}\le \operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,G_n+1).
}
\tag{1}
$$

This bound is generally much weaker than (C), but it holds without any conjectural assumptions.

### Proof

Fix $n$, write $G=G_n$, and set

$$
L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,G+1).
$$

For every integer $k$ satisfying $2\le k\le G+1$, we have $k\mid L$. Therefore

$$
L+k\equiv0\pmod{k}.
$$

Since $L+k>k$, each of these numbers is composite.

Consequently,

$$
L+2,L+3,\ldots,L+G+1
$$

is a run of $G$ consecutive composite integers.

Let $u$ be the largest prime smaller than $L+2$, and let $v$ be the smallest prime greater than $L+G+1$.

The primes $u,v$ are consecutive, and

$$
u\le L+1,\qquad v\ge L+G+2.
$$

It follows that

$$
v-u\ge G+1>G.
$$

Thus a gap strictly exceeding $G$ occurs no later than the gap beginning at $u$.

Because $G$ is itself a record, the first gap exceeding $G$ must occur after its first appearance. That first larger gap is exactly the next record $G_{n+1}$.

Let $s$ denote the prime at which this next record begins. Then

$$
s\le u\le L+1.
$$

By Bertrand's postulate, for every integer $s>1$, there is a prime strictly between $s$ and $2s$.

If $t$ is the prime immediately after $s$, this gives

$$
t<2s.
$$

Therefore

$$
G_{n+1}=t-s<s\le L+1.
$$

Since $G_{n+1}$ and $L$ are integers,

$$
G_{n+1}\le L.
$$

Substituting the definition of $L$,

$$
\boxed{
G_{n+1}\le\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,G_n+1).
}
$$

$\square$

### Interpretation and limitations

This is a **proved**, elementary upper bound, not a proof of the original conjecture.

For example, if $G_n=14$, the proposition yields

$$
G_{n+1}\le\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,15)=360360,
$$

whereas the conjecture would require $G_{n+1}\le28$.

The construction uses the classical composite-block argument for the existence of arbitrarily large prime gaps, together with Bertrand's postulate. I make no novelty claim for these ingredients or their combination.

One further elementary observation is that every record gap after $G_1=1$ is even. Thus, for $n\ge2$, a counterexample to (C) would necessarily satisfy

$$
G_{n+1}\ge2G_n+2.
$$

This observation is proved, but does not rule out such a counterexample.

## 3. Computational verification

I performed two checks: an independent exhaustive sieve through $10^8$, and a separate comparison of the 85 published record gaps in OEIS A005250.

### Independent sieve

The program implements an odd-only Sieve of Eratosthenes, enumerates every consecutive prime pair with both endpoints at most $10^8$, and records each strictly larger gap.

It also checks the small instances of the composite-block lemma used above.

The complete executable Python source is available here:

```python

for i in range(1, len(sieve)):

        continue
    p = 2 * i + 1
    if p > LIMIT:
        break

    gap = p - prev
    if gap > records[-1]:
        records.append(gap)
    prev = p

violations = [
    (n, a, b)
    for n, (a, b) in enumerate(
        zip(records, records[1:]), 1

    if b > 2*a
```

## 4. Verdict

**Status as of November 12, 2026: apparently open.** The conjecture $A053695(n)\le A005250(n)$, equivalently $G_{n+1}\le2G_n$, is well-posed and survives all 84 comparisons in the published OEIS data. An independent sieve verified its first 24 comparisons through $10^8$, with no counterexample. I found no valid general proof or refutation; Nicholson's 2018 Ramanujan-prime argument does not establish the required inequality, while probabilistic objections remain heuristic. The unconditional bound $G_{n+1}\le\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,G_n+1)$ is proved above, but is far too weak to settle the conjecture and is not claimed as novel mathematics. Confidence is high in the elementary proof and executed computations, but the literature-status assessment remains limited by incomplete direct access to scholarly databases and archive search interfaces.
