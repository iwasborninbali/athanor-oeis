## 2. Proof attempt and rigorous partial results

### 2.1. Precise formulation

Let $P(n)$ be the parent of $n$ in the Doudna tree, and let $L(n)$ be its left child.

If $n=\prod p^{e_p}$, define

$$
L(n)=\prod_{p^{e_p}\parallel n}\operatorname{nextprime}(p)^{e_p}.
$$

For $n>1$,

$$
P(n)=
\begin{cases}
n/2,&2\mid n,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\prod_{p^{e_p}\parallel n}
\operatorname{prevprime}(p)^{e_p},&2\nmid n.
\end{cases}
$$

The two children of a node $m$ are $L(m)$ and $2m$. These definitions agree with OEIS A005940 and A252463.

For $n>2$, its sibling is therefore

$$
S(n)=
\begin{cases}
L(n/2),&n\text{ even},\\
2P(n),&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$

The conjecture is that the set

$$
\mathcal A=\{n>2:\sigma(n)\text{ is a descendant of }S(n),
\text{ including }S(n)\}
$$

is finite.

### 2.2. A necessary condition involving the largest prime factor

**Lemma 1 (proved).** Let $P^+(m)$ denote the largest prime factor of $m>1$. If $u$ is a descendant of $v>1$ in the Doudna tree, then

$$
\boxed{P^+(u)\ge P^+(v).}
$$

**Proof.** Each downward edge performs one of two operations:

- $m\mapsto2m$, which cannot decrease the largest prime factor.
- $m\mapsto L(m)$, which replaces every prime divisor by its successor and therefore strictly increases the largest prime factor.

The result follows by induction on the number of edges. $\square$

Consequently, a necessary condition for membership in A347391 is

$$
\boxed{P^+(\sigma(n))\ge P^+(S(n)).}\tag{1}
$$

This condition is not sufficient, but it eliminates several infinite families.

### 2.3. Complete classification of squarefree terms

**Theorem 1 (proved).** The squarefree members of A347391 are exactly

$$
\boxed{\{3,5,15\}.}
$$

**Proof.** We consider odd and even squarefree integers separately.

*Case A: $n$ odd and squarefree.*

Let $p$ be the largest prime divisor of $n$, and suppose $p\ge7$. Write

$$
q=\operatorname{prevprime}(p).
$$

We use the classical strengthening of Bertrand's postulate: for every integer $t>3$, there is a prime strictly between $t$ and $2t-2$, attributed to Chebyshev (1852). A statement of this bound appears in OEIS A113755.

Applying it to $t=(p+1)/2$ gives

$$
q>\frac{p+1}{2}.
$$

Because $n$ is squarefree,

$$
\sigma(n)=\prod_{r\mid n}(r+1).
$$

Every odd prime divisor of $r+1$ is at most $(r+1)/2$, since $r+1$ is even. Hence

$$
P^+(\sigma(n))\le\frac{p+1}{2}<q.
$$

But the sibling of odd $n$ is $2P(n)$, which contains $q$ as a prime factor. In fact $q$ is its largest prime factor. Thus

$$
P^+(\sigma(n))<P^+(S(n)),
$$

contradicting Lemma 1.

Therefore an odd squarefree member has no prime factor greater than 5. The only possibilities greater than 1 are $3,5,15$. All three qualify:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
n&S(n)&\sigma(n)\\ \hline
3&4&4\\
5&6&6\\
15&12&24
\end{array}
$$

For $15$, the value $24$ is the right child of $12$.

*Case B: $n$ even and squarefree.*

Write $n=2u$, where $u>1$ is odd and squarefree, and let $p=P^+(u)$.

The sibling of $n$ is $L(u)$, whose largest prime factor is $\operatorname{nextprime}(p)>p$. On the other hand,

$$
\sigma(2u)=3\prod_{r\mid u}(r+1).
$$

Every prime divisor of this expression is at most

$$
\max\left(3,\frac{p+1}{2}\right)
<\operatorname{nextprime}(p).
$$

Again Lemma 1 rules out membership.

Finally, $n=2$ does not qualify: $\sigma(2)=3$ is a descendant of $2$ itself, not a sibling.

This proves the theorem. $\square$

### 2.4. Two additional infinite families classified

**Theorem 2 (proved).** The only power of two in A347391 is $4$.

**Proof.** For $n=2^a$, $a\ge2$,

$$
S(n)=3^{a-1},
\qquad
\sigma(n)=2^{a+1}-1.
$$

For $a\ge5$,

$$
2^{a+1}-1<3^{a-1}.
$$

Both kinds of downward edges strictly increase the numerical value, so $\sigma(n)$ cannot be a descendant of the larger number $S(n)$.

The remaining cases are direct:

- $a=2$: $S(4)=3$ and $7\to5\to3$ along parent edges, so $4$ qualifies.
- $a=3$: $\sigma(8)=15$, while $S(8)=9$; both children of $9$ exceed $15$.
- $a=4$: $\sigma(16)=31$, while $S(16)=27$; both children of $27$ exceed $31$.

Also, $2$ does not qualify. $\square$

**Theorem 3 (proved).** For an odd prime $p$,

$$
\boxed{4p\in\mathcal A\iff p=5.}
$$

**Proof.** The sibling of $4p$ is

$$
S(4p)=3\operatorname{nextprime}(p),
$$

while multiplicativity gives

$$
\sigma(4p)=7(p+1).
$$

For $p\ge7$, every prime factor of $7(p+1)$ is at most

$$
\max\left(7,\frac{p+1}{2}\right)
<\operatorname{nextprime}(p).
$$

Lemma 1 rules out membership.

The remaining possibilities are $p=3,5$. For $p=3$, $\sigma(12)=28$, but the sibling is $15$, whose children are $30$ and $35$; neither $15$ nor any descendant can equal $28$. For $p=5$,

$$
\sigma(20)=42=2\cdot21=2S(20),
$$

so $20$ qualifies. $\square$

### 2.5. What remains unsolved

These theorems rule out every squarefree candidate except three, every power of two except one, and all numbers $4p$ except $20$. They do **not** prove that A347391 is finite: integers with repeated odd prime factors remain largely unclassified.

In particular, for odd $n$, the sufficient condition

$$
\sigma(n)=2^aP(n),\qquad a\ge1,
$$

produces a member, since $\sigma(n)=2^{a-1}S(n)$. The coprime-product closure of this family is already known from Karttunen's July 2024 OEIS comment; it is not a new result here.

For example,

$$
19679625=3^3\cdot5^3\cdot7^3\cdot17
$$

satisfies

$$
P(19679625)=351000,\qquad
\sigma(19679625)=44928000=2^7\cdot351000.
$$

This is an exact membership certificate, not evidence for infinitely many solutions.

The three classification theorems above are proved here, but I have not established publication priority. I found no identical statements in the references inspected; that alone does not make them historically new.

## 3. Computational verification

**Computed, not proved:** I independently enumerated all integers

$$
2\le n\le 2^{25}=33\,554\,432.
$$

The test uses the exact divisor sum $\sigma(n)$ and the Doudna parent map. It checks whether the ancestor chain of $\sigma(n)$ reaches $P(n)$ without first reaching $n$.

### Reproducible C++17 program

```

#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

int main() {
    const int N = 1 << 25;
    vector<long long> sigma(N+1, 1);

    for (int d=2; d<=N; ++d)
        for (int k=d; k<=N; k+=d)
            sigma[k] += d;

    int M = *max_element(sigma.begin()+1, sigma.end());
    vector<int> spf(M+1), prev(M+1), primes;

    // Linear smallest-prime-factor sieve
    for (int i=2; i<=M; ++i) {
        if (!spf[i]) {
            spf[i]=i;
            primes.push_back(i);
        }
        for (int p : primes) {
            long long j=1LL*i*p;
            if (j>M || p>spf[i]) break;
            spf[j]=p;
        }
    }

    int last=0;
    for (int i=1; i<=M; ++i) {
        prev[i]=last;
        if (i>=2 && spf[i]==i) last=i;
    }

    auto parent = [&](int x) {
        if (x%2==0) return x/2;
        int y=1;
        while (x>1) {
            int p=spf[x], q=prev[p];
            do {
                y*=q;
                x/=p;
            } while (x%p==0);
        }
        return y;
    };

    vector<int> terms, squarefree, powers2, fourp;
    int odd_other=0;

    for (int n=2; n<=N; ++n) {
        int m=parent(n), s=(int)sigma[n];

        while (s>m && s!=n)
            s=parent(s);

        if (s!=m) continue;
        terms.push_back(n);

        int x=n;
        bool sqfree=true;
        while (x>1) {
            int p=spf[x];
            x/=p;
            if (x%p==0) sqfree=false;
            while (x%p==0) x/=p;
        }
        if (sqfree) squarefree.push_back(n);

        if ((n&(n-1))==0)
            powers2.push_back(n);

        if (n%4==0 && n/4>=3 &&
            (n/4)%2 && spf[n/4]==n/4)
            fourp.push_back(n);

        if (n%2 && (sigma[n]%m ||
            (((sigma[n]/m)&((sigma[n]/m)-1))!=0)))
            ++odd_other;
    }

    auto print=[&](const string& label,
                   const vector<int>& a) {
        cout << label << "=";
        for (size_t i=0; i<a.size(); ++i)
            cout << (i?",":"") << a[i];
        cout << "\n";
    };

    cout << "N=" << N
         << "\nmax_sigma=" << M << "\n";

    print("terms", terms);
    print("squarefree_terms", squarefree);
    print("powers_of_2_terms", powers2);
    print("four_times_prime_terms", fourp);

    cout << "odd_terms_not_power2_times_parent="
         << odd_other << "\n";
}
```
