# A004137 — Status and proof attempt for the Wichmann ruler conjecture

**Investigation date:** 12 November 2026 **Sequence:** [OEIS A004137](https://oeis.org/A004137) — maximal number of edges in a graceful graph on $n$ vertices.

## 1. Status: the literal conjecture is refuted

**Verdict: Refuted as written. A weaker, related optimal-length conjecture remains open.**

The statement quoted in A004137 is:

> If the conjecture is true that an optimal ruler with more than 12 segments is a Wichmann ruler then the sequence continues 232, 251, 270, 289, 308, 327, ...

— Peter Luschny, OEIS A004137, 9 October 2011, updated 1 October 2015.

There is a counterexample already documented in OEIS A193802, *Length of optimal Wichmann rulers*, authored by Peter Luschny on 22 October 2011:

> By contrast [0, 1, 2, 8, 15, 16, 26, 36, 46, 56, 59, 63, 65, 68] is an optimal ruler with length 68 which is not a Wichmann ruler.



This ruler has **14 marks, 13 segments, and length 68**. Consequently, it satisfies the hypothesis “more than 12 segments,” but is not a Wichmann ruler.

The distinction matters:

- **Refuted:** Every optimal ruler with more than 12 segments must itself be a Wichmann ruler.
- **Not refuted by this example:** For each sufficiently large number of marks, at least one maximum-length ruler is a Wichmann ruler.
- **Still open:** Whether the Wichmann construction always attains the maximum possible length in the unverified range.

The 2026 OEIS entry A395909 records a refined version, restricting attention to rulers with more than 13 segments and allowing reflection.

Thus the counterexample resolves the literal assertion, but it does **not** disprove the proposed continuation of A004137.

### Established frontier and recent literature

The distinction between the two conjectures is explicit in Luschny's [Perfect Rulers](https://oeis.org/wiki/User:Peter_Luschny/PerfectRulers) notebook. His stronger formulation concerns all optimal rulers with more than 13 segments, up to reflection. His weaker formulation requires only one optimal Wichmann ruler for each segment count greater than 12. The counterexample above does not settle either properly qualified formulation.

The most relevant sources are:

| Author, year, source | Established result |
| --- | --- |
| B. Wichmann, 1963, *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* 38, 465–466 | Explicit construction of restricted difference bases. |
| Peter Luschny, 2011, OEIS A193802 and *Perfect Rulers* | Explicit optimal non-Wichmann ruler of length 68; distinction between the strong and weak conjectures. |

The decisive sentence from Schwartau et al.'s abstract is:

> Our compute cluster found perfect rulers with all lengths up to 244 in 443 years of combined CPU time.



**Known:** A004137 is verified through $a(26)=232$. The exhaustive length-244 computation does not establish $a(27)=251$, since a longer 27-mark ruler might exist.

## 2. Counterexample and mathematical verification

### 2.1 Precise formulation

Let

$$
R=\{0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_{n-1}=L\}.
$$

Define its positive difference set by

$$
D(R)=\{x_j-x_i:0\leq i<j<n\}.
$$

The ruler is *complete* if

$$
D(R)=\{1,2,\ldots,L\}.
$$

Then

$$
a(n)=\max\{L:\text{a complete ruler of length }L
\text{ has }n\text{ marks}\}.
$$

This is equivalent to A004137's graceful-graph definition: a complete ruler permits choosing one distinct edge for every difference $1,\ldots,L$, producing a graceful graph with $L$ edges. Conversely, the vertex labels of a graceful graph with $L$ edges necessarily measure all differences $1,\ldots,L$.

### 2.2 The explicit counterexample

Consider the previously published ruler

$$
R=(0,1,2,8,15,16,26,36,46,56,59,63,65,68).
$$

It has 14 marks, hence 13 segments. Its consecutive gaps are

$$
(1,1,6,7,1,10,10,10,10,3,4,2,3).
$$

**Completeness (finite verification):** Its 91 mark-pairs realize every integer difference from 1 through 68. The program in Section 3 independently checks

$$
D(R)=\{1,\ldots,68\}.
$$

**Optimality (known result):** OEIS A004137 establishes $a(14)=68$. Therefore a complete 14-mark ruler of length 68 is optimal.

**Failure of Wichmann form (proved):**

The standard Wichmann construction has consecutive gaps

$$
W(r,s)=
1^r,\ (r+1),\ (2r+1)^r,\ (4r+3)^s,\
(2r+2)^{r+1},\ 1^r,
$$

where $u^v$ denotes $v$ consecutive gaps of size $u$, and $r,s\geq0$.

Its number of marks and total length are

$$
N=4r+s+3,
$$

$$
L=4r(r+s+2)+3(s+1).
$$

These are Wichmann's established construction formulas.

For $N=14$, we need

$$
4r+s=11.
$$

Because $r,s$ are nonnegative integers, only three parameter pairs are possible.

| $r$ | $s$ | Wichmann length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 11 | 36 |
| 1 | 7 | 64 |
| 2 | 3 | 68 |

Therefore a standard Wichmann ruler with 14 marks and length 68 must be $W(2,3)$, whose gap sequence is

$$
(1,1,3,5,5,11,11,11,6,6,6,1,1).
$$

Its possible gap sizes are $\{1,3,5,6,11\}$. Our counterexample has gaps of sizes 7 and 10. Thus it cannot equal $W(2,3)$ or its reflection.

The minor Wichmann variant likewise cannot produce this ruler: for each $(r,s)$, its length is one less than that of the corresponding major variant.

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
R\text{ is optimal,}\\
R\text{ has }13>12\text{ segments,}\\
R\text{ is not of Wichmann type, even up to reflection.}
\end{gathered}
}
$$

This refutes the literal universal assertion. It is a verification of a **previously published counterexample**, not a new discovery.

### 2.3 What the counterexample does not establish

The weaker conjecture concerns the numerical equality

$$
a(n)=W_{\max}(n-1)
$$

for sufficiently large $n$, where $W_{\max}(k)$ denotes the greatest length supplied by a Wichmann ruler with $k$ segments.

OEIS A289761 gives

$$
W_{\max}(k)=
\frac{k^2-((k\bmod6)-3)^2}{3}+k.
$$



**Known construction, not established optimality:** Substituting $k=n-1$ produces the valid lower bounds

| $n$, marks | Constructed length | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 26 | 232 | Proven optimal |
| 27 | 251 | Lower bound only |
| 28 | 270 | Lower bound only |
| 29 | 289 | Lower bound only |
| 30 | 308 | Lower bound only |
| 31 | 327 | Lower bound only |
| 32 | 350 | Lower bound only |

For the unverified entries, the remaining question is whether a complete ruler with the same number of marks can be longer. The counterexample at $n=14$ does not answer that question.

## 3. Computational check

I wrote and executed the following Python 3 program using only the standard library.

It independently checks the counterexample, enumerates small optimal rulers by exhaustive search, tests both Wichmann families for a finite parameter range, and computes the conjectured continuation.

```python

from itertools import combinations

def complete(a):
    L = a[-1]
    distances = {
        y-x for i,x in enumerate(a)
        for y in a[i+1:]
    }
    return distances == set(range(1, L+1))

def wichmann(r, s, minor=False):
    gaps = (
        [1]*r + [r+1]
        + [2*r+1]*(r+int(minor))
        + [4*r+3]*s
        + [2*r+2]*(r+1-int(minor))
        + [1]*r
    )
    a = [0]
    for g in gaps:
        a.append(a[-1] + g)
    return a

def exact_max(n):
    if n == 1:
        return 0
    for L in range(n*(n-1)//2, n-2, -1):
        for interior in combinations(range(1, L), n-2):
            if complete((0, *interior, L)):
                return L
    raise AssertionError(n)

C = [0,1,2,8,15,16,26,36,46,56,59,63,65,68]

print('counterexample: marks=%d segments=%d length=%d complete=%s' %
      (len(C),len(C)-1,C[-1],complete(C)))

print('counterexample gaps:',
      [y-x for x,y in zip(C,C[1:])])

possible = [
    wichmann(r, 11-4*r, minor)
    for r in range(3)
    for minor in (False, True)
]

print('Wichmann or reflected among 14-mark rulers:',
      any(C == a or
          C == [a[-1]-x for x in a[::-1]]
          for a in possible))

print('Wichmann 14-mark (r,s,length):',
      [(r,11-4*r,wichmann(r,11-4*r)[-1])
       for r in range(3)])

print('exhaustive max length n=1..8:',
      [exact_max(n) for n in range(1,9)])

assert all(
    complete(wichmann(r,s,minor))
    for r in range(21)
    for s in range(21)
    for minor in (False,True)
)

print('Wichmann completeness: PASS (r,s=0..20, both types: 882 rulers)')

print('Wichmann lower bounds n=27..32:',
      [max(wichmann(r,n-4*r-3)[-1]
           for r in range((n-3)//4+1))
       for n in range(27,33)])
```

**All checks passed.**

The exact enumeration for $1\leq n\leq8$ reproduces A004137. The 882 Wichmann-family checks cover both major and minor variants for $0\leq r,s\leq20$. They test completeness, not global optimality. In particular, the values for $n=27,\ldots,32$ are certified constructions, **not** independently proven sequence terms.

The program does not independently exhaust all 14-mark rulers to prove $a(14)=68$; that optimality statement relies on the established OEIS value. The direct checks of completeness and non-Wichmann structure are independent.

## 4. Verdict

**Final classification: refuted under the literal universal interpretation; open under the intended existential or suitably qualified universal interpretation.** An optimal 14-mark, 13-segment non-Wichmann ruler of length 68 is explicitly recorded by Peter Luschny in OEIS A193802 (22 October 2011), so the unqualified assertion that every optimal ruler with more than 12 segments is Wichmann is false. The conjecture that Wichmann constructions always attain the maximum length beyond the verified range remains open; $a(26)=232$ is established, whereas $a(27)=251$ and subsequent values are only lower bounds. This investigation contributes an independent computational verification of the published counterexample, but no new mathematical theorem or improved bound. Confidence is high in the counterexample and its implications, and qualified regarding the absence of very recent literature because the direct arXiv and Zenodo interfaces could not be searched. An appropriate OEIS comment would distinguish the formulations: “The universal assertion for rulers with more than 12 segments is false: OEIS A193802 gives an optimal, non-Wichmann 14-mark ruler of length 68. The conjecture that a Wichmann ruler attains the maximal length for every sufficiently large number of marks remains open.” No OEIS submission or repository edit was made.
