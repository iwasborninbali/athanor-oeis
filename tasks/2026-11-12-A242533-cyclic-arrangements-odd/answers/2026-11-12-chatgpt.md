## 2. Proof: Complete and elementary

**Theorem (known, Martin Fuller, 2026).** Let $N(m)$ count cyclic arrangements of $\{1,\ldots,m\}$, modulo rotation and reversal, satisfying

$$
\gcd(|x-y|,x+y)=1
$$

for every pair of cyclically adjacent elements $x,y$.

Then

$$
\boxed{N(m)=0\quad\text{for every odd }m\ge1.}
$$

In fact, the stronger existence characterization holds:

$$
\boxed{N(m)>0\iff m\text{ is even}.}
$$

### Lemma: Characterization of admissible neighbors

For distinct positive integers $a,b$,

$$
\gcd(|a-b|,a+b)=1
$$

if and only if

$$
a\not\equiv b\pmod2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\gcd(a,b)=1.
$$

**Proof.**

Suppose $a,b$ have the same parity. Both $a+b$ and $a-b$ are even. Consequently,

$$
\gcd(|a-b|,a+b)\ge2.
$$

Hence admissible neighbors must have opposite parity.

Now suppose $a,b$ have opposite parity, and put

$$
d=\gcd(|a-b|,a+b).
$$

Both arguments of this gcd are odd, so $d$ is odd. Moreover, $d$ divides

$$
(a+b)+(a-b)=2a
$$

and

$$
(a+b)-(a-b)=2b.
$$

Since $d$ is odd, it follows that $d\mid a$ and $d\mid b$. Conversely, every common divisor of $a,b$ divides both $a+b$ and $a-b$.

Therefore,

$$
\gcd(|a-b|,a+b)=\gcd(a,b)
$$

whenever $a,b$ have opposite parity. This proves the lemma. $\square$

### Proof of the conjecture

Consider an admissible cyclic arrangement

$$
(c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_m),\qquad c_{m+1}=c_1.
$$

By the lemma, every adjacent pair consists of one odd and one even number.

Thus the parity sequence must alternate:

$$
\text{odd},\text{even},\text{odd},\text{even},\ldots
$$

A closed cycle with alternating parities necessarily has even length: after an odd number of steps, parity has changed, so the last edge cannot return to its starting parity.

Therefore no admissible cycle of odd length exists.

$$
\boxed{N(2k+1)=0\quad(k\ge0).}
$$

This proves Fuller's conjecture. $\square$

### Stronger conclusion: All positive even lengths work

For every even $m\ge2$, consider the cycle

$$
C_m=(1,2,3,\ldots,m).
$$

Every consecutive pair $k,k+1$ satisfies

$$
\gcd((k+1)-k,(k+1)+k)
=\gcd(1,2k+1)=1.
$$

For the closing pair $m,1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gcd(m-1,m+1)
&=\gcd(m-1,2)\\
&=1,
\end{aligned}
$$

because $m$ is even.

Hence $C_m$ is admissible. Together with the odd-length obstruction, this proves the existence characterization. The length-two case uses the convention in the original NPC definition.

**Novelty assessment:** This is a detailed reconstruction of the known parity argument, supplemented by an elementary construction for even lengths. It is not claimed as a new mathematical discovery.

The graph-theoretic interpretation is immediate: admissible edges form a bipartite graph partitioned by parity, and every Hamiltonian cycle in such a graph has even length. The NPC-to-Hamiltonian-cycle correspondence is described in Stanislav Sykora (2014), *On Neighbor-Property Cycles*, *Stan's Library*, Volume V, DOI [10.3247/SL5Math14.002](https://doi.org/10.3247/SL5Math14.002).

## 3. Independent computational check

**Method:** Exact subset dynamic programming for Hamiltonian cycles, with vertex 1 fixed to remove rotational symmetry. Reversal symmetry is removed by dividing the oriented count by 2 for lengths at least 3.

The program checks both the neighbor-characterization lemma and the resulting cycle counts.

```python

from functools import cache
from math import gcd

def count_cycles(m):
    adj = [
        sum(
            1 << (b - 1)
            for b in range(1, m + 1)
            if a != b
            and gcd(abs(a - b), a + b) == 1
        )
        for a in range(1, m + 1)
    ]

    full = (1 << m) - 1

    @cache
    def paths(mask, last):
        if mask == full:
            return int(bool(adj[last] & 1))

        available = adj[last] & ~mask & full
        total = 0

        while available:
            bit = available & -available
            available -= bit
            total += paths(
                mask | bit,
                bit.bit_length() - 1
            )

        return total

    oriented = paths(1, 0)
    return oriented if m <= 2 else oriented // 2

limit = 18

for a in range(1, limit + 1):
    for b in range(a + 1, limit + 1):
        assert (
            gcd(b - a, b + a) == 1
        ) == (
            (a - b) % 2 != 0
            and gcd(a, b) == 1
        )

print(
    f"edge_equivalence: OK, all 153 "
    f"unordered distinct pairs in 1..{limit}"
)

for m in range(1, limit + 1):
    print(f"m={m:2d} cycles={count_cycles(m)}")
```
