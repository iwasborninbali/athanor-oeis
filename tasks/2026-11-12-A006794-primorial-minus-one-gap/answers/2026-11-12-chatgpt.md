# OEIS A006794 — Status and proof attempt

**Report date:** November 12, 2026 **Subject:** Arkadiusz Wesolowski's conjecture on prime gaps preceding primorial-minus-one primes.

## 1. Status

**Verdict: Apparently open. No proof or counterexample was found in the sources examined.**

The conjecture appears in [OEIS A006794](https://oeis.org/A006794) as a comment by Arkadiusz Wesolowski, dated June 19, 2016:

> Conjecture: if p# - 1 is a prime number, then the previous prime is greater than p# - exp(1)*p.

### Precise formulation

Let

$$
P=p\#=\prod_{\substack{\ell\le p\\\ell\text{ prime}}}\ell,
$$

and suppose $N=P-1$ is prime. Write $q=\operatorname{prevprime}(N)$, where `prevprime` means the greatest prime strictly smaller than $N$.

The conjecture becomes

$$
\boxed{P-q<ep.}
$$

Equivalently, the gap between consecutive primes $q<N$ satisfies

$$
N-q<ep-1.
$$

This interpretation is important. If “previous prime” instead means the largest prime below $P$, the claim is trivial because $P-1$ itself is prime. The nontrivial interpretation concerns the prime preceding $P-1$.

### Literature and source checks

**OEIS and related entries.** A006794 still labels the statement a conjecture. A057704 gives the indices of primorial-minus-one primes. A055211, introduced by Robert G. Wilson V in 2000 and subsequently extended, is directly relevant:

$$
A055211(n)=p_n\#-\operatorname{prevprime}(p_n\#-1).
$$

Its published definition is:

> a(n) is the smallest m such that m > 1 and A002110(n) - m is prime.

Consequently, Wesolowski's conjecture is equivalent to

$$
A055211(n)<e\,p_n
$$

for every $n$ belonging to A057704.

**Closest relevant published theorem.** Antonín Čejchan, Michal Křížek, and Lawrence Somer (2022), *On Remarkable Properties of Primes Near Factorials and Primorials*, *Journal of Integer Sequences*, Vol. 25, Article 22.1.4, Theorem 18, prove the following.

If $s<p$ are consecutive primes and $q$ is prime with

$$
p\#-s^2<q<p\#-1,
$$

then $p\#-q$ is prime.

Their decisive conclusion is:

> then q# − p is also prime.

Here the quotation uses the paper's variable names.

This is a theorem about the form of a prime's distance from a primorial. It does **not** establish that a prime must exist within distance $ep$ of $p\#$.

The paper separately identifies the related lesser-Fortunate-number assertion as Conjecture 14 and reports verification for the first 2,000 primes using A055211. That assertion concerns the primality of the first offset, not Wesolowski's numerical upper bound.

**Recent literature.** I searched arXiv and Zenodo using A006794, primorial-minus-one primes, preceding primes, prime gaps, and related keywords, including searches targeting October 15–November 12, 2026. No directly relevant proof or counterexample was found.

Derek Cannon Tucker's September 23, 2026 Zenodo preprint, *The Atlas of Maximal Gaps: Exact Covering Enumeration for Primorial Sieves* (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22919682), addresses Jacobsthal functions and primorial sieves, not the stated conjecture. Michael M. Ross's March 2026 preprint *Sieve Structures of Primorial Goldbach Complements* establishes related pre-sieving properties but not the necessary existence of a prime.

The 2022 Čejchan–Křížek–Somer paper is also represented in public zbMATH-linked bibliographic metadata. portal I did not directly access the full MathSciNet subscription database or exhaustively inspect every recent arXiv or Zenodo submission. Therefore, the literature conclusion is qualified rather than an assertion of absolute novelty.

No equivalence to a named open problem was established.

## 2. Proof attempt and strongest partial result

### Proposition (proved)

For every prime $p\ge5$, let $P=p\#$. Then

$$
\operatorname{prevprime}(P-1)>P-ep
$$

if and only if there exists a prime $r$ such that

$$
\boxed{p<r<ep\quad\text{and}\quad P-r\text{ is prime}.}
$$

This equivalence does not actually require $P-1$ itself to be prime; that additional hypothesis selects the cases relevant to A006794.

### Proof

Suppose first that

$$
q=\operatorname{prevprime}(P-1)>P-ep.
$$

Set $d=P-q$. Then $d<ep$.

Since $p\ge5$, the primorial includes $2$, $3$, and $p$, so $P\ge6p$. Hence

$$
q>P-ep\ge(6-e)p>p.
$$

Also, because $p>e$,

$$
d<ep<p^2.
$$

Assume that $d$ is composite. It then has a prime divisor $\ell$ satisfying

$$
\ell\le\sqrt d<p.
$$

But $\ell\mid P$, since every prime below $p$ divides the primorial. Consequently,

$$
\ell\mid(P-d)=q.
$$

This contradicts the primality of $q>p>\ell$.

Therefore $d$ is prime.

Moreover, $d>p$: otherwise $d$, being prime and at most $p$, would divide both $P$ and $q=P-d$, giving the same contradiction.

Thus $r=d$ satisfies

$$
p<r<ep,\qquad P-r=q\text{ is prime}.
$$

Conversely, suppose a prime $r$ satisfies

$$
p<r<ep,\qquad P-r\text{ prime}.
$$

Since $P-r<P-1$, the greatest prime $q$ below $P-1$ must satisfy

$$
q\ge P-r>P-ep.
$$

This proves both directions. $\square$

The exceptional case $p=3$ is immediate: $3\#=6$, $N=5$, and its previous prime is $3$, giving $P-q=3<3e$.

### Significance and limitation

**Proved:** The conjecture reduces to the existence of a prime among the finite candidates

$$
p\#-r,\qquad r\text{ prime},\quad p<r<ep.
$$

This eliminates every composite offset from consideration.

**Known mathematical context:** The divisibility argument is essentially the same mechanism used in Theorem 18 of Čejchan–Křížek–Somer (2022). I do not claim it as a novel theorem. The particular $ep$-window equivalence is an elementary specialization.

**Unproved step:** Nothing established here guarantees that at least one of those candidates is prime whenever $p\#-1$ is prime.

That existence statement is precisely the unresolved substance of Wesolowski's conjecture. Ordinary prime-density heuristics cannot replace a proof at these specially structured integers.

## 3. Computational check

I implemented two checks: a Python/SymPy verification over every prime $p\le337$, and a GMP-backed C++ checker for the first 17 known members of A006794.

### Check A: all prime inputs through 337

The following program was executed:

```python

from math import e
from sympy import primerange, isprime, prevprime

P = 1
seen = 0

for p in primerange(2, 338):
    P *= p
    if not isprime(P - 1):
        continue

    q = prevprime(P - 1)
    d = P - q
    witness = (
        (p == 3 and d == 3)
        or any(isprime(P - r)
               for r in primerange(p + 1, int(e*p) + 1))
    )

    assert d < e*p
    assert witness
    if p >= 5:
        assert isprime(d) and p < d

    seen += 1
    print(p, d)

print("qualifying_cases =", seen)
```
