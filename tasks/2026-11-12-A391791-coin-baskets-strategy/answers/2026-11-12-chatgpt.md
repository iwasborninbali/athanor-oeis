## 2. Proof attempt and strongest partial result

### Precise formulation

There are two baskets and coins with distinct integer values $1,\ldots,n$. On each turn, one basket is chosen independently with probability $1/2$. After observing the chosen basket, the player selects one unused coin and deposits it there. After all coins have been placed, the player's payoff is the larger basket sum.

Let $W_n(k)$ denote the maximum expected payoff, conditional on depositing coin $k$ in the first selected basket.

The conjecture is that, for every $n\geq10$,

$$
\boxed{
W_n\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n+3}{2}\right\rfloor\right)
=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}W_n(k).
}
$$

This interpretation allows ties. The computations below establish uniqueness throughout the tested conjecture range.

### Exact Bellman recurrence

Let

$$
S_n=\frac{n(n+1)}2
$$

be the total value of all coins, and let $D$ be the difference between the two current basket sums.

Because

$$
\max(B_1,B_2)=\frac{S_n+|D|}{2}
$$

at the end of the game, maximizing expected payoff is equivalent to maximizing the expected absolute final difference.

For any set $M$ of remaining coins and any integer $d$, define

$$
F(M,d)=2^{|M|}
\left(\text{maximum expected final }|D|
\text{ starting from }(M,d)\right).
$$

The following recurrence is exact:

$$
F(\varnothing,d)=|d|.
$$

For nonempty $M$,

$$
\boxed{
F(M,d)=
\max_{x\in M}F(M\setminus\{x\},d+x)
+
\max_{x\in M}F(M\setminus\{x\},d-x).
}
\tag{1}
$$

**Proof.** The next basket is chosen uniformly at random. If the first basket is selected, adding coin $x$ changes the difference to $d+x$. If the second basket is selected, it changes the difference to $d-x$.

The player observes which basket was selected before choosing the coin. Therefore the two maximizations are independent. Applying the definition of $F$, the factor $1/2$ is canceled by the change from $2^{|M|}$ to $2^{|M|-1}$. This establishes (1).

After the first coin $k$, the remaining state is

$$
M_k=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{k\},\qquad d=k.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
a(n)=
\frac{
2^{n-1}S_n+
\max_{1\leq k\leq n}F(M_k,k)
}{2}.
}
\tag{2}
$$

Equations (1) and (2) give an exact, finite, integer-arithmetic algorithm for determining all optimal opening coins.

### General structural lemma — proved

For every finite set of positive coin values $M$, the function $d\mapsto F(M,d)$ satisfies the following properties:

1. It is even: $F(M,d)=F(M,-d)$.
2. It is convex.
3. It is $2^{|M|}$-Lipschitz.
4. It is affine for $d\geq\sum_{x\in M}x$, with slope $2^{|M|}$.

**Proof.** All four statements follow by induction on $|M|$.

For $M=\varnothing$, they follow from $F(\varnothing,d)=|d|$.

Assume them for every proper subset of $M$. Symmetry follows because replacing $d$ by $-d$ exchanges the two maxima in (1).

Translations preserve convexity, pointwise maxima of convex functions are convex, and sums of convex functions are convex. Thus (1) preserves convexity.

Each maximum of $2^{|M|-1}$-Lipschitz functions is $2^{|M|-1}$-Lipschitz. Their sum is $2^{|M|}$-Lipschitz.

Finally, if $d\geq\sum M$, then for every $x\in M$, both $d+x$ and $d-x$ lie in the affine-tail region of the corresponding child state. Each term inside either maximum therefore has slope $2^{|M|-1}$. Their sum has slope $2^{|M|}$.

This completes the induction.

These structural observations hold for arbitrary finite positive coin sets. They do not, by themselves, imply the proposed optimal opening coin.

### Strongest finite result

$$

This follows by exhaustive evaluation of recurrence (1) over all reachable states and comparison of every candidate first coin.

## 3. Independent computational verification

Two implementations were used: a Python memoized recurrence and an optimized C++ implementation using a bitmask representation of the remaining coins and two layers of dynamic-programming states.

The C++ algorithm uses the following exact state reduction. If $M$ is the set of remaining coins, then the total value of coins already placed is

$$
U=S_n-\sum_{x\in M}x.
$$

Every reachable nonnegative difference has the form

$$
d=(U\bmod2)+2j,\qquad
0\leq j\leq\lfloor U/2\rfloor.
$$

Combined with $F(M,-d)=F(M,d)$, this permits storing only the reachable nonnegative differences. Each state requires values from the preceding subset-cardinality layer, so older layers can be discarded.

## 4. Verdict

**Status: Open as far as the searched literature establishes; computationally verified through $n=25$.** The original conjecture is well-posed when interpreted as specifying an optimal first move, and no counterexample was found. The new contribution relative to the live OEIS entry is the exact extension by three terms, $a(23)=884741548$, $a(24)=1922512940$, and $a(25)=4164198462$, together with confirmation that the predicted opening coins $13,13,14$ are uniquely optimal. An exact difference-based Bellman formulation and elementary convexity/affine-tail properties are also proved, without claiming that those observations are unpublished. Confidence is high in the finite computations, supported by agreement with published terms and independent implementations; there is no claim of an all-$n$ proof. A suitable proposed OEIS comment would be: “Conjectured optimal first coin verified by exact dynamic programming for 10 ≤ n ≤ 25 (unique in this range). Further terms: a(23)=884741548, a(24)=1922512940, a(25)=4164198462. Optimal first coins for these terms are 13, 13, and 14, respectively.” No OEIS submission or edit was made.
