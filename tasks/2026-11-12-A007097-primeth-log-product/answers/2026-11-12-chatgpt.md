# A007097 — Status and proof of the primeth logarithmic-product conjecture

**Research report — 12 November 2026**

**Result: The conjecture is false.** A stronger asymptotic can be proved unconditionally using the classical expansion of the $n$-th prime.

Let $a_0=1$, $a_{n+1}=p_{a_n}$, where $p_m$ is the $m$-th prime, and put

$$
P_n=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\log a_k.
$$

The result is

$$
\boxed{
P_n\sim C\,a_n\log\log a_n
\qquad(n\to\infty)
}
$$

for some constant $C\in(0,\infty)$.

In particular,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P_n}{a_n}=+\infty,}
$$

not $1$, as conjectured.

The proof also establishes the sharper estimate

$$
\boxed{
P_n=C\,a_n\log\log a_n
\left(1+O\left(\frac1{\log a_n}\right)\right).
}
$$

The proof below is self-contained apart from the established asymptotic expansion of the $n$-th prime. No unproved hypothesis is required.

## 1. Status and literature check

### 1.1 Original conjecture

Thomas Ordowski, **26 March 2015**, OEIS [A007097](https://oeis.org/A007097), COMMENTS:

> Conjecture: log(a(1))*log(a(2))*...*log(a(n)) ~ a(n).

The OEIS entry has offset $0$, so $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$. The conjectured product correctly starts at $a_1$; it does not contain the zero factor $\log a_0$.

The mathematical meaning of the conjecture is

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\log a_k}{a_n}=1.
$$

**Status: Refuted by the proof in Section 2.**

### 1.3 Established results used

The decisive mathematical input is the classical Cipolla expansion for the $n$-th prime.

**Mario Cipolla (1902)**, *La determinazione asintotica dell’n-esimo numero primo*, *Rendiconti dell'Accademia delle Scienze Fisiche e Matematiche di Napoli*, series 3, volume 8, pp. 132–166.

A modern source is **Christian Axler (2017)**, *New estimates for the nth prime number*, arXiv:1706.03651, Introduction, equations (1.1)–(1.2). Axler writes:

> Cipolla [...] found a more precise result.

The source gives the expansion, from which we need only

$$
p_m=m\left(
\log m+\log\log m-1+
O\left(\frac{\log\log m}{\log m}\right)
\right).
\tag{1}
$$

Axler's equation (1.2) supplies a stronger expansion with explicit polynomial terms.

## 2. Proof and corrected asymptotic

### Theorem

Let $p_m$ denote the $m$-th prime and define

$$
a_0=1,\qquad a_{n+1}=p_{a_n}.
$$

Then there exists a constant $C>0$ such that

$$
\prod_{k=1}^{n}\log a_k
=
C\,a_n\log\log a_n
\left(1+O\left(\frac1{\log a_n}\right)\right).
\tag{2}
$$

Consequently, Ordowski's conjectured equivalence $P_n\sim a_n$ is false.

### Proof

All logarithms are natural. The sequence is strictly increasing and unbounded, since $p_m>m$.

Set

$$
t_n=\log a_n,\qquad
q_n=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{p_{a_n}}{a_n}.
$$

In particular, $t_n\to\infty$.

**Step 1. Obtain the requisite precision from the prime asymptotic.**

Apply (1) with $m=a_n$. Then

$$
q_n=t_n+\log t_n-1+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}\right).
\tag{3}
$$

Taking logarithms,

$$
\log q_n
=\log t_n+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}\right).
\tag{4}
$$

The recurrence gives the exact identity

$$
t_{n+1}=t_n+\log q_n.
\tag{5}
$$

Combining (4) and (5),

$$
t_{n+1}
=t_n+\log t_n+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}\right).
\tag{6}
$$

On the other hand, subtracting (3) from (6),

$$
t_{n+1}-q_n
=1+O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}\right).
$$

Because $q_n=t_n(1+o(1))$, division gives

$$
\frac{t_{n+1}}{q_n}
=
1+\frac1{t_n}
+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}\right).
\tag{7}
$$

Thus

$$
\log\frac{t_{n+1}}{q_n}
=
\frac1{t_n}
+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}\right).
\tag{8}
$$

**Step 2. Identify the missing normalizing factor.**

Equation (6) implies

$$
\frac{t_{n+1}-t_n}{t_n}
=
\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}
+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}\right).
$$

Using $\log(1+x)=x+O(x^2)$,

$$
\log t_{n+1}-\log t_n
=
\frac{\log t_n}{t_n}
+
O\left(\frac{(\log t_n)^2}{t_n^2}\right).
$$

Dividing by $\log t_n$ and applying the same expansion once more yields

$$
\log\frac{\log t_{n+1}}{\log t_n}
=
\frac1{t_n}
+
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}\right).
\tag{9}
$$

Notice that the leading terms in (8) and (9) agree exactly.

**Step 3. Construct a convergent normalized product.**

For sufficiently large $n$, define

$$
D_n=
\frac{P_n}{a_n\log t_n}
=
\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\log a_k}
{a_n\log\log a_n}.
$$

Because $P_{n+1}=P_nt_{n+1}$ and $a_{n+1}=a_nq_n$,

$$
\frac{D_{n+1}}{D_n}
=
\frac{t_{n+1}}{q_n}
\frac{\log t_n}{\log t_{n+1}}.
$$

Taking logarithms and applying (8)–(9),

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log D_{n+1}-\log D_n
&=
\log\frac{t_{n+1}}{q_n}
-
\log\frac{\log t_{n+1}}{\log t_n}\\
&=
O\left(\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

It remains to establish that these errors are absolutely summable.

**Step 4. Prove convergence, rather than merely assuming it.**

From (4)–(5),

$$
t_{n+1}-t_n
=\log t_n\,(1+o(1)).
$$

Hence, for all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
\frac12\log t_n
\le t_{n+1}-t_n
\le 2\log t_n
$$

and $t_{n+1}\le2t_n$.

Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}
&\le
2\frac{t_{n+1}-t_n}{t_n^2}\\
&\le
4\left(\frac1{t_n}-\frac1{t_{n+1}}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last expression telescopes. Consequently,

$$
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}
\frac{\log t_n}{t_n^2}
=
O\left(\frac1{t_N}\right).
\tag{11}
$$

Equations (10)–(11) prove that $(\log D_n)$ converges to a finite real number $L$, with

$$
\log D_n=L+O(1/t_n).
$$

Define $C=e^L$. Then $0<C<\infty$ and exponentiation gives

$$
D_n=C\left(1+O(1/t_n)\right).
$$

Since $t_n=\log a_n$,

$$
\boxed{
P_n=C\,a_n\log\log a_n
\left(1+O\left(\frac1{\log a_n}\right)\right).
}
$$

Finally, $\log\log a_n\to\infty$, so

$$
\frac{P_n}{a_n}
=
C\log\log a_n\,(1+o(1))
\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$

This contradicts the conjectured limit $1$.

**Q.E.D.**

*Proof status:* Unconditional, assuming only the established Cipolla expansion (1). Neither the Riemann hypothesis nor an unproved conjecture about primes is used.

## 3. Computational check

**Status: Computed.** I independently generated the recurrence through $n=14$, checked the values against the OEIS table, evaluated both normalizations, and tested a lower-bound ingredient of the proof.

The computation used Python, SymPy for exact $m$-th-prime evaluation, and mpmath at 60 decimal digits of working precision.

### Reproducible program

Python 3 · SymPy · mpmath

```python
from sympy import prime
from mpmath import mp

mp.dps = 60
expected = [
    1, 2, 3, 5, 11, 31, 127, 709, 5381, 52711,
    648391, 9737333, 174440041, 3657500101,
    88362852307
]

a = [1]
for n in range(14):
    a.append(int(prime(a[-1])))

assert a == expected
print('Recurrence and OEIS terms n=0..14: PASS')

P = mp.mpf(1)
print('n  a(n)          P_n/a_n        P_n/(a_n*loglog(a_n))')
for n in range(1, 15):
    P *= mp.log(a[n])
    if n in (5, 8, 11, 14):
        R = P / a[n]
        D = R / mp.log(mp.log(a[n]))
        print(
            f'{n:<2} {a[n]:<13} '
            f'{float(R):.12f}   {float(D):.12f}'
        )

for n in range(3, 14):
    t = mp.log(a[n])
    q = mp.mpf(a[n+1]) / a[n]
    assert q > t + mp.log(t) - 1

print('Prime lower-bound samples n=3..13: PASS')

print('n  t_n*(t_(n+1)/q_n - 1)')
for n in (5, 8, 11, 13):
    t = mp.log(a[n])
    q = mp.mpf(a[n+1]) / a[n]
    E = t * (mp.log(a[n+1]) / q - 1)
    print(f'{n:<2} {float(E):.12f}')
```

### Interpretation

The numerical results are consistent with the theorem:

- $P_n/a_n$ grows over the reported sample points.
- $P_n/(a_n\log\log a_n)$ remains near $0.238$ for the larger computed indices, consistent with convergence to a positive constant.
- The quantity $t_n(t_{n+1}/q_n-1)$, which equation (7) predicts tends to $1$, moves toward $1$ over these samples.
- The prime lower-bound inequality is verified for the 11 tested recurrence steps $n=3,\ldots,13$.

The computations do not establish monotonicity, the precise value of $C$, or any infinite limit. Those claims rest on the proof in Section 2, not on numerical extrapolation.

## 4. Verdict

**Refuted, with an unconditional proof.** Thomas Ordowski's 2015 conjecture in OEIS A007097 is false: the product of logarithms is asymptotic not to $a_n$, but to $C a_n\log\log a_n$, for a positive finite constant $C$. This conclusion follows rigorously from Cipolla's classical expansion for the $n$-th prime and a convergent-error argument; the proof does not rely on the withdrawn 2020 MathOverflow disproof. The corrected asymptotic and its $O(1/\log a_n)$ relative error are established here, and the independently computed terms through $n=14$ agree with the OEIS data. I found no prior direct proof in the accessible sources checked, but publication priority remains unverified because the literature search was not exhaustive. **Suggested OEIS comment:** “The conjecture of Mar 26 2015 is false. In fact, Product_ log(a(k)) ~ C*a(n)*log(log(a(n))) for some C > 0. More precisely, the relative error is O(1/log(a(n))). This follows from the classical expansion prime(m)/m = log(m) + log(log(m)) - 1 + O(log(log(m))/log(m)) and a summable-error argument.” No OEIS submission or repository edit was made.
