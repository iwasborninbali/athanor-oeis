# OEIS A000170 — Status and proof assessment

**Date:** 12 November 2026 **Sequence:** [OEIS A000170](https://oeis.org/A000170) — Number of solutions to the $n$-queens problem.

## 1. Status and literature

### 1.1 Main finding

The two conjectures attributed to Benoit Cloitre (10 November 2002) have different statuses.

| Statement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Weak conjecture: $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log(n!/a(n))$ exists | **Proved** by Michael Simkin (2021; published 2023) |
| Strong conjecture: $a(n)\sim n!/c^n$ for some constant $c$ | **Not established** by Simkin's theorem |
| Numerical prediction $c\approx2.54$ | **Inaccurate:** the exponential rate implies $c\approx2.570$ |
| Numerical prediction of a limiting exponent $0.90\ldots$ | **Inaccurate:** the established exponent is approximately $0.944$ |

Here $a(n)=Q(n)$, the number of placements of $n$ mutually nonattacking queens on an $n\times n$ chessboard.

### 1.2 Decisive published result

**Michael Simkin**, *The number of n-queens configurations*, *Advances in Mathematics* **427** (2023), article 109127; DOI: [10.1016/j.aim.2023.109127](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2023.109127). Originally arXiv:2107.13460 (28 July 2021).

The paper's abstract states:

> “We show that there exists a constant $\alpha=1.942\pm3\times10^{-3}$”

and gives the asymptotic formula

$$
\boxed{Q(n)=\bigl((1+o(1))ne^{-\alpha}\bigr)^n.}
$$

This establishes the exponential growth rate, not a multiplicative asymptotic equivalent.

**Parth Nobel, Akshay Agrawal and Stephen Boyd**, *Computing tighter bounds on the n-queens constant via Newton's method*, *Optimization Letters* **17** (2023), 1229–1240; DOI: [10.1007/s11590-022-01933-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11590-022-01933-2). Published online 17 September 2022.

They report the much narrower numerical interval

$$
1.944000752\leq\alpha\leq1.944001082.
$$

Their paper explicitly attributes the existence of the limit to Simkin:

> “Recent work by Simkin [1] has shown that”

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q(n)^{1/n}}{n}=e^{-\alpha}.
$$



**Precision qualification:** Nobel–Agrawal–Boyd used high-accuracy floating-point optimization. On page 1233, they explicitly state that they did not complete the rational-arithmetic certification needed to make their numerical bounds fully rigorous. The existence of $\alpha$, however, is a theorem independently of this numerical refinement.

## 2. Proof and precise formulation

### 2.1 The weak conjecture follows rigorously from Simkin

**Theorem (known consequence of Simkin, not new).**

Let $Q(n)=a(n)$ and let $\alpha$ be Simkin's $n$-queens constant. Then

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{n!}{Q(n)}
=\alpha-1.
}
$$

**Proof.**

Simkin's theorem gives

$$
Q(n)=\bigl((1+o(1))ne^{-\alpha}\bigr)^n.
$$

Taking logarithms,

$$
\log Q(n)=n\log n-\alpha n+o(n).
$$

By Stirling's formula,

$$
\log n!
=n\log n-n+\frac12\log(2\pi n)+O(1/n).
$$

Subtracting,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{n!}{Q(n)}
&=(\alpha-1)n+\frac12\log(2\pi n)+o(n).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\log n=o(n)$, division by $n$ gives

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{n!}{Q(n)}
=\alpha-1.
$$

$\square$

Using Nobel–Agrawal–Boyd's numerical estimates:

$$
\boxed{
\alpha-1\approx0.944001,
\qquad
e^{\alpha-1}\approx2.570244.
}
$$

Their reported numerical interval translates to

$$
2.570243784\lesssim e^{\alpha-1}
\lesssim2.570244631.
$$

These decimal bounds retain the numerical-certification qualification mentioned above.

### 2.2 The strong conjecture requires substantially more

The original strong conjecture must be written as

$$
\boxed{
Q(n)\sim\frac{n!}{c^n}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q(n)c^n}{n!}=1.
}
$$

**Proved:** If this holds for any fixed $c>0$, then necessarily

$$
\boxed{c=c_*:=e^{\alpha-1}.}
$$

Indeed, taking logarithms of the supposed asymptotic relation gives

$$
\frac1n\log\frac{n!}{Q(n)}
=\log c+o(1).
$$

The weak theorem therefore forces $\log c=\alpha-1$.

In particular, **the choice $c=2.54$ exactly is refuted**, not merely unsupported. Simkin's rigorous bound $\alpha>1.94$ gives

$$
c_*>e^{0.94}>2.54.
$$

More generally,

$$
\frac1n\log\left(\frac{Q(n)c^n}{n!}\right)
\longrightarrow \log\frac{c}{c_*}.
$$

Consequently, when $c<c_*$, the proposed ratio tends to zero; when $c>c_*$, it tends to infinity. Only $c=c_*$ is a candidate for ratio convergence to one.

This does **not** refute the original existential wording “a constant $c$ around 2.54”: $2.570244\ldots$ could reasonably be regarded as an approximate correction.

### 2.3 Exact characterization of the missing estimate

Define the remainder

$$
r_n=\log Q(n)-n\log n+\alpha n.
$$

Simkin proves only $r_n=o(n)$.

Applying Stirling's formula again gives

$$
\log\left(\frac{Q(n)c_*^n}{n!}\right)
=r_n-\frac12\log(2\pi n)+O(1/n).
$$

Thus the strong conjecture is equivalent to the substantially sharper statement

$$
\boxed{
r_n=\frac12\log(2\pi n)+o(1).
}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
\log Q(n)
=n\log n-\alpha n
+\frac12\log(2\pi n)+o(1).
}
$$

This equivalence is elementary and proved here, but it is not a solution of the remaining asymptotic problem. The bound $r_n=o(n)$ allows errors much larger than $\log n$, and therefore cannot imply the required conclusion.

**Result of the proof attempt:** the weak conjecture is settled; the literal $c=2.54$ formula is false; the only possible constant for the strong conjecture is identified; and the strong conjecture is reduced to a precise second-order asymptotic estimate. No new proof of that estimate or counterexample to the existential strong conjecture is obtained.

## 3. Computational verification

### 3.1 Exhaustive enumeration

**Method:** bitmask backtracking, placing one queen per row and tracking occupied columns and attacked diagonals.

The program was compiled with C++17 (`-O3`) and executed successfully. It exhaustively enumerates $Q(n)$ for $0\leq n\leq16$, and independently checks the results for $0\leq n\leq9$ using permutation enumeration.

Reproducible C++17 source

```

#include <cmath>
#include <cstdint>
#include <iomanip>
#include <iostream>
#include <algorithm>
#include <numeric>
#include <vector>
using namespace std;

uint64_t search(uint64_t col, uint64_t ld,
                uint64_t rd, uint64_t full) {
    if (col == full) return 1;
    uint64_t avail = full & ~(col | ld | rd), ans = 0;
    while (avail) {
        uint64_t bit = avail & -avail;
        avail -= bit;
        ans += search(col | bit, (ld | bit) << 1,
                      (rd | bit) >> 1, full);
    }
    return ans;
}

uint64_t brute(int n) {
    vector<int> p(n);
    iota(p.begin(), p.end(), 0);
    uint64_t ans = 0;
    do {
        bool ok = true;
        for (int i = 0; i < n; i++)
            for (int j = i + 1; j < n; j++)
                if (abs(p[i] - p[j]) == j - i)
                    ok = false;
        ans += ok;
    } while (next_permutation(p.begin(), p.end()));
    return ans;
}

int main() {
    cout << fixed << setprecision(6);
    cout << "n Q(n) log(n!/Q(n))/n "
            "exp(log(n!/Q(n))/n)\n";
    for (int n = 0; n <= 16; n++) {
        uint64_t q = search(0, 0, 0, (1ULL << n) - 1);
        if (n <= 9 && q != brute(n)) {
            cerr << "mismatch at " << n << "\n";
            return 1;
        }
        cout << n << " " << q;
        if (n >= 4) {
            double h = (lgamma(n + 1.0) - log((double)q)) / n;
            cout << " " << h << " " << exp(h);
        } else {
            cout << " NA NA";
        }
        cout << "\n";
    }
    cout << "cross_check: n=0..9 PASS\n";
}
```

## 4. Verdict

**Verdict (12 November 2026):** Cloitre's weak conjecture is **proved**, as a direct consequence of Michael Simkin's 2021 theorem published in *Advances in Mathematics* in 2023. Its constant is $\alpha-1\approx0.944001$, and the associated exponential base is $e^{\alpha-1}\approx2.570244$, numerically refined by Nobel, Agrawal and Boyd (2022/2023). The strong assertion $Q(n)\sim n!/c^n$ is **not established by these results** and remains unresolved in the literature located during this investigation. Its only possible constant is $c=e^{\alpha-1}$; the exact choice $c=2.54$ is refuted. No genuinely new theorem or counterexample to the existential strong assertion is claimed here. An appropriate OEIS comment would be: *“Cloitre's weak asymptotic conjecture follows from Simkin (2023): $\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log(n!/a(n))=\alpha-1\approx0.944001$, where $\alpha$ is the n-queens constant (A359441). Thus any asymptotic $a(n)\sim n!/c^n$ requires $c=e^{\alpha-1}\approx2.570244$. Simkin's theorem establishes the exponential rate, not the stronger ratio asymptotic.”* This is a proposed comment only; **nothing was submitted or edited on OEIS or GitHub**. Confidence is high in the mathematical conclusions and computation, with the stated limitation on exhaustiveness of the literature search.
