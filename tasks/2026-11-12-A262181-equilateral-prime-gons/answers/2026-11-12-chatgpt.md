# OEIS A262181 — Status and Proof Report

**Date:** 12 November 2026 **Problem:** Uniqueness of convex equilateral prime-sided polygons with angles that are integer multiples of $\pi/n$.

## 1. Status: proved using known mathematics

**Verdict: The conjecture is true and admits an elementary proof.** It follows from classical results about vanishing sums of roots of unity, in particular the classification established by T. Y. Lam and K. H. Leung.

The conjecture in [OEIS A262181](https://oeis.org/A262181) states:

> Conjecture: There is only one convex equilateral n-gon for prime n.

The precise claim is that for every odd prime $p$, every convex equilateral $p$-gon whose interior angles are integer multiples of $\pi/p$ is regular, up to similarity.

### Prior literature

**T. Y. Lam and K. H. Leung (2000)**, *On Vanishing Sums of Roots of Unity*, *Journal of Algebra* **224**(1), 91–109, DOI: [10.1006/jabr.1999.8089](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1999.8089).

Their Corollary 3.4 classifies minimal vanishing sums of roots of unity when the order has at most two distinct prime factors. In the special case of $2p$-th roots, it implies that the only minimal vanishing sums, up to rotation, consist of either an antipodal pair or a complete regular $p$-cycle. Problems+1

This is decisive because the edge vectors of a polygon in A262181, after normalizing their common length and rotating the first edge, are $2p$-th roots of unity whose sum is zero.

The relevant mathematical ingredient is therefore **known**, rather than a new conjectural result. What follows is a self-contained proof of its application to A262181.

## 2. Self-contained proof

### Theorem

For every odd prime $p$, every convex equilateral $p$-gon with interior angles in

$$
\left\{\frac{\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi}{p},\ldots,\pi\right\}
$$

is a regular $p$-gon.

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{A262181}(p)=1\quad\text{for every prime }p\ge3.}
$$

The prime $2$ is excluded because the sequence begins at $n=3$, and an ordinary nondegenerate polygon has at least three sides.

### Proof

**Step 1: Convert the polygon into a vanishing sum of roots of unity.**

Let the polygon's successive oriented edge vectors be $v_0,\ldots,v_{p-1}$. Because the polygon is equilateral, we may normalize the side length to $1$ and rotate the polygon so that $v_0=1$.

The interior angles are

$$
\alpha_j=\frac{m_j\pi}{p},\qquad 1\le m_j\le p.
$$

The corresponding exterior turns are

$$
\theta_j=\pi-\alpha_j
=\frac{(p-m_j)\pi}{p}.
$$

Therefore all oriented edge directions are powers of

$$
\zeta=e^{i\pi/p},
$$

a primitive $2p$-th root of unity.

Closure of the polygon gives

$$
\boxed{v_0+v_1+\cdots+v_{p-1}=0.}
\tag{1}
$$

**Step 2: Express closure as a polynomial identity.**

For $0\le j<2p$, let $a_j$ count the edges whose direction is $\zeta^j$. Then

$$
a_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{2p-1}a_j=p,
$$

and equation (1) becomes

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{2p-1}a_j\zeta^j=0.
$$

Since $\zeta^p=-1$, define

$$
b_j=a_j-a_{j+p},\qquad 0\le j<p.
$$

We obtain

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_j\zeta^j=0.
\tag{2}
$$

Let

$$
B(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jx^j.
$$

For odd prime $p$, the cyclotomic polynomial of order $2p$ is

$$
\Phi_{2p}(x)
=\frac{x^p+1}{x+1}
=1-x+x^2-\cdots+x^{p-1}.
$$

It is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb Q$ and has degree $p-1$.

From $B(\zeta)=0$ and $\deg B\le p-1$, it follows that

$$
B(x)=c\Phi_{2p}(x)
$$

for some integer $c$. Thus

$$
\boxed{b_j=c(-1)^j\quad(0\le j<p).}
\tag{3}
$$

**Step 3: Use the fact that there are exactly $p$ edges.**

We have

$$
p=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(a_j+a_{j+p})
\ge\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}|a_j-a_{j+p}|
=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}|b_j|
=p|c|.
$$

Hence $|c|\le1$.

If $c=0$, then $a_j=a_{j+p}$ for every $j$, so

$$
p=2\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}a_j,
$$

which contradicts the oddness of $p$.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{c\in\{-1,1\}.}
$$

The preceding inequality must now be an equality. For every $j$,

$$
a_j+a_{j+p}=|a_j-a_{j+p}|=1.
$$

Consequently, exactly one edge direction is selected from each antipodal pair, and equation (3) determines which one.

When $c=1$, the complete multiset of directions is

$$
\left\{(-1)^j\zeta^j:0\le j<p\right\}
=\left\{(-\zeta)^j:0\le j<p\right\}.
$$

Since $p$ is odd,

$$
-\zeta=e^{2\pi i(p+1)/(2p)}
$$

is a primitive $p$-th root of unity. The set is therefore precisely the set of all $p$-th roots of unity.

When $c=-1$, all these directions are negated.

Thus **every vanishing sum of exactly $p$ $2p$-th roots of unity is, as a multiset, a rotated regular $p$-cycle.** In particular, no edge direction is repeated.

This proves the algebraic lemma without relying on an external classification theorem.

**Step 4: Convexity determines the edge order.**

For a convex polygon traversed counterclockwise, the oriented edge directions occur in circular order; its exterior turns are nonnegative and sum to $2\pi$.

The $p$ directions just obtained are equally spaced around the unit circle, with angular separation $2\pi/p$. As each occurs exactly once, their convex cyclic ordering forces every consecutive exterior turn to be $2\pi/p$.

Every interior angle is therefore

$$
\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p}=\frac{(p-2)\pi}{p}.
$$

The polygon is both equilateral and equiangular, hence regular.

Conversely, the regular $p$-gon satisfies every condition, so existence and uniqueness both follow.

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{A262181}(p)=1\quad\text{for all odd primes }p.}
\qquad\square
$$

**Proof status:** Complete. The cyclotomic irreducibility fact is standard; all deductions specific to the polygon have been given explicitly. No numerical approximation or unproved enumeration formula is used.

## 3. Computational verification

I independently checked the conjecture using exact integer arithmetic in the cyclotomic fields $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2n})$. No floating-point tolerance was used.

The program performs two tests:

1. Enumerates possible exterior-angle sequences for convex equilateral polygons, checks exact closure, and identifies polygons up to cyclic rotation. Reflections remain distinct, consistent with A262181.
2. Exhaustively enumerates all multisets of $p$ $2p$-th roots of unity for $p=3,5,7$, checking the algebraic lemma independently of convexity.

### Reproducible Python program

Requires Python 3 and SymPy.

```python

from itertools import combinations_with_replacement
from functools import lru_cache
from math import comb
from sympy import Poly, cyclotomic_poly, symbols

x = symbols('x')

@lru_cache(None)
def basis(n):
    q = Poly(cyclotomic_poly(2*n, x), x)
    return [
        tuple(int(Poly(x**j, x).rem(q).nth(i))
              for i in range(q.degree()))
        for j in range(2*n)
    ]

def zerosum(n, exps):
    v = basis(n)
    return not any(
        sum(v[k % (2*n)][i] for k in exps)
        for i in range(len(v[0]))
    )

def count_polygons(n):
    v, d = basis(n), len(basis(n)[0])
    classes = set()
    turns = []
    raw = 0

    def visit(i, remain, exponent, sums):
        nonlocal raw
        if i == n-1:
            if 0 <= remain < n and not any(sums):
                t = tuple(turns + [remain])
                raw += 1
                classes.add(
                    min(t[j:] + t[:j] for j in range(n))
                )
            return

        lo = max(0, remain-(n-i-1)*(n-1))
        hi = min(n-1, remain)
        for turn in range(lo, hi+1):
            e = (exponent+turn) % (2*n)
            turns.append(turn)
            visit(i+1, remain-turn, e,
                  tuple(a+b for a,b in zip(sums,v[e])))
            turns.pop()

    visit(0, 2*n, 0, basis(n)[0])
    return raw, len(classes)

for n in range(3, 10):
    raw, classes = count_polygons(n)
    print(f'n={n}: closed_turn_sequences={raw}, '
          f'cyclic_classes={classes}', flush=True)

for p in (3, 5, 7):
    zero = [
        c for c in combinations_with_replacement(
            range(2*p), p)
        if zerosum(p, c)
    ]
    print(f'p={p}: tested_multisets={comb(3*p-1,p)}, '
          f'zero_sum={len(zero)}, multisets={zero}',
          flush=True)
```
