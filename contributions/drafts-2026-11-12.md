## A092506 (Comments)

Cloitre's conjecture of Mar 02 2018 is true in the following form: for x >= 2, A000010(x) + A000010(x-1) =
floor((3x-2)/2) if and only if x is a prime of the form 2^n + 1 (n >= 0), i.e., x is 2 or a Fermat prime. Proof:
phi(m) <= m-1 with equality iff m is prime, and phi(m) <= m/2 for even m with equality iff m is a power of 2. For even
x >= 4 the left side is at most 3x/2 - 2 < 3x/2 - 1; for odd x >= 3 it is at most 3(x-1)/2 = floor((3x-2)/2), with
equality iff x is prime and x-1 is a power of 2. Whether the listed terms are all solutions is therefore equivalent to
whether there are no further Fermat primes. - _Aleksei Kudriashov_, Nov 12 2026

## A007540 (Comments), batch 2

The conjecture of Ordowski and Resta (Jul 25 2018) is true: by Lerch's congruence (1905), for every odd prime p,
Sum_{a=1..p-1} a^(p-1) == (p-1)! + p (mod p^2), so the power-sum condition holds iff (p-1)! == -1 (mod p^2).
See A197632 and Sondow (2014). - _Aleksei Kudriashov_, Nov 12 2026

## A064591 (Comments), batch 2

With s0, s1 the sums of reciprocals of the even and odd divisors of n, 2*s0 - s1 = 1 holds exactly when n = 4*P for a
perfect number P: writing n = 2^a*m with m odd, 2*s0 - s1 = (1 - 2^(1-a))*sigma(m)/m; a = 2 gives sigma(m) = 2m, and
a >= 3 forces m = 2^(a-1) - 1 prime. So Lagneau's conjecture is equivalent to Ligh and Wall's conjecture that every
nonunitary perfect number is 4 times an even perfect number, together with the nonexistence of odd perfect numbers.
- _Aleksei Kudriashov_, Nov 12 2026

## A007097 (Comments), batch 3 (priority: other groups are working on this)

The conjecture of Mar 26 2015 is false: Product_{k=1..n} log(a(k)) = C*a(n)*log(log(a(n)))*(1 + O(1/log(a(n)))) for a
constant C (numerically about 0.238), so the ratio to a(n) tends to infinity. Proof sketch: with t(n) = log(a(n)) and
q(n) = a(n+1)/a(n), Cipolla's expansion gives q(n) = t(n) + log(t(n)) - 1 + O(log(t(n))/t(n)); then log(t(n+1)/q(n)) and
log(log(t(n+1))/log(t(n))) both equal 1/t(n) + O(log(t(n))/t(n)^2), and the error terms are summable.
- _Aleksei Kudriashov_, Nov 12 2026

## A332101 (Formula/Comments), batch 10 (new, certified)

At n = 777451915729368, a(n) = 1121626023352385 = A078607(n) + 1 = round(n/log(2)) + 2, so the formula
a(n) = A078607(n) + 2 fails there. Certificate with k = A078607(n) = 1121626023352384 and N = k + 1:
2*((k-1)/k)^n < 1 < 2*(k/(k+1))^n (margins -3.28*10^-32 and 6.18*10^-16), and Sum_{h=1..120} ((N-h)/N)^n > 1
(margin 2.20*10^-32), whence a(n) <= N; and a(n) >= A078607(n) + 1 for all n, since
E(m+1) - E(m) = (m+1)^n - 2*m^n with E(m) = m^n - Sum_{j<m} j^n and E(1) = 1. Checked with two independent
directed-rounding implementations. - _Aleksei Kudriashov_, Nov 12 2026
